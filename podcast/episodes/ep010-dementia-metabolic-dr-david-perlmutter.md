## What this episode covers

- Alzheimer's reframed as a primarily metabolic brain condition, not primarily an amyloid disease: he says it is the third leading cause of death in America, risk by age 85 is close to the high 40% range, women carry twice the risk of men, and it is significantly preventable with no meaningful treatment yet.
- Microglia, the brain's immune cells (about 10% of brain cells): supportive M2 cells nurture synapses and the blood-brain barrier, while destructive M1 cells destroy synapses; high blood sugar, inflammation, poor sleep, stress, alcohol and social isolation drive the M2-to-M1 shift.
- His top five brain harms, in his order: offloading agency for your brain to the pharmaceutical industry, being sedentary, lack of social connection, non-restorative sleep, and poor diet.
- APOE4 genetics: about 25% of people carry one copy (he puts it at about a fivefold Alzheimer's risk) and two copies at up to a twelvefold risk — a predisposition, not a determinant; "genetics loads the gun, lifestyle pulls the trigger."
- Exercise: a November 2025 JAMA-reported Framingham Heart Study analysis (about 4,000 people) found the most active middle-aged adults had about a 45% lower dementia risk, and older APOE4 carriers who were most active about a 67% reduced risk; contracting muscle releases myokines that raise BDNF; combine aerobic and resistance training.
- Sleep and sugar: restorative sleep consolidates memory and activates glymphatic waste clearance, and even one poor night impairs blood-sugar control; a 12 oz glass of orange juice holds 8 teaspoons of sugar (10 for soda), and type 2 diabetes raises Alzheimer's risk by at least 75–85% on more recent data.
- Hope for people already affected: a Harvard-associated lifestyle study of 51 people with Alzheimer's or mild cognitive impairment (exercise, plant-forward Mediterranean-type diet, stress reduction, 1 hour of meditation a day for 20 weeks) saw 70% stabilise or improve.
- Caution: his position that FDA-approved amyloid-targeting drugs are largely ineffective and should not be used is contested, not consensus; the 40 Hz light and sound approach rests on early research he acknowledges he cannot prove works for him personally.
