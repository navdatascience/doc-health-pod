## What this episode covers

- Fasting can benefit everyone, Dr Vishakha says, but only under medical guidance: intermittent fasting (16:8, 5:2) can cause hypoglycaemia in diabetics on sugar-lowering medication, which she calls a medical emergency; she fasts three times a week herself and advises against fasting all seven days.
- Fresh fruit juice is "very unhealthy" in her framing — essentially fructose, which she calls the worst kind of sugar, linked to fatty liver and heart disease; jalebi is her worst dessert ("just fried sugar syrup"), and she states sugar does not cause diabetes but makes it worse if you have it.
- Sleep quality matters more than hours: deep sleep is the real metric, textbook guidance is 6–7 hours, bedroom as cool as possible around 20°C, blue light blocked at least two hours before bed, and caffeine (half-life 10–12 hours) stopped by roughly 2 PM for a midnight bedtime — including tea and chocolate.
- Supplements must follow blood tests plus symptoms — "we don't treat the report, we treat the patient" — with only omega-3 supplemented (omega-6 and omega-9 are pro-inflammatory and already abundant in food); magnesium for cravings, blood pressure, sleep and anxiety, and melatonin only short-term.
- Vagus-nerve activation for stress: make the exhalation longer than the inhalation for about 5 minutes to lower resting heart rate and blood pressure.
- Exercise minimums she cites: American Heart Association 150 minutes of cardio per week, Tabata (8 minutes of high-intensity exercise three times a week) for the same cardiovascular benefit once tolerance is built, and weight training 20 minutes three times a week for longevity; beginners should start slow.
- Caution: her excess-protein / mTOR cancer claim, the seven-day water fast cancer framing, her absolute fructose claims, and the 3–4 hour sleep anecdote are her positions or anecdotes, not settled guidance.
