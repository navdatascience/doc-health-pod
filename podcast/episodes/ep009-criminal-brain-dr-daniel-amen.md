## What this episode covers

- No single criminal brain: Dr Amen says he has scanned about 300,000 people including about 1,000 convicted criminals; impulsive offenders show low frontal function, compulsive offenders an overactive front, with head injury, infections and toxins as other routes to violent behaviour.
- The prefrontal cortex as the brain's brake: it stops you acting on every thought and plays out consequences; low activity (ADHD, alcohol, repeated head impacts) weakens the brake, while overactivity makes thoughts loop compulsively.
- The reversible-cause case: his 9-year-old nephew (1995) attacked a girl unprovoked; a scan found a golf-ball-sized cyst in the left temporal lobe, and after a neurosurgeon drained it his behaviour reportedly returned to normal.
- Four circles assessment — biological, psychological, social, spiritual — and risk is not destiny: a low-activity scan means about 10x more likely to commit a criminal act, not certain to.
- Brain reserve and blood flow: the brain gets 20% of blood flow; Japanese walking (3 minutes normal, 3 minutes fast, repeated 5 times), lifting weights, sleep and avoiding alcohol; devices called the number-one parental mistake for under-10s.
- You are not stuck with your brain: change described from age 6 to 94, and the daily positivity practice — start with "today is going to be a great day" and close by asking "what went well today?"
- Caution: SPECT imaging for psychiatric diagnosis and criminal-risk prediction is contested in mainstream psychiatry; many precise figures are stated without sources shown, and several recommendations are product-linked to his own clinics.
