## Important tidbits

- Lauren Wasser says that on 3 October 2012, aged 24, she developed flu-like symptoms while on her period, having changed her super-absorbent tampon as she normally did; she passed out at home and was found face down on her bedroom floor after her mother requested police welfare checks.
- She says she arrived at St John's hospital with a 107 fever, was "10 minutes from death," had two heart attacks and failing kidneys and organs, and was given a 1% chance of survival; an infectious-disease doctor on call asked whether she had a tampon in, and the tampon tested positive for TSST-1, confirming toxic shock syndrome (TSS).
- She says she was in a coma on life support for about a week and a half, and was pumped so full of fluids (around 100 lb) that she woke up weighing 200 lb.
- She says the damage to her extremities happened because her body preserved blood for her brain, heart and organs while she was dying; her feet and hands turned black, and although her hands recovered, gangrene set into her right leg and doctors told her it had to be amputated or she would die.
- She says she first learned of the amputation by overhearing a nurse on the phone behind a curtain arranging a below-the-knee amputation and a transfer to UCLA for hyperbaric treatment, and that she was sent into the hyperbaric chamber three times a day to try to salvage blood flow.
- She says that after the amputation she was kept off pain medicine for 24 hours because of concerns about her heart, and felt everything that was done to her.
- She says she spent 8 months in a wheelchair, was severely depressed and thought about suicide every day, but held on partly because her younger brother would have been the first to find her.
- She says she kept her left leg for about 7 years despite constant pain — her body overproduced calcium so bone protruded through her skin, requiring repeated surgery, wound care every other Monday, hyperbaric sessions and "apple grafts" (baby-foreskin grafts) on her heel — before deciding at around age 30 to amputate it too, which she describes as the decision that gave her her freedom.
- She says her running blades cost around $125,000 and her everyday legs around $100,000, that she is sponsored by prosthetics company Össur, and that healthcare generally only provides basic prosthetics that get you from point A to point B, with running, swimming or ocean use treated as a "luxury."
- She says she chose gold prosthetic legs inspired by A$AP Rocky's gold grill, calling them her "jewellery piece" and "trophies," and that owning and displaying them attracts curiosity instead of shutting people away.
- She argues that tampons contain chlorine, bleach, dioxin and synthetic fibres, that even organic cotton tampons are sprayed with pesticides, and that there is currently nothing on the market she considers safe for women; she also says TSS can occur with menstrual cups.
- She says TSS has killed "hundreds and thousands of women" since the early 1980s and is frequently misdiagnosed because its flu-like symptoms are so vague.
- She campaigns with Dawn, a mother who lost her teenage daughter Madeline to TSS (foundation: dontshockme.org), and describes meeting Congresswoman Carolyn Maloney to push for reintroduction of the Robin Danielson Act — named after a woman who died of TSS in 1998 — which she says Congress rejected 10 times.
- Asked whether she would change anything, she says she would not take her old life back, because her purpose is to fight until women are given products that won't kill them for a basic monthly necessity.

## Claims to be careful with

- Wasser's central claim — that the chemicals in tampons (chlorine, bleach, dioxin, synthetic fibres, pesticides) are what cause toxic shock syndrome and make all current products unsafe — is her activist position. Mainstream medicine attributes TSS to toxins produced by Staphylococcus aureus bacteria, with risk associated with tampon absorbency and duration of use; her chemical-causation framing goes beyond the established consensus and is contested.
- Her figures — that TSS has killed "hundreds and thousands of women," that her blades cost ~$125,000, that the Robin Danielson Act was rejected 10 times, and that girls now get periods at 8–10 because of hormones in food — are stated from memory in conversation and are not sourced or verified in the episode.
- She says she changed her tampon normally and as directed, and that TSS "has nothing to do with leaving your tampon in too long"; product guidance and much medical advice still emphasise changing tampons regularly and using the lowest absorbency needed, so her account should not be read as making duration of use irrelevant.
- Her claim that nothing on the market is safe, and her advice never to use a tampon again, are personal positions arising from her experience, not medical guidelines.
- Details of her medical course (1% survival chance, 100 lb of fluid, 24 hours without pain medicine) are her recollections of events she was often unconscious or heavily medicated for, and may not match the medical record exactly.

## Summary

Lauren Wasser recounts how, at 24, toxic shock syndrome from a tampon she was using normally left her 10 minutes from death, in a coma with two heart attacks and organ failure, and led to the amputation of her right leg — followed by 8 months in a wheelchair, daily suicidal thoughts, and 7 years of pain before she chose to amputate her left leg too, a decision she says gave her freedom. Now known for her gold prosthetic legs, she campaigns for transparency and safer menstrual products, arguing that what happened to her was preventable and that her job is done only when no other woman faces the same risk.
