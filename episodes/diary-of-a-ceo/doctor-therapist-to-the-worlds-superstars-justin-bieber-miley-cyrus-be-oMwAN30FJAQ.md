## Important tidbits
- Amen's core thesis: most psychiatric illnesses are not "mental illnesses" but brain health issues — "when you reimagine mental health as brain health, it changes everything." He argues psychiatry is the only specialty that never looks at the organ it treats.
- His clinics have performed over 225,000 brain SPECT scans (he later says 230,000). SPECT is a nuclear medicine study of blood flow and activity, showing whether brain activity is good, too low, or too high.
- He evaluates patients through four circles: biology (physical brain/body function), psychology (thoughts, including "ANTs" — automatic negative thoughts), social (relationships, job, money), and spiritual (meaning and purpose). He likens the brain to hardware and the mind to software.
- Celebrity patients he names publicly: he has been Miley Cyrus's doctor for 11 years and appears in Justin Bieber's docu-series *Seasons*; Bella Hadid publicly said she stopped drinking because of him. He says four of his patients have a billion followers.
- Population stats he cites: 50% of the population is diabetic or pre-diabetic, 72% are overweight or obese, and depression has gone up 400% since Prozac came on the market. He published three studies showing that as weight goes up, brain size and function go down.
- Diet claims from a Mayo Clinic study he cites: people eating a fat-based diet (nuts, seeds, green leafy vegetables, healthy fish, healthy oils) have 42% less risk of Alzheimer's disease, while a simple-carbohydrate-based diet (bread, pasta, potatoes, rice, fruit juice, sugar) carries a 400% increased risk. He recommends keeping fasting blood sugar under 90 and calls Alzheimer's "type 3 diabetes."
- His "mother tiny habit" (from BJ Fogg's work on behaviour change): at every decision point, ask "Is this good for my brain or bad for it?" — a 3-second question he says drives better choices when answered with information and self-love.
- The ACE (adverse childhood experiences) quiz: 10 common childhood traumas, from a CDC/Kaiser study of 17,000 people. Scoring 4 or more raises the risk of seven of the top 10 leading causes of death; 6 or more means dying 20 years earlier. He has collected 10,000 ACE scores from his own patients and found high scores tend to activate the medial frontal lobe, producing hyper-alertness. He calls EMDR his favourite psychotherapy for trauma.
- His BRIGHT MINDS framework covers 11 risk factors that "steal your mind": Blood flow, Retirement/aging, Inflammation, Genetics, Head trauma, Toxins, Mental health, Immunity/infections, Neurohormone issues, Diabesity, and Sleep. Low blood flow is, he says, the number one brain-imaging predictor of Alzheimer's.
- On caffeine: he calls it an addictive drug that constricts brain blood flow by 30% on SPECT, increases cortisol, and shrinks the hippocampus. A Venti Starbucks has 330 mg; his patient Jeff, a pilot, was drinking ~600 mg/day and tapered off by 5% a day over 3 weeks with no withdrawal headaches, reporting "unbelievable energy and clarity."
- Head trauma: mild traumatic brain injury is, he says, a major hidden cause of psychiatric problems. In his NFL study he scanned 350 players and reports 80% improved on a brain rehabilitation program; he recounts an MMA fighter whose scan looked "remarkably better" 2.5 hours after taking his NFL supplement formula.
- His nephew Andrew, aged 9, drew pictures of suicide and shooting other children; a scan revealed a golf-ball-sized cyst on his left temporal lobe, and after a neurosurgeon drained it, his behaviour returned completely to normal — the moment Amen says "the war began" for him to change psychiatry.
- On toxins: he says more than half of young men now have low-normal or low testosterone, which he attributes to hormone disruptors (parabens, phthalates) in personal care products; he recommends scanning products with the Think Dirty app or the Environmental Working Group's app (his Barbasol shaving cream scored 9/10 for toxicity; he switched to Kiss My Face, a 2).
- On vitamin D: low levels occur in ~60% of the population; he cites a prospective study in which people taking a vitamin D supplement had a 40% decreased risk of Alzheimer's, and recommends knowing your level and supplementing with D3 plus K2 if needed.
- Hormones: he recommends testing DHEA, testosterone, thyroid, and (for women) estrogen and progesterone every year. Women's progesterone drops about 10 years before menopause, which he links to new anxiety and insomnia around age 40 being treated with Ambien, Xanax, and Lexapro instead.
- Brain types and happiness: he describes 16 brain types built from five primary ones — balanced, spontaneous, persistent, sensitive, and cautious — and argues knowing your type (and your partner's or children's) predicts where you'll struggle. In his "Couples from Hell" study of 500 couples who had failed marital therapy, 80% had scans showing one or both partners needed a brain "tune-up."
- Sleep advice: avoid caffeine, warm/noisy/lit rooms, and blue light after dark; don't eat within 3 hours of bed (eating late makes you a "non-dipper" whose blood pressure doesn't fall overnight, raising heart attack and stroke risk). His own nightly ritual is prayer plus reviewing "what went well today," which he says increases happiness after 3 weeks.

## Claims to be careful with
- Brain SPECT imaging for psychiatric diagnosis is Amen's signature method but remains controversial and outside mainstream psychiatric practice; he himself describes being ostracised by colleagues over it, and SPECT is not a standard diagnostic tool for conditions like depression or ADD.
- Several headline statistics are stated without full sourcing in the conversation and should be treated as his claims, not established fact: depression up 400% since Prozac, 42% lower / 400% higher Alzheimer's risk by diet type (attributed to a Mayo Clinic study), caffeine shrinking the hippocampus, and 40% lower Alzheimer's risk from vitamin D supplements.
- The claim that large-scale studies show antidepressants are "no better than placebo" is a contested characterisation of the literature.
- The Holland elimination-diet study he cites (300 children with ADD, 72% no longer meeting criteria after 3 months) and the single-patient stories (the MMA fighter improving in 2.5 hours, his nephew's cyst) are anecdotes or single studies, not proof of general efficacy.
- His attribution of falling testosterone mainly to cosmetic-product toxins, and of his wife's commitment difficulties to PCOS, are personal interpretations rather than established causal findings.
- Amen sells supplements, books, clinic scans, and courses (including "Brain Thrive by 25" and his "Focus and Energy" formula), so his recommendations carry a commercial interest.

## Summary
Psychiatrist and brain-imaging pioneer Dr Daniel Amen argues that mental illness should be reframed as brain health, built on 225,000+ SPECT scans at his clinics and illustrated through celebrity patients (Miley Cyrus, Justin Bieber, Bella Hadid), his father's late-life transformation, and his nephew's brain cyst. He lays out his four-circle model of a person, the BRIGHT MINDS risk factors, brain types, and practical habits — from the "is this good for my brain?" question to sleep, diet, hormone testing, and toxin avoidance — while Steven presses him on forgiveness, self-worth, and why people fail to act on what they know.
