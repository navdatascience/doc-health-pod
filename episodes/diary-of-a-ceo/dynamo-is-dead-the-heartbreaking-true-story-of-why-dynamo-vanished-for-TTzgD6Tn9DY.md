# “Dynamo Is Dead!” The Heartbreaking True Story Of Why Dynamo Vanished For Years!

- **Source:** The Diary Of A CEO
- **Guest:** Dynamo (Steven), magician and illusionist
- **YouTube:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TTzgD6Tn9DY
- **Video ID:** TTzgD6Tn9DY
- **Collected:** 2026-11-18

## Important tidbits
- Dynamo was diagnosed with Crohn's disease at about 13 years old (in 1995); he describes it as a lifelong, potentially life-threatening inflammation of the bowel — like "an open wound" inside the stomach that eating constantly re-irritates — and he has had several life-threatening operations, including surgery at 17 that removed part of his stomach/bowel.
- A side effect he suffers from is reactive arthritis: when his Crohn's flares (which stress alone can trigger), his joints seize up and ache, he gets shooting pains "like electric shocks," and at times he cannot hold his cards, walk, or get out of bed.
- In 2017, during his "Seeing is Believing" live tour era, he got campylobacter food poisoning on top of the Crohn's — vomiting blood, rushed to hospital, morphine not touching the pain — which caused the Crohn's to spread to a different part of his bowel and triggered the reactive arthritis for the first time.
- He then spent years cycling through medical trials, noting each new medication had to be tried for 3 months before doctors would write it off, even when he knew within 2 weeks it wasn't working; the 9th-month medication, infliximab (an 8-hour hospital drip every few weeks), finally worked and gave him "a new lease of life."
- COVID stopped the infliximab from working, putting him back on medical trials in 2020; a working medication was only found at the beginning of 2021, and even on it he says he still has about 3 bad days a week.
- His rock bottom was 6 November 2020, the day he tried to kill himself; his wife found him unconscious in the dog bed and called his gastroenterologist/Crohn's specialist rather than an ambulance, and he came round while she was on the phone crying.
- He describes the suicidal thinking plainly: he felt he had so many problems he couldn't solve even one of them, and believed "if I'm not here, then everybody else might be happier" — he felt he himself was the problem.
- Before that he was self-harming and, when he saw himself in a mirror, would slam his head into it repeatedly to try to make the "noise" in his head go away; he says he sometimes hurt himself worse on earlier occasions, but had never been *found* before.
- His wife is the one who got him into therapy; he credits her non-judgmental response — she "didn't make me feel ashamed" — and her love with keeping him alive, and he names his therapist, Edward Sim, as someone who offered him therapy for free.
- Several crises converged: his grandmother ("nana"), his biggest supporter and "backbone," died in the previous couple of years; his body was deteriorating; and a legal/management dispute meant he lost the rights to use the name Dynamo and his social media channels.
- His grandfather taught him magic as a bullied child on a Bradford council estate (his father, who is Pathan, went to prison when Dynamo was about 4; his mother had him at 16) — magic was a way to get positive attention and "stand out in a different way."
- His comeback is the Sky show "Dynamo Is Dead," built around the idea that "the magic is in other people," followed by him literally burying himself alive as a "cleansing" to kill off the old Dynamo identity — an idea he'd held since 7 May 2021.
- He says he is still dealing with the mental-health side now, and that a core pain is imposter feelings: "I'm a magician who feels like I can't do the first thing that a magician needs to do — perform magic."
- He explicitly wants the show to tell people from working-class backgrounds like his that "it's okay to ask for help, it's okay to be broken," countering the "suck it up, be a man" culture he grew up in.

## Claims to be careful with
- This is a personal memoir-style interview, not medical guidance: the details of his Crohn's course, medication responses (including COVID stopping infliximab working), and trial timelines are his individual experience and should not be generalised to other Crohn's patients.
- The episode discusses suicide and self-harm in detail, including method-adjacent description; his account of what helped (a supportive partner, therapy) is anecdotal, not a treatment protocol.
- Figures and dates (diagnosis age, operation history, tour details) are as he recalls them in conversation and are not independently verified in the episode.
- The legal dispute over the Dynamo name is only described in outline because it is a live legal matter; his side is the only one presented.

## Summary
Magician Dynamo (Steven) explains why he vanished after 2017: campylobacter food poisoning catastrophically worsened his lifelong Crohn's disease, spreading it and triggering reactive arthritis that left him unable to hold cards or walk, followed by years of failed medication trials, a working drug (infliximab) that COVID then neutralised, the death of his grandmother, and a legal dispute that cost him his own stage name. The collapse culminated in a suicide attempt on 6 November 2020, after which his wife found him and got him into therapy. He is returning with the Sky show "Dynamo Is Dead" — ending with him burying himself alive — reframing his purpose as finding "the magic in other people" and showing working-class audiences it is okay to ask for help.
