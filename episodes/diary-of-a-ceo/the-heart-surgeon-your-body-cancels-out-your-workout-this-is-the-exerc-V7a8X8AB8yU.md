# The Heart Surgeon: Your Body Cancels Out Your Workout! This Is The Exercise That Actually Works!

- **Source:** The Diary Of A CEO
- **Guest:** Dr Philip Ovadia, heart surgeon and author of *Stay Off My Operating Table*
- **YouTube:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V7a8X8AB8yU

## Important tidbits
- Ovadia has performed over 3,000 heart surgeries, yet at around 40 he was morbidly obese — 100 lb heavier than he is today — and pre-diabetic, and realised he was "going to end up on my own operating table." His father had heart surgery and his grandmother died of heart disease.
- His turning point was hearing journalist Gary Taubes (author of *The Case Against Sugar*) speak at a heart surgeons' conference: the idea that the types of food we eat may matter more than the amount sent him down a path of cutting sugar and changing food quality — and, unlike his earlier calorie-counting, low-fat attempts, the weight loss was still maintained 8 years later.
- He argues very little of today's chronic disease is truly genetic: human genetics don't change in 100–150 years, yet obesity, diabetes and heart disease (almost undescribed ~120 years ago) have exploded in that window — so the cause must be environmental, mainly food.
- He defines metabolic health as the body properly using its inputs: food should be turned into immediate energy, used to build/repair tissue, or only a little stored. In the modern environment that system breaks — we store too much and can never access the stored energy — producing heart disease, diabetes, cancer and Alzheimer's. He repeatedly cites that 88% of adults are metabolically unhealthy, and that 600,000 people die of heart disease every year.
- The emotional core of his book: a 38-year-old mother died on his table after a 10+ hour operation for an aortic dissection caused by high blood pressure; he had to tell her young children she wasn't coming home, and he believes her condition was 100% preventable through food. In 20 years his patients have got younger — from mostly 60–80-year-olds to routinely 40-year-olds.
- His diet answer: the common thread across carnivore, keto, Mediterranean, paleo and vegan diets that work is eating whole, real food and eliminating processed food. His rule: "eat the things that grow in the ground and eat the things that eat the things that grow in the ground."
- He favours animal products over plants, arguing humans evolved eating meat and that ruminant animals are designed to extract nutrients from plants which we then get from their meat; a well-built vegan diet beats the standard Western diet but, he says, needs supplementation and hard planning long-term, whereas an optimal human diet shouldn't require supplements.
- First of his seven principles for metabolic health: reframe health as a system, not a goal — short-term goals ("lose 20 lb") end in either relapse after success or giving up after failure, while habits that support a system are sustainable.
- On exercise: cardio is "an unreliable tactic for fat loss" and not effective for weight loss, for two reasons — most people get hungry after cardio and eat back the calories, and the calories-in/calories-out model itself is flawed. He wants people to focus on activity throughout the whole day rather than one gym hour, and on building and maintaining muscle, because muscle is metabolically active all day.
- He says processed food and sugar are addictive — citing the claim that sugar lights up the brain's addiction centres as much as or more than heroin — and that the food industry puts sugar in everything deliberately. From his own experience, the compulsion fades once you quit: 10 years ago he always wanted the Girl Scout cookies; today he can take or leave them.
- He advises judging your own flexibility by metabolic measures and tools like a continuous glucose monitor: when he was obese, every cookie made him more unhealthy; now metabolically healthy, an occasional cookie "is not going to break the system."

## Claims to be careful with
- "Sugar is more addictive than heroin" is a contested pop-science claim drawn from animal/brain-imaging studies; Ovadia presents it as established fact, but addiction researchers dispute the comparison.
- His position that cardio does not help weight or fat loss overstates the evidence: trials show modest fat-loss effects from aerobic exercise, and his dismissal of calories-in/calories-out is a minority position in obesity medicine, even if diet quality and compensation effects are real.
- The "88% of adults are not healthy" figure refers to one US study's strict five-marker definition of metabolic health; it is definition-sensitive and US-specific, and the episode applies it loosely.
- His pro-meat, anti-supplementation stance ("our ancestors didn't evolve to take supplements, so we shouldn't either") is an evolutionary argument, not trial evidence, and contradicts mainstream dietary guidance; he also sells a book and a metabolic-health programme built on these views.

## Summary
Heart surgeon Dr Philip Ovadia — who has done over 3,000 heart surgeries but was himself morbidly obese and pre-diabetic at 40 — argues that chronic disease is overwhelmingly caused by food, not genetics, and that the breakdown of "metabolic health" (he cites 88% of adults as unhealthy) is the root of heart disease, diabetes, cancer and Alzheimer's. Inspired by Gary Taubes, he lost 100 lb by cutting sugar and processed food and keeping it off for 8 years. His prescription: eat whole real food (he personally favours animal products), treat health as a system of habits rather than a short-term goal, prioritise all-day activity and muscle-building over cardio — which he says your body cancels out — and use metabolic markers or a CGM to judge how much flexibility your body can handle.
