## Important tidbits
- On K-pop "rules": Jackson says his company never explicitly banned dating — dating was simply a topic idols didn't discuss publicly. During training/early debut the members handed in their phones for about a year, until they pushed back and got them back.
- In the first two years of his career, everything he posted on social media needed approvals; he says entertainers have a responsibility to be careful because posts can be received in "a million different ways."
- The viral claim that idols can't sneeze, burp or yawn in public is, in his framing, just common-sense manners for the setting you're in — "not an idol thing."
- He debuted at 19, missed out on his youth, and worked more than 10 years without real rest — always "outputting" but never receiving, thinking or reflecting — before taking a year off.
- His album Magic Man 2 is built from personal diaries and has four chapters — manic highs, losing control, realization, and acceptance — which he says is the process of any situation in life. Songs he names include "High Alone," "GBAD," and the closing "Made Me a Man."
- A trusted "brother" figure in a working relationship took advantage of his trust and "took everything away"; he says the real loss was the trust, not the material. He wanted revenge at first, and it took him about a year to accept it and move on.
- His first heartbreak was in grade 6: after holding hands for the first time at the movies (watching Narnia), his girlfriend texted him a few days later that she was breaking up with him for another boy; he cried on a bus in the rain listening to Eminem's "Stan" on his MP3 player.
- His definition of ideal love: a partner who has her own craft and dream and is super focused on it, while he is focused on his — "together we empower each other." He describes marriage as finding a life partner/best friend who is there for each other "till the day we leave."
- His parents are his idols: his mother moved from Shanghai to Hong Kong and worked three to four jobs a day for roughly 30 years, eating leftovers so her sons ate first, with one goal — saving enough to give him and his brother a house so they'd always have somewhere to live. His father moved from Guangzhou to Hong Kong and became head coach of Team Hong Kong. Jackson lives with his parents in Shanghai.
- On turning 30 (he is 31): he says it felt like one day his body told him "we're going to shut down" — slower recovery and getting sleepy after meals — so he feels he has to take extra care of his body and work harder on himself.
- His routine on free days: sleep around 8–9 pm, wake at 5–6 am, work out on an empty stomach, shower, then a green juice. Later he has one big meal (salad/noodles or similar) and an Americano after eating.
- His green juice recipe (which he says is designed for detox and anti-inflammation without bloating): a little ginger, a little peeled garlic, half a lemon, celery, one or two broccoli florets, one banana, a little turmeric powder, four to five blueberries, collagen powder "if you're over 30," parsley, cucumber cut into three or four pieces, ice, and coconut water.
- Supplements he takes after his post-meal coffee, based on his own body-check report: vitamin C, a probiotic/"super enzyme," B12, B6, vitamin D3, folic acid, and omega-3.
- On skincare: he does no multi-step cosmetic routine — he attributes skin to lifestyle: whether you're going to the gym enough, sleeping enough, and what you eat and drink.
- On money: more than half of what he earns goes to his mother, and he spends heavily on production rather than personal luxury. He once lent someone roughly 30–40% of his income for what was claimed to be a family need; the person and the money vanished, and he is now very cautious about lending.

## Claims to be careful with
- Jackson's description of turning 30 — that the body and immune system suddenly "break down" and recovery switches off almost overnight — is his personal anecdote, not a medical threshold.
- The green juice is described as "detox" and anti-inflammatory; "detox" benefits from juices are a wellness framing rather than an established medical effect.
- His supplement stack is explicitly based on his own body-check report, and he admits he has forgotten what the folic acid is for — it is a personal regimen, not general advice.
- Raj cites a study he read "3–4 days ago" (2 million people across 15–16 countries; three out of four people never got money lent to friends back) from memory — the figures are unverified.

## Summary
Jackson Wang talks openly about the hidden machinery and costs of K-pop stardom — phone confiscation, social-media approvals, and a decade without rest — and how betrayal by a trusted partner pushed him into a year of reflection that produced Magic Man 2, an album structured as highs, losing control, realization, and acceptance. He shares his first heartbreak at age 11–12, defines ideal love as two focused people empowering each other, credits his parents' sacrifices as his foundation, and lays out his health routine: early sleep, fasted morning workouts, a daily green juice, one big meal, and a supplement stack driven by his own medical report.
