## Important tidbits
- A baby's skin tone is genetic: saffron, tea and ubtan do not lighten it, and the true tone settles by about 6 months of age, per Dr Gupta.
- Five common mistakes he lists: (1) excess oil on the scalp, which worsens cradle cap — the soft spot closes on its own at 9–18 months regardless; (2) oil in the private parts/foreskin — do nothing there for the first 6 months, as interference risks phimosis/stricture problems; (3) ubtan/besan pastes on newborn skin; (4) oil in the ears and nose; (5) squeezing a newborn's breasts — the "witch's milk" swelling is hormonal and squeezing can cause an abscess.
- Vigorous massage is a real injury risk: he says he sees 2–3 babies a week with clavicle injuries from hard massage. Massage's proven benefits are bonding, digestion/colic relief and sleep — not demonstrated bone strength.
- Safest massage oil per him: coconut oil. He does not recommend mustard oil, saying roughly 40% of babies react to it.
- Mundan (ceremonial head shaving) does not make hair grow back thicker — that is a myth, he says.
- Product rules: pH 5.5, paraben-free (he links parabens to endocrine disruption and later PCOS/infertility risk), alcohol-free and talc-free. He cites the AAP warning on talc — inhalation risk and an asbestos/cancer link — and says no powder under 6 months and never near the face.
- His CMP routine: Cleanse, Moisturise (within 5–10 minutes of the bath), and Protect with physical/mineral sunscreen only — and no sunscreen at all under 6 months; use shade and clothing instead.
- Regular soap is pH 9–10 and too harsh; syndet bars at pH 5.5 are preferred, and he advises against Dettol baths. Newborn skin is about 30% thinner than adult skin and loses water about 5× faster.
- Until the umbilical cord falls off (about 7–10 days), sponge with lukewarm water only, and put nothing on the cord — he describes sepsis admissions caused by ghee, oil or cow dung applied to it.
- Take the baby out after about 6 weeks, and do not let strangers kiss the baby. Preterm babies' skin is about 10× thinner: no products until the baby reaches 2 kg, and keep them covered (cap, mittens, socks) with one extra layer.
- Diapers: disposables are fine for the first 3 months; change at least every 3 hours — prevention beats treatment for diaper rash. At home use cotton and lukewarm water; reserve water-based wipes for travel.
- Rashes not to panic over: stork bites/angel kisses, baby acne (clears in 4–6 weeks), milia, and erythema toxicum (appears at 48–72 hours, gone by about 14 days). Introduce one nut at a time when weaning, and watch for cow's-milk protein allergy signs.

## Claims to be careful with
- The episode is brand-sponsored (Cetaphil) and the guest endorses that brand's products throughout — treat product recommendations as partly promotional.
- The figure that ~40% of babies react to mustard oil is the guest's clinical estimate, not a cited study.
- The suggested link between parabens in baby products and later PCOS/infertility is presented as a risk concern; the causal evidence in infants is not established.
- Specific timings (e.g., no products for preterm babies until 2 kg) reflect the guest's practice guidance; individual NICU advice may differ.

## Summary
Paediatrician Dr Arpit Gupta works through the newborn-care mistakes he sees most: oil in the scalp, ears, nose and private parts, ubtan on newborn skin, squeezing "witch's milk" breasts, and massage hard enough to injure the clavicle. He sets out product rules (pH 5.5, paraben-, alcohol- and talc-free), a cleanse–moisturise–protect routine, strict umbilical-cord hygiene, diaper-changing discipline, and a calm guide to the normal rashes parents panic over — in an episode sponsored by Cetaphil, whose products he recommends by name.
