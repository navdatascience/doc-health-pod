## Important tidbits
- Dr Nair's core thesis: genes cannot be changed, but their expression can be controlled through epigenetics — lifestyle "blocks the triggers" of disease variants you carry.
- Raj's own 65-page genetic predisposition report (from Nair's program) showed higher risk for LDL cholesterol, myocardial infarction, and a prolonged QT interval linked to sudden cardiac arrest.
- Because of the prolonged QT finding, HIIT and marathons were not recommended for Raj unless managed with functional-medicine support; his magnesium absorption is genetically low, requiring roughly 400 mg/day.
- Nair tells of a Gujarat multimillionaire marathoner with prolonged QT who collapsed at the 2023 Delhi marathon, then resumed marathons after magnesium IV treatment.
- He warns that trend diets and workouts can backfire genetically: a keto diet is dangerous if your fat-metabolism genes are impaired. He cites Angelina Jolie's BRCA case, saying her risk fell from 89% to 2% after mastectomy, as he stated it.
- His EPLIMO program is built on three "codes" — DNA, metabolic profile, and microbiome — and costs ₹45,000 including three months of handholding/coaching; the genetic test itself is once-in-a-lifetime.
- He argues many biohacking techniques are rebranded Eastern practices: breathwork is pranayama, plus grounding/earthing and fermented foods.
- On the brain: his PhD area is thought-process re-engineering. Thoughts come from stored data (the brain stores experience from three months before birth); you cannot change thoughts directly, but you can insert a "choice" filter between thought and action.
- He says the non-conscious brain controls 93–96% of behaviour versus 4–6% conscious, and that the brain defaults to the path of least resistance — habits form by repetition: "repetition is the master of the brain."
- Brain foods he recommends: A2 desi ghee (a spoonful on an empty stomach) because the brain is mostly fat, and coffee for its polyphenol antioxidants, not just caffeine; he cautions that cholesterol medication can negatively affect the brain.
- BDNF (brain-derived neurotrophic factor) is described as a "fertilizer" for brain hormones, raised by exercise, dance, movement, and controlled stress.
- On burnout: it happens when the body and brain cannot support where the mind wants to go; the only fix, he says, is raising brain and body function — starting with sleep, then gut, then brain (serotonin and melatonin are gut-linked).
- He ranks sleep above exercise under all circumstances: if you slept badly, sleep the extra hour instead of going to the gym, and never pair hard exercise with junk food.
- On supplements: soil magnesium is depleted (he cites a survey finding 58% of "organic" vegetables carried chemicals), so personalised supplements are necessary — his generic shortlist is natural vitamin C, occasional vitamin E, omega-3, magnesium, a pro/prebiotic, and a natural multivitamin; he criticises biohackers taking 52–100 pills a day as "carpet bombing."
- His ideal "superhuman life": celebrate your 100th birthday after completing a 5 km run with your great-grandchildren, then go do work you love — he points to Blue Zones such as parts of Japan and Sardinia, where he says people live to 120.

## Claims to be careful with
- The claim that changing your ZIP code/country is "the WHO's third largest cause of death" (via gene triggering) is stated without a verifiable source and should be treated as the guest's assertion.
- His speculation that COVID vaccines may have triggered gene variants in some people is explicitly offered as a guess in the episode.
- The book claim he endorses — that by 2045 "death becomes optional" (from "The Death of Death" by José Cordeiro and David Wood) — is futurist speculation, not established science.
- Gene-editing and "designer baby" discussion (editing for intelligence or athleticism) is presented as emerging possibility; he concedes it is peripheral and not possible today.
- The guest sells the ₹45,000 EPLIMO program discussed throughout; recommendations for genetic testing and personalised supplement stacks come from an interested party.

## Summary
Longevity researcher Dr Sajeev Nair argues that your genes load the gun but lifestyle pulls the trigger: through epigenetic testing (DNA, metabolic, and microbiome profiles) you can block the triggers for diseases you are predisposed to, from heart risk to depression. Using Raj's own genetic report as the worked example, he covers brain optimisation, ghee and coffee as brain foods, the primacy of sleep, personalised supplements, and his vision of a healthy 100-year "superhuman" life.
