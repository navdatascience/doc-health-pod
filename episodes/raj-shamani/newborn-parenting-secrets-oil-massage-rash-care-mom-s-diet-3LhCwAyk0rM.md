## Important tidbits
- Dr Nihar said fungal infection is the most common skin problem in Indian babies, and the biggest driver is heat and sweat — overheating, not cold, is the usual enemy.
- He said around 28.3% of infants have eczema or sensitive skin, and that dryness is the root ingredient behind most skin problems in children under three.
- Baby massage, he said, mainly aids brain and nerve development (skin has dermatomes connected to the brain), not stronger bones or joints — it must be gentle, never vigorous rubbing.
- Traditional practices he advised against: removing hair with flour dough, putting oil in or blowing into the nose and ears, and squeezing a baby's breasts ("witch's milk") — all of these can cause harm.
- Moisturize a baby's skin twice daily with gentle, pH ~5.5 products; he named ingredients such as calendula, vitamin E and chamomile as helpful.
- A mother's stress and sleep loss directly reduce her milk supply, he said; and a breastfeeding mother should continue feeding through a fever — infections spread by proximity, not through milk (use a mask).
- A newborn girl passing a little blood (pseudomenstruation) is normal and comes from the mother's hormones, he explained.
- Practical feeding and hygiene numbers he gave: feeds of about 10–15 minutes per side and no more than ~40 minutes total; 3–15 stools a day can be normal; change diapers every 3–4 hours; clean baby girls top-to-bottom because the short urethra raises UTI risk.
- Post-fever rashes he distinguished: dengue (itchy rash as the fever falls, when platelets drop), measles, and hand-foot-mouth disease, which he said is self-limiting but very contagious.
- He recommended keeping the baby's room at about 23–24 °C rather than 16–18 °C, because large temperature swings cause congestion.
- On the hygiene hypothesis, he said reactive airway disease in children has risen from about 1 in 5 to about 1 in 2, and that rural babies fall sick less — which he linked to air quality, crowding/ventilation and home-grown food.
- C-section babies' skin sensitivity, he said, tracks birth weight and prematurity rather than the delivery mode itself.
- Most birthmarks are temporary and fade, he said, but multiple or large café-au-lait spots need medical assessment; neonatal acne comes from hormones via the mother's milk and resolves on its own.
- Never let a baby air-dry after a bath — residual dampness invites infection — and dress babies in pure cotton rather than dry-fit fabrics, he advised.
- On the mother's diet: only about 1% of what she eats passes into the milk; he advised avoiding MSG-heavy Chinese food and raw food, and said alcohol requires pumping and discarding milk for 3–4 hours. For a colicky baby, he said the research-backed fix is the mother stopping dairy — and drinking milk does not increase milk production.
- Taste buds develop around 9–12 months, so he advised moving the baby to the family diet then, with a little salt from 9 months; refined sugar should be avoided, with dates/jaggery sweetness only in moderation.

## Claims to be careful with
- The specific figures he cited (28.3% infant eczema/sensitive skin; reactive airway disease rising from 1-in-5 to 1-in-2 children; ~1% of the mother's diet passing into milk) are presented as his statements and were not independently verified.
- His view that massage primarily benefits brain/nerve development rather than bones is his framing of the evidence.
- Product-ingredient endorsements (calendula, vitamin E, chamomile) reflect his clinical preference; individual babies can react to any ingredient.

## Summary
Pediatrician Dr Nihar walks through newborn skin, feeding and hygiene care for Indian parents: fungal infection and dryness are the most common problems, massage should be gentle and is mainly for brain development rather than bones, several traditional practices (flour-dough hair removal, oil in ears/nose, squeezing "witch's milk") should be dropped, and the mother's own stress, sleep and diet — including stopping dairy for a colicky baby — matter as much as anything applied to the baby.
