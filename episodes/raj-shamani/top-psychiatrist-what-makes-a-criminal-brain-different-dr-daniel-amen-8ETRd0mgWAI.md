## Important tidbits
- Dr Amen says he has scanned about 300,000 people (3 lakh, as Raj puts it), including about 1,000 convicted criminals and more than 100 murderers; in his published study of 40 murderers, he says, the younger ones had low frontal-lobe function and the older ones had globally low activity.
- The prefrontal cortex is "the brain's brake": it stops you saying or doing what you should not and plays out future consequences; everybody has weird, violent or sexual thoughts, he says, but with low prefrontal activity — from ADHD, alcohol (he gives six beers as an example), or repeated head impacts in football/soccer — the thought is acted on instead of evaluated, while too much frontal activity makes thoughts loop compulsively (e.g. road-rage rumination) toward a compulsive act.
- There is no single criminal brain, he says: impulsive murderers (low frontal function), compulsive murderers / serial killers (overactive front), traumatic brain injury, infections such as Lyme or COVID, and toxins such as a mould-filled environment can all produce violent behaviour — which is why, he argues, psychiatrists should look at the brain (SPECT imaging of blood flow/activity: good, too little, or too much) instead of guessing, and why depression, anxiety and ADHD each have multiple subtypes that should not all be treated the same.
- His favourite case (1995): his 9-year-old nephew Andrew attacked a girl on a baseball field unprovoked, had stopped smiling, and had drawn himself hanging and shooting other children; a scan found a cyst the size of a golf ball occupying the left temporal lobe — an area he had correlated with violence — and when a neurosurgeon drained it, he says, Andrew's behaviour returned completely to normal, showing some causes of violence are reversible.
- He assesses every person in four circles — biological (brain/body), psychological (mind/development), social (relationships/modelling) and spiritual (meaning/purpose) — and says a damaged-looking brain does not doom a person: he describes a spiritual teacher with a terrible scan who had been an alcoholic "rager" before transforming through not believing her thoughts, and estimates a person with a low-activity "holes" scan is about 10 times more likely to commit a criminal act, not certain to.
- "Brain reserve" starts before birth: he says a father's teenage smoking, a mother's chronic stress, illness or not wanting the pregnancy, and birth trauma (he says 3% of babies) all reduce reserve; vaginal delivery and breastfeeding populate a healthier gut microbiome (100 trillion gut bugs) than C-section, though C-section is better when a baby is in trouble; two soldiers in the same tank blast fare differently, he says, because of the brain each brought into the trauma.
- The number-one parental mistake ruining under-10s' brains is devices, he says — "dopamine destroyers" best kept from children as much as possible, noting Silicon Valley nanny contracts barring phones around kids — alongside early heading of soccer balls / tackle football, ultra-processed food (he says 70% of American food), lack of vitamin D from staying indoors, and parents treating marijuana and alcohol as harmless.
- Chronic childhood trauma heightens the emotional brain for years (his adverse-childhood-experiences study: more ACEs, more heightened emotional circuitry), so survivors notice what is wrong faster than what is right; but a person violent/angry for 10 years can change if they want to — his prescription is training positivity: start each day with "today is going to be a great day", notice little things you like during the day, and at bedtime ask "what went well today?" hour by hour, which he says raises happiness in 3 weeks, improves dreams/sleep, and thereby memory (the brain cleans itself in sleep; go to bed half an hour early).
- You are not stuck with your brain at any age — youngest transformer 6, oldest 94 in his practice; his 90-year-old father's hallucinations of little people on the ceiling cleared after 6 hyperbaric-oxygen sessions for low blood flow, he says; and the hippocampus ("seahorse") produces about 700 new baby stem cells daily at any age, which behaviour either grows (sleep, salad with wild salmon and olive oil, doing something you love that is not bad for your brain) or "murders" (alcohol, marijuana, not sleeping, mould, poor food, no exercise).
- His underrated exercise is Japanese walking — 3 minutes normal, 3 minutes as fast as you can, repeated 5 times — which he says beats plain 10,000 steps for vascular flexibility, plus weight lifting (stronger as you age = less likely to get Alzheimer's, he says); the brain gets 20% of the body's blood flow, so anything damaging blood vessels damages the brain — he links erectile dysfunction rates (40% of 40-year-olds, 70% of 70-year-olds in the US) to brain dysfunction, and cites walking "like you're late" for 45 minutes 4 times a week as equally effective to the SSRI Zoloft at 12 weeks in a head-to-head study, without sexual side-effects or dependence.
- BRIGHT Minds: to keep a brain healthy or rescue it you must prevent/treat 11 major risk factors, starting with B = blood flow; blood flow is damaged by alcohol, marijuana, being overweight, not sleeping, being sedentary and low-quality food, and increased by exercise, beets and ginkgo, he says — and very few psychiatrists, he notes, talk about blood pressure even though about 60% of the population is hypertensive or pre-hypertensive.
- Successful people differ from criminals in focus and balance, not simply in obsession/aggression: obsession becomes compulsion/pathological when you cannot stop even though it is hurting you and your family; his "one page miracle" exercise has patients write what they want across relationships, work, money and physical, emotional and spiritual health, then check whether behaviour fits; and some anxiety is protective — he targets about 15% rather than zero, saying people with low anxiety / low physiological reactivity go to jail more and lie detectors fail on them, while free-solo climber Alex Honnold's low-reactivity amygdala is an advantage only for that specific goal.
- Loneliness is "really bad for the brain" — chronic loneliness has the same negative health effects as smoking 15 cigarettes a day, per the Surgeon General report he cites; friendships are down 40% since 1990, and social-media algorithms keep people by making them angry/anxious and more extreme; the antidote he teaches is managing the mind: do not believe every stupid thought you think — write the thought down when sad/mad/nervous/out of control, and apply Byron Katie's questions (Is it true? Absolutely true? How does it make you feel? How would you feel without it? Turn it to its opposite), an exercise he says becomes automatic after about 30 repetitions.
- Chronic pain is partly brain circuitry ("Change Your Brain, Change Your Pain"): pain, rejection and suffering share circuitry in a "doom loop" (pain → automatic negative thoughts → tension → more pain → bad habits), while a "healing loop" starts with curiosity not fury, calming the circuit (he highlights saffron — which he says reduces pain and depression — plus curcumin and zinc, relaxation/hypnosis), and processing repressed rage (following John Sarno's work); he notes 80% of pain-free people his age have terrible MRIs, so a bad MRI alone does not explain chronic pain.
- Hope is the one trait he would build into an exceptional brain — defined as agency plus multiple pathways ("tomorrow can be better, and I have lots of ways to get there"); it is teachable by eliminating blame (100% responsibility for your response), and generating four ways to get what you want; the three thoughts not to believe are "you're not enough", "I can't", and "I'm stuck with the brain I have" — reducing everything to one question: "Is what I'm doing now good for my brain or bad for it?"
- Ranking brain-damaging habits, direct poisons are worst — alcohol and marijuana (in his study of 64,000 people, he says, marijuana was worse than alcohol, though he adds he is not sure he believes that; also nicotine) — then pornography (dopamine circuits), sugar/pro-inflammatory and bad oils and not exercising near the top, doomscrolling in the top 10, and watching morning news (27% less happy in the afternoon, he says); outsourcing thinking to AI is bad if AI thinks for you (lifting 1 lb instead of 25 lb) but positive if it helps you think/teaches you; fame likewise wears out dopamine circuits, which he links to the misery he describes in famous patients.
- His 30-day routine for a distracted, anxious young adult: the daily positivity start and "what went well" close, consistent sleep time, sunlight, exercise and hydration, a day-1 "one page miracle", questioning negative thoughts in writing, a multivitamin and omega-3 (plus vitamin D / brain-type supplements such as saffron for sadness or theanine for anxiety, after blood work — "I can't change what I don't measure"), and table tennis as the best sport because eyes, hands, feet and spin-reading together activate the cerebellum.

## Claims to be careful with
- SPECT brain imaging for psychiatric diagnosis and criminal-risk prediction is Dr Amen's signature method and remains contested in mainstream psychiatry; his claims in the episode — including estimating criminal likelihood (about 10x) from a scan's appearance — are not presented with peer-reviewed validation in the conversation and should not be treated as established diagnostic practice.
- Many precise figures are stated without sources shown: 700 new hippocampal stem cells a day, walking equalling Zoloft at 12 weeks, morning news making people 27% less happy, loneliness equalling 15 cigarettes a day, and the 64,000-person marijuana-vs-alcohol study; verify each against primary literature before quoting.
- Product-linked recommendations appear throughout — his own saffron/curcumin supplement ("happy saffron", described as a best-seller), hyperbaric oxygen therapy (89 sessions in one case, 6 in another), and brain-type supplements — where he has a commercial interest through Amen Clinics and related products.
- The nephew cyst case and celebrity-patient anecdotes (named public figures) are single cases told by Dr Amen; they illustrate his approach but do not by themselves establish that scanning or draining explains violence generally.

## Summary
Psychiatrist Dr Daniel Amen tells Raj Shamani that criminal and self-destructive behaviour often has a brain basis visible on the SPECT scans he says he has performed on about 300,000 people — younger murderers with low activity in the prefrontal "brake", compulsive killers with an overactive front, and reversible causes from head injury, infection, mould and even a golf-ball-sized temporal-lobe cyst in his own nephew — but that biology is only one of four circles (with mind, relationships and purpose), and a damaged brain can be improved at any age through blood-flow habits (Japanese walking, lifting, sleep, no alcohol/marijuana), training positivity ("what went well today?"), questioning negative thoughts, and asking of every action: is this good for my brain or bad for it?
