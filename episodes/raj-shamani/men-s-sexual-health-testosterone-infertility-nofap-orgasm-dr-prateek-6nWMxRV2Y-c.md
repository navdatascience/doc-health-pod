## Important tidbits
- Dr Prateek says erectile dysfunction can be an early sign of heart disease — if erections fail, the underlying vascular problem may not yet be big, so it should be checked rather than ignored.
- Morning erections are his quick self-test: during good sleep men get 3–5 erections a night; if the frequency of waking with an erection drops noticeably over 3–4 months, that signals a possible problem.
- Premature ejaculation has hormonal causes (imbalances in luteinizing hormone, oxytocin, prolactin), psychological causes, and physical causes such as pelvic infection/surgery or an over-sensitive glans.
- Techniques he describes for lasting longer: pace the build-up with foreplay, the start-and-stop method, the squeeze method, masturbating 30–60 minutes before sex (keeping the refractory period in mind), and "climax delay" condoms containing lignocaine gel that numbs the glans; even a normal condom reduces sensitivity.
- The refractory period averages 15–30 minutes, though it can be as short as 5 minutes; two rounds is a reasonable norm for a healthy man, he says.
- On the hymen/virginity question: he says the hymen may be absent from birth and can break through sports, horse riding or swimming — and that "virginity" does not exist in medical literature; it is a social construct, not a medical term.
- Infertility has not risen as much as testing has, he argues: testing is more accessible and the taboo lower, so more cases surface; lifestyle and environmental factors add a real increase on top.
- Volatile organic compounds in fragrances, lotions and vehicle exhaust affect sperm production; his advice on what individuals can control is to stop smoking and cut alcohol.
- Vaping, he says, has the same effects as cigarettes minus the tar: nicotine effects remain, the flavouring liquids are carcinogenic when burnt, and because vaping can be done indoors its frequency rises — he describes a condition he calls "vape punch" where lung volume shrinks and air sacs collapse, which can be fatal if ignored.
- Sperm quality rests on three things — production (testis), maturation (epididymis) and transport (vas deferens); sperm make up only about 5–15% of semen volume, the rest being secretions from three glands along the route.
- He recommends three supplements for essentially everyone (unless allergic): a multivitamin, antioxidants (to reverse oxidative damage to sperm and DNA), and folic acid — antioxidants typically need 3–6 months of use, taken like a multivitamin over the long term.
- Testosterone is falling, he says, mainly because of sedentary "couch potato" lifestyles, processed food, lack of sunlight and chronic stress raising cortisol; he also cites a study he read that men with low testosterone tend to be angrier and more depressed, while high-testosterone men are calmer — the opposite of the "alpha male" stereotype.
- The pull-out method is unreliable: "you only need to fail once," and he says his lab has examined pre-ejaculate samples under a slide and found actively motile sperm in them.
- Nightfall is completely normal — sperm production runs daily on a roughly three-month cycle, and unused sperm is expelled; it has nothing to do with high or low testosterone.
- Masturbation does not lower testosterone or sperm quality; its benefits, per Dr Prateek, include relaxation, better sleep, lower stress and it being the safest form of sex; the standard abstinence window for a semen analysis is 2–7 days.
- He cites a 2020 "Sex in India" survey in which ~70% of men reported orgasm every time during sex versus ~30% of women — the "orgasm gap" — which he attributes largely to missing foreplay and poor sex education, noting the clitoris has 8,000–15,000 nerve endings and most women do not orgasm from penetration alone.

## Claims to be careful with
- The claim that penis-enlargement pills sold in shops work — he explicitly says they do not increase size; no product claim is endorsed in the episode.
- Anabolic steroids: he states long-term steroid use affects sexual health, sperm quality and the body's own testosterone production — presented as his clinical position.
- The "vape punch" lung condition and its fatality risk are described by the guest without a cited study in the episode.
- The testosterone-and-aggression reversal (low testosterone = angrier men) rests on "a study I read," unnamed in the episode.
- Gene editing / "Barbie baby" designer babies: he is clear this is still theoretical and experimental, tested on animals, and not in commercial human use — food, yoga or positions cannot determine a baby's gender.

## Summary
Embryologist Dr Prateek Makwana answers common men's sexual-health questions in Hindi: premature ejaculation techniques, erectile dysfunction as an early heart-disease signal, why testosterone is falling (sedentary lifestyle, stress, poor food), why the pull-out method fails, the normality of nightfall and masturbation, supplements he recommends for sperm quality, and the male–female orgasm gap — while debunking myths about virginity, enlargement pills and designer babies.
