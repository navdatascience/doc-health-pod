## Important tidbits
- Note: this is a nation-building and geopolitics episode rather than a medical one — the health content is incidental (an opening remark on fasting, and a closing discussion of habits and happiness), and the tidbits below reflect what was actually discussed.
- Opening point: Indians embrace "intermittent fasting" but feel embarrassed about vrat (traditional religious fasting) — the same practice becomes acceptable when given an English name and a Japanese theory, he says; practices rooted in Indian tradition get dismissed as not matching one's "class."
- India is, in his telling, the only country that has successfully tackled terrorism on multiple fronts — Jammu & Kashmir, the Khalistan movement in Punjab, the Northeast, and Naxalism, which once covered roughly 30–40% of India's districts.
- India runs free food for about 85 crore (850 million) people with a five-year commitment — a country once branded as starving now runs the world's largest free-food scheme, he says.
- India should aim to be a "Vishwamitra" (friend of the world), not a superpower: "When India grows, the world is happy; India's decline is the world's decline."
- On Vaccine Maitri: India sent COVID vaccines to African and other countries that could not afford them while, he says, Canada had bought seven to eight vaccines per person. He frames it not as one leader's act but as the nation's collective consciousness permitting and welcoming it.
- Why Chandrayaan-3 went to the Moon's south pole: the region holds the solar system's second-biggest crater (about 13 km deep), and permanently shadowed crater areas — where sunlight has never reached in billions of years — can preserve water as ice; liquid water cannot survive on the airless Moon.
- Lunar water can be split into hydrogen (rocket fuel) and oxygen (for burning fuel and for breathing), making the Moon a future launch station, since launching from the Moon is far cheaper due to low gravity.
- Helium-3: lunar dust holds, by his figure, about 11 million tonnes of helium-3, which Earth essentially lacks; if fusion power is cracked, he claims converting the Moon's helium-3 could generate Earth's electricity for roughly 40,000 years, making the Moon a future powerhouse.
- The mission cost about ₹800–900 crore; he frames it as an investment, arguing that when space/Antarctica treaties lapse (he says around 2032–34), countries will claim the lunar regions they reached, and India can stake mining rights in the mineral-rich south polar region — worth potentially thousands of times the mission cost.
- Women in the workforce: Raj cites an article's figures — only about 19.7 women per 100 men hold manufacturing jobs, about 72% of women workers come from southern states, and a woman earns about ₹87 for the same work for which a man earns ₹100.
- Singh's counter-view: do not force 50-50 participation in every profession. Homemaking is an economic activity and an investment in the future — today's working generation is the product of mothers who did that full-time job — and many educated women choose it. What must end is unequal pay for equal work and anyone being forced out of the workforce; he notes some states have piloted pensions for homemakers (e.g. Uttar Pradesh, varying by number of children), and men can be homemakers too.
- Dr Kalam as a mentor: a mentee must figuratively "stand on the teacher's shoulders" — build your own wisdom on the teacher's knowledge and see the world through your own eyes. A mentor facilitates and offers contrary views but never dictates; the goal is to become your own unique self, not a copy. Kalam, a vegetarian, would quietly swap plates with the non-vegetarian Singh and never asked him to change.
- Kalam stayed a student until his last day: on the flight to Shillong on 27 July 2015, aged 83, he carried three heavy books — one he was finishing, a second to start, and a third in case the second wasn't good. Among his last words, per Singh: a teacher should never make students wait — and he died while teaching at IIM Shillong.
- Kalam never began a meal until the birds outside his dining room — parrots, pigeons, a peacock family, sparrows — were fed, each with separate food so they would not fight. When Singh once suggested skipping it because they were late, Kalam replied that doing it once would teach his mind it can be skipped, since "circumstances will never complain" — good habits must be protected, not just learned. Singh now feeds a three-legged dog outside his office on the same principle.
- Happiness, from Kalam's philosophy and Singh's book title "What Can I Give": keep asking "what can I give?" — giving even 10–15 minutes makes you happier and more productive, and helps you sleep well.

## Claims to be careful with
- The geopolitical and historical assertions (India as the only country to have tackled terrorism, the vaccine-diplomacy framing) are the guest's interpretations and national narrative, not neutral findings.
- The helium-3 figures (11 million tonnes; 40,000 years of Earth's electricity) and the claim that space treaties expire around 2032–34, enabling territorial claims on the Moon, are speculative and stated without sources.
- The workforce statistics (19.7 women per 100 men in manufacturing; 72% from southern states; ₹87 vs ₹100 pay) come from an article the host read that day; they are not verified in the episode.
- The intermittent-fasting/vrat remark is a cultural observation, not health or medical guidance; this episode contains no clinical advice.

## Summary
Srijan Pal Singh — author and former aide to Dr A.P.J. Abdul Kalam, and the show's fastest-returning guest — ranges over India's counter-terrorism record, free-food scheme and vaccine diplomacy, the strategic science of Chandrayaan-3's south-pole landing (water ice, helium-3, future mining claims), and why India's aim should be "Vishwamitra" rather than superpower. He pushes back on forcing 50-50 workforce numbers while condemning unequal pay, and closes with intimate lessons from Kalam: stand on your teacher's shoulders, keep learning to your last day, protect good habits because "circumstances will never complain," and ask "what can I give?"

योरसेल्फ दिस क्वेश्चन इट्स अ ग्रेट क्वेश्चन थैंक यू सो मच थैंक यू फॉर हैविंग अ डीप एंड लॉन्ग कन्वर्सेशन विथ अस वंस अगेन आई वाना टेक गेट यू बैक अगेन एंड अगेन एंडस इट्स अ प्लेजर टू हैव यू यर थैंक यू सो मच टू कम ऑन फिगरिंग आउट एज आई लव टू से यू आर नॉट अ गेस्ट ऑन फिगरिंग आउट यू आर अ टीचर ऑन फिगरिंग आउट सो यू शुड कीप कमिंग ऑन अगेन एंड अगेन एंड टीचर्स एब्सलूट हैप्पी टू डू दैट एंड माय बेस्ट विशेस टू यू योर टीम थैंक यू सो मच ये एपिसोड एंड तक देखने के लिए हमें बताओ क्या ऐसे और गेस्ट आप देखना चाहते हो जो इंडिया के बारे में बात करें हिस्ट्री के बारे में बात करें ग्रोथ स्टोरी के बारे में बात करें और फ्यूचर में हम कैसे अपने आप को वेल इक्विप्ड नेशन और वेल इक्विप्ड लीडर बना सकते हैं उसके बारे में बात करें अगर हां तो हमें बताओ कि अगली बार हम ऐसे जब गेस्ट लेके आए तो उनसे क्वेश्चंस क्या पूछे ताकि हमें पता चले कि आप किसमें इंटरेस्टेड हो और वैसे ही टाइप के कन्वर्सेशन हम आपके लिए लेके आ पाए यू नो व्हाट टू डू आपको यह एपिसोड कि किसी एक इंसान के साथ शेयर करना है जिसको इस कन्वर्सेशन से हेल्प होगी और वह
