## Important tidbits
- Deep eye contact held for more than about 8 seconds, she says, signals one of two things: physical intimacy or aggression — in her words, "intimacy or murder."
- Men and women communicate differently, and the "zeroth step" before approaching a woman is to check whether she is open to being approached — a step she says most men skip, while women are comparatively sharp at decoding body language.
- The difference between a confident, a cocky/arrogant, and an under-confident approach is visible in the body: a confident approach shows chin gestures, but the chin should not be held up — the gesture should stay respectful, with the chin and body lowered slightly.
- How to compliment a partner: if your girlfriend or wife comes in dressed up, look at her for two to three seconds, take her in step by step from top to bottom, and then say "wow."
- Couples who are genuinely together mirror each other — sitting postures match, and even their walking stride syncs when they walk together.
- Laughter is an underrated tell: if one partner cracks a joke, the other should laugh genuinely — "I believe that you are funny" laughter, not fake laughter. How much a couple laughs together shows whether they are really together.
- Physical proximity gives couples away: sitting close, a hand resting on the partner's leg, playing with their hair. Conversely, she says you can see when a couple "can't even tolerate each other" — she gives the example of a diamond-merchant husband and socialite wife whose body language showed exactly that.
- On Virat Kohli: after Anushka Sharma came into his life, his body language changed — he became softer and gentler.
- Waving works when you look at a specific person while doing it: Hillary Clinton points at and "recognizes" individuals in crowds, which builds connection even if it is partly performed. She contrasts this with Obama, whose gestures she reads as genuine — a genuine person owns all their feelings (sad, angry) instead of hiding one side, whereas Hillary's speeches often looked heavily coached to her.
- For first meetings and arranged-marriage setups, her first rule is self-awareness: know your own biases, and learn to distinguish between your bias and the other person's actual signals. She runs a course/workshop on nonverbal decoding for dating and on what to observe in arranged-marriage meetings.
- She links authenticity and attachment: if you are not genuine with yourself, your authenticity keeps fluctuating; and how attachment was (or wasn't) formed with parents shapes how a person develops attachment as an adult.
- She criticizes the blame mentality amplified by figures like Andrew Tate and characters like Kabir Singh — men who would rather blame women than work on themselves — and notes that followers only see the aggressive side such figures show, not whether they respect women.
- On Donald Trump: love him or hate him, she says you cannot take away that "you dream big, you achieve big, and whatever you say, you make it reality" — and she found him very sharp and witty in the debate with Hillary Clinton.

## Claims to be careful with
- Body-language "decoding" of this kind is interpretive, not a validated diagnostic method: single cues (eye contact duration, posture, stride-matching) cannot reliably establish cheating, attraction, or character, and she presents her readings as expert opinion.
- Her suggestion that a woman who dresses to attract a lot of "eyeballs" may have an absent, neglected, or weak father is a speculative psychological inference about strangers' clothing, not an established finding — Raj pushes back on it in the episode itself.
- Celebrity readings (Kohli, Clinton, Obama, Trump) are her subjective interpretations of public footage, offered without any clinical or first-hand basis.

## Summary
Body language expert Kanan Tandi returns to decode how nonverbal cues play out in love and leadership: the 8-second eye-contact rule, how a confident approach differs from a cocky or under-confident one, and how mirroring, synced walking, genuine laughter, and physical proximity reveal whether a couple is truly together. She also reads public figures — Kohli's post-Anushka softness, Hillary Clinton's crowd work, Obama's genuineness, Trump's follow-through — and argues that self-awareness about your own biases and attachment patterns is the real foundation of reading anyone else.
