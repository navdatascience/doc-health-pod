## Important tidbits
- Ravi Kishan described a childhood of extreme poverty and a very strict priest (Brahmin) father who beat him daily; as a boy he played Sita in Ramleela wearing his mother's saree, and his father called him "nachaniya" and wanted him to farm or take a government job.
- He ran away from home to pursue acting — his mother gave him ₹500 because she feared his father would kill him that day — and he said he experienced the beatings as that household's only form of communication, since there were no hugs.
- He described himself as permanently wounded by that childhood yet fearless, and said he built his career with no godfather, money or connections, certain his "sun would rise."
- Recognition beyond the "Bhojpuri superstar" label took him 33 years, he said (including IIFA and best-actor awards); after *Tere Naam* he took a Bhojpuri film, *Saiyaan Hamar*, against warnings, and it ran to a silver jubilee.
- His father's respect came only in the Bhojpuri era, once earnings brought flights, clothes and a Lokhandwala house; his father cried and apologized, and Ravi refused the apology, calling his father his guru and God — while caring for him through hospitals and 20–22 days on a ventilator in Banaras, and not crying at his death.
- Lessons from his father that he keeps: honesty, no cheating, fraud or lying, and never humiliate anyone. The one thing he refuses to pass on is hitting children — he talks to his four children instead and has laid out life's "black and white" for them (his daughter Ishita Shukla is preparing for the army as a sniper).
- He retold an early humiliation: on the 1992 film *Udghar Ki Zindagi* he was promised about ₹85,000–90,000 but paid only ₹7,000, and at dubbing the producer threatened to cut his role if he demanded the balance; he walked out and cried in the rain because he had promised his father the money to redeem their mortgaged 1.5-bigha field.
- His definition of manliness: respecting and protecting women, respecting the poor and weaker, appreciating others' care and controlling anger — not six-packs or beards.
- On ego and power, he said MP status must not inflate the ego, credited PM Modi and CM Yogi as his models of humility and service, and noted being named a best parliamentarian / Sansad Ratna with the most questions asked in his second term; he said only the poor and voiceless come to an MP for help, and he entered politics because nobody helped him when he was poor.
- He joined the BJP, he said, after Modi's 2014 promise of a toilet in every home touched him — he had seen village women forced to go outdoors — and he framed his politics as service, not power, pointing to projects he says he brought to Gorakhpur, including a ₹700-crore elevated flyover.
- He credited Yogi Adityanath with ending "gunda raj" and mafia rule in UP, said drugs are a worldwide problem entering via the open Nepal border and drones from Pakistan, and made a direct plea to fans: "please, don't do drugs" — any drug, he said, gives an hour or two of momentum and invites death.
- On caste, he said caste-based voting has kept people backward and must end in UP and Bihar; he hides his surname (Shukla) because the public knows and votes for "Ravi Kishan," and said he would have become Ravi Kishan whatever his caste.
- His advice to strugglers: exercise daily (run 3–5 km or do 200 dand/baithak even without a gym, barefoot if needed), wake before sunrise and look at the sun, eat soaked chana and drink its water from a copper vessel, pray/meditate, then go to auditions and interviews — and chase name (fame) before money, because "money follows name."
- He admitted fame briefly went to his head around the Bigg Boss era (18–19 years ago) when every film ran 25 weeks; his wife put him in the Bigg Boss house for three months, and later losses — his father and two elder brothers — quietened him.
- He said he has raised questions in Parliament for the 15–20 lakh struggling junior artists who die unnoticed, asking for land and pensions for them, and that PM Modi personally advised him to speak carefully and avoid controversy because he is a celebrity.

## Claims to be careful with
- This is a politics-and-life conversation, not a medical episode; the health-adjacent content is his personal regimen advice (sunrise waking, daily running/dand, soaked chana and copper-vessel water), offered as personal experience rather than medical guidance.
- His political claims — e.g. that mafia rule has entirely ended in UP, the scale of Gorakhpur's development projects, and characterizations of opponents — are his statements as a sitting MP of the ruling party and were not independently verified.
- His claim that the "gut is the first brain" and that one thinks from the gut is a popular framing he voiced, not a scientific statement.
- Biographical figures (750+ films, the ₹85,000–90,000 vs ₹7,000 payment story, award details) are as he told them in the conversation.

## Summary
Actor-politician Ravi Kishan traces his arc from a violent, impoverished childhood — beaten daily by his priest father, running away with ₹500 from his mother — through 33 years of struggle to Bhojpuri superstardom and Parliament, arguing that honesty, humility, daily exercise and service are what kept him grounded, that caste politics and drugs are holding India back, and that fame must never be allowed to inflate the ego.
