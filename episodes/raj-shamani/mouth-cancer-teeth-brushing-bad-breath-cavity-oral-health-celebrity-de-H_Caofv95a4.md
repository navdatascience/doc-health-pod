## Important tidbits
- Every oral cancer is preceded by a visible pre-cancer stage, Dr Mayekar says — unlike cancers elsewhere in the body, the mouth lets you catch it early, and removing the pre-cancer patch can prevent the cancer entirely.
- The main cause is habit: keeping tobacco with lime (chuna) in one spot damages the tissue, producing a white patch (leukoplakia) that can ulcerate and turn cancerous; smoking burns tissue the same way.
- Pan masala's areca nut (supari) causes submucous fibrosis — the cheek stiffens, and rubbing against teeth creates ulcers that can become cancer.
- Lichen planus, a white lace-like patch on the cheek or gums, is a pre-cancerous condition he links to tension/stress; fewer people progress to cancer from it, but it is classed as pre-cancer.
- Chronic irritation alone can cause oral cancer: he recounts a patient whose loose tooth was fixed with a wire by an old dentist; the wire's point kept rubbing the cheek until cancer developed. Ill-fitting dentures carry the same risk.
- His practical advice to a tobacco-chewing fruit vendor: rotate the spot where you keep the tobacco, and pull your cheek out every morning to check for a white patch; six-monthly check-ups catch pre-cancer in time.
- Gum disease bacteria enter the blood circulation from the gums and can affect the heart, lungs and kidneys; the episode links poor oral health to diabetes, heart attacks and even premature babies.
- Bad breath has four main sources, he says: gum infection (pus on pressing), the coated back of the tongue, an upset stomach/acidity, and chest problems — like perfume, you cannot smell your own, so a family member has to tell you.
- Gut problems start from the mouth: food must be chewed with saliva 24–30 times until it becomes a paste for proper digestion; most people swallow rather than chew.
- Children's cavities come more from potato chips, glucose biscuits and sandwiches — sticky starches that do not wash away with water — than from chocolate; the other big cause is "milk bottle syndrome," putting babies to sleep with a milk bottle so sugar and starch sit on the teeth all night.
- Baby teeth must be saved, he insists: a tooth meant to fall at age 12 that rots at age 5 leaves the child unable to chew properly for years, and children who cannot chew do not develop jaw muscles properly.
- Cola drinks dissolve teeth — he cites his daughter's school experiment where a tooth placed in cola dissolved; wine is paired with cheese, he explains, because wine is acidic and cheese is alkaline, balancing pH.
- On mouthwash: routine mouthwash should not contain alcohol, and mouthwash has medical versus merely cosmetic uses — using it just to mask bad breath hides the cause instead of treating it.
- Toothpaste should be squeezed vertically so it presses down into the bristles rather than sitting on top; he personally does not use Patanjali toothpaste, and says a brush whose bristles have splayed like a flower should be thrown away immediately.
- Braces/aligners are functional, not just cosmetic: back teeth should touch, front teeth should not, with a slight overlap; a bad bite (e.g., deep bite) can lock the jaw and cause headaches and neck pain. Raj's own case — a front gap ("anterior negative space") — could be fixed with a maximum of ~14 aligners, about 14 weeks, he estimates.

## Claims to be careful with
- The claim that wrong toothpaste use can cause cancer is teased in the host's introduction; in the conversation itself, cancer causation is attributed to tobacco, smoking, pan masala and chronic irritation, not to a named toothpaste.
- The statement that poor oral health causes premature babies and heart attacks reflects association-level evidence as presented by the guest; the episode does not cite specific studies.
- Lichen planus being caused by "tension" (including a patient stressed by a TV serial) is the guest's clinical anecdote, not an established aetiology.
- Oral sex and oral cancer (HPV) is posed as a question in the episode's teaser; listeners should not treat the teaser as a medical conclusion.

## Summary
Celebrity dentist Dr Sandesh Mayekar explains oral health in Hindi: oral cancer is almost always preceded by a catchable pre-cancer stage driven by tobacco, pan masala and chronic irritation; gum bacteria can reach the heart, lungs and kidneys; bad breath usually comes from gums, tongue coating, stomach or chest; children's cavities come mainly from sticky starches and night-time milk bottles rather than chocolate; and chewing food 24–30 times is where digestion — and gut health — actually begins.
