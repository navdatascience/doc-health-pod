## Important tidbits
- His field — interventional/regenerative orthopedics — treats joint and spine pain without surgery, using the body's own healing material: platelets, stem cells and growth factors, either as PRP from blood or as bone marrow aspirate taken from the iliac crest (hip).
- Labrum, cartilage, tendons and ligaments cannot heal themselves because they have very poor blood supply; surgery cuts or sutures a torn labrum but does not heal it, and removing that cushion can lead to degeneration and arthritis years later.
- The procedure as he describes it: outpatient, local anesthesia, cells extracted and processed over a few hours, then injected precisely into the tear under fluoroscopy/ultrasound guidance with fine (25-gauge) needles after a nerve block; sling for a day, normal daily activity in 3–4 days, return to sport in about 2–3 months versus 6–8 months after surgery.
- Roughly 1 in 10 patients (e.g., a grade-4 labrum fully pulled off) still genuinely needs surgery; the other 7–8 out of 10 are the majority his approach targets.
- The harvest sites are not damaged: the needle holes close on their own, and the body regenerates the blood and bone marrow taken within 24–48 hours.
- The knee is the most common joint he treats worldwide, with the spine close behind; the knee's cartilage, meniscus and ligaments all have to work together because mobility depends on it.
- Avascular necrosis (AVN) of the hip has surged in India since COVID, which he attributes to heavy steroid use during the Delta wave; it now shows up in men in their 20s–40s. It has stages 1–4, is time-sensitive, needs an MRI to catch early (X-rays miss early stages), and he says early stages can be stopped or reversed with regenerative treatment before the hip collapses.
- On sitting: exercising 30–60 minutes does not cancel out 8–10 hours of "un-exercising." If you cannot stand every 20 minutes, at least keep changing position so the load direction on the discs keeps shifting. He sits on an exercise ball at work himself, which he says forces constant core engagement and cuts intradiscal pressure by about 30%.
- Knee protection is about four muscle groups plus the core, treated as one kinetic chain (a bad knee changes your walk, then the hip hurts, then the back): quads via leg extensions — externally rotate the foot to hit the VMO on the inner thigh so the kneecap tracks correctly; hamstrings via prone curls, but mostly stretching/foam rolling since sitting makes them tight; glutes via squats done sitting-back (knees not travelling forward over the toes); core via planks and bridging.
- He explicitly advises against crunches and military presses: crunches squeeze the discs, and he says he has seen many disc bulges from them over the last 8–10 years.
- The most common spine problems are mechanical back pain, disc bulges/herniation with sciatica, and disc degeneration/desiccation (loss of disc height and stability). In young, active people he blames improper gym training and lifting technique as much as sitting.
- On bodybuilding: lifting done properly is not the main danger — the illegal steroids, testosterone and supplements used for quick gains are what he has seen cause serious harm and even loss of life.
- Safety claims for autologous (your own) cells: only procedural side effects — a little pain, bruising or redness for a day or two; he says no tumor or cancer case from autologous procedures has been reported anywhere in the world, while the tumor stories involve illegal umbilical/embryonic cell procedures abroad. He says he has been doing these injections for about 18 years.
- Costs as he states them: PRP around ₹30–40k, bone marrow procedures roughly ₹1–2 lakh in India depending on areas treated; the same procedure at his Dallas office costs about what Raj was quoted for surgery (~₹5 lakh). PRP is enough for mild inflammation/tendonitis; structural damage (tears, degeneration, arthritis) needs the stronger bone marrow aspirate.
- Chair and pillow guidance: a firm/medium-firm ergonomic chair with real lumbar support beats soft chairs you sink into; recliners and zero-gravity chairs are good for decompressing the spine at home but not for productive work; contour pillows suit most people with neck pain but take about two weeks to adapt to.
- Where the field is heading, per the guest: injectable scaffolds/hydrogels and nanotechnology to grow whole tissue (e.g., a new meniscus) instead of just repairing it — inspired partly by salamanders regrowing tails; not yet available in humans.

## Claims to be careful with
- The guest sells these procedures, and his strongest claims — regenerative injections matching or beating surgery for ACL tears, regenerating discs, reversing early AVN — reflect his own clinical experience and practice, not a settled medical consensus; regenerative orthopedics is still a maturing field.
- The link he draws between COVID-era steroid use and India's AVN surge is his inference from his case load.
- "No side effects ever reported worldwide" and the ~30% intradiscal-pressure figure are stated absolutely, without cited studies in the episode.
- He himself warns that some practitioners over-claim regenerative medicine (e.g., for cancer or spinal cord injury), which he says it cannot treat today.

## Summary
Regenerative orthopedist Dr Venkatesh Movva explains how knee, shoulder, spine and hip problems can often be treated without surgery by extracting a patient's own platelets and bone-marrow cells and injecting them precisely into damaged tissue, cutting recovery from 6–8 months to 2–3. He covers why sitting all day wrecks the spine, the four leg muscle groups plus core that protect the knee, why crunches are out, the post-COVID rise of hip AVN in young Indians, realistic costs in India versus the US, and where tissue regeneration is heading next.
