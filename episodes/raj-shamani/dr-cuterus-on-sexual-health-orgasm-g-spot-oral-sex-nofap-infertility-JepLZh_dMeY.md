## Important tidbits
- Infertility breaks down as roughly 40% male factor, 40% female factor and 20% unexplained or combined, she says — yet infertility is still socially blamed on women ("baanjh"). One reason it seems more visible now is that options exist: IVF has only been available for about the last 50 years; earlier, couples were told to remarry or adopt.
- The simplest single fix for male fertility: quit smoking ("sutta"). Sperm is produced in fresh cycles of about three months, so three months after quitting, the new sperm is no longer tainted by smoking. Rising stress and rising smoking in India are, she says, major drivers of infertility.
- Sex education is so absent that a couple came to her after trying for seven years to conceive — every test normal — because the man had been penetrating his wife's belly button, believing the baby comes out of the stomach.
- Oral sex can lead to mouth and throat (oropharyngeal) cancer — flagged in the episode's opening highlights.
- On masturbation and NoFap: masturbation has no dramatic health effect either way; it is a problem only when it becomes compulsive or addictive. Her position: if you want to do it, do it; if you don't, don't — either way there is stress and anxiety when people fight themselves over it.
- Marketing-gimmick products to avoid: vaginal washes and dedicated penis/ball washes are "bakwaas" (nonsense) — the vagina needs no washing and the external part cleans with plain water; over-washing causes infections. Genital lightening/brightening creams, scrubs and lotions not only don't work, their bleaching agents can trigger immune reactions that darken the skin further. Breast-firming creams cannot fight gravity. Penis-lengthening creams, pills and lotions cannot increase penis length.
- Penile lengthening surgery only cuts a ligament so more of the internally anchored penis hangs outside — it can leave the penis pointing in an odd direction, is "not very successful," and adds at most about an inch to an inch and a half.
- She recounts fighting a year-long court case after a genital-lightening brand sued her for defamation over a reel saying such creams are unnecessary; the case ended, and she continues to campaign against the category.
- Sexual pleasure products in India occupy a legal grey zone: selling them is not illegal in itself, but if the product or its advertising is judged "obscene" — a subjective call — prosecution can follow. She recounts a bullet vibrator forgotten in her suitcase being questioned at Allahabad airport security, where she called it a "neck massager" and was recognised by CRPF staff from her content. The host cites a roughly ₹600 crore pleasure-products market, mostly imported.
- On abortion and being a support system: men should educate themselves on the laws so a partner, sister or friend is not exploited, because judgmental doctors exist. She recounts a psychiatrist who judged a gay patient and prescribed pills to suppress his thoughts, and gynaecologists who told an investment banker that her PCOS was caused by her ambition raising her testosterone and advised her to quit banking and marry — both presented as examples of harmful, unscientific practice.
- Five surprising penis facts she lists: male babies can have erections inside the uterus — normal sexual development, seen on ultrasound; the global average erect penis size is 5.1 to 5.5 inches, not the 7 inches people assume; a penis pointing upward, sideways or downward is normal variation; phimosis (foreskin stuck) and paraphimosis (foreskin trapped after being forcefully pulled back) are common hospital presentations; and the penis can fracture despite having no bone — a blunt-force injury during sex or masturbation that needs immediate surgery.
- Taboo breeds misinformation: the more a topic is hidden, the more myths grow around it (her analogy: Area 51 conspiracy theories). She notes incest-themed categories — "bhabhi" porn and Savita Bhabhi — are among India's most searched, which she frames as a symptom of repression; open conversation normalises sexual health and reduces harm.

## Claims to be careful with
- Market and internet figures quoted in conversation — a ~₹600 crore pleasure-products market, "50% of internet content is porn," and porn-category rankings — are loosely sourced and should be treated as indicative, not verified statistics.
- Patient stories (the belly-button couple, the PCOS banker, the gay patient) are her recountings from practice and DMs; they illustrate her argument but are anecdotal.
- The legal discussion of obscenity law is simplified; she herself says she stays away from legal detail, and individual cases would depend on courts' interpretation.
- Her product verdicts are stated absolutely ("bakwaas"); while mainstream gynaecology agrees intimate washes and lightening creams are unnecessary, readers should treat this as education, not a personal medical consultation.

## Summary
Dr Tanaya Narendra — Oxford-trained sexual-health doctor known as Dr Cuterus — gives a frank tour of India's sexual-health illiteracy: infertility's 40/40/20 male-female-unexplained split and the simple sperm benefits of quitting smoking, a couple who tried for seven years via the belly button, why masturbation only matters when compulsive, a debunking of vaginal washes, genital-lightening and penis-lengthening products (and the defamation case she fought over one), the obscenity-law grey zone around pleasure products, judgmental doctors, and five penis facts — all in service of her argument that taboo, not sex, is what produces misinformation and harm.
