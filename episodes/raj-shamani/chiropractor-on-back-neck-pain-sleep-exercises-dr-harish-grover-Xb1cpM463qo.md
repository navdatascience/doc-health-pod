## Important tidbits
- Dr Grover says the one sleeping position to avoid is sleeping on your stomach (prone): the neck stays rotated roughly 55–65 degrees all night (65–75 degrees if you also use a pillow), which he links to cervical spondylitis, jaw clicking/TMJ problems, one-sided facial wrinkles and asymmetry from compromised blood supply on one side, and poor digestion.
- His recommended position is "savasana" — sleeping straight on your back, or on your left side — with a thin pillow (less than 1 inch) and a pillow under the knees/feet; a slight 10–15 degree neck tilt is fine.
- He advises against sleeping all night in strong AC (around 17°C): with body temperature at 37°C, the body diverts blood to vital organs (heart, kidney, liver, pancreas), muscles get less blood and start to contract and stiffen; around 22°C is what he suggests.
- He says most modern headaches come from neck malposition: as the neck drifts forward, fine nerves get irritated, producing hammering pain and pressure around the eyes — and people keep taking migraine tablets for it instead of fixing the neck.
- On painkillers, he warns that taking them for 2–3 years can lead to kidney disease, and recounts a ship captain who took 12 paracetamol a day over an 18-day voyage and ended up with CKD on dialysis.
- Chiropractor vs physiotherapist, per Dr Grover: a chiropractor mainly works on the spine — opening locked facet joints and restoring their mobility so the joint sends a "everything is aligned" signal to the brain; physiotherapy is a broader umbrella covering muscles, ligaments, lifestyle, posture and sports rehabilitation.
- Facet joints lock up from sitting 2–3 hours in one position; a stiff or overstretched joint triggers pain receptors that stop the neck from moving (the classic stiff neck on waking), which an adjustment reopens.
- Desk setup he recommends: a chair angled at about 100 degrees (or a backrest added to any chair), the desk close enough to touch your tummy and about 2–3 inches above navel height, and getting up every 45 minutes — sitting 2–3 hours straight is not advisable.
- He says the neck and lower back both have a natural C-curve that must be maintained; in most IT workers' X-rays both curves have gone flat, which brings nerves close to the discs and facet joints, causing more injuries and slower healing.
- His top 3 exercises to keep lower back pain sorted: walking (45 minutes of dedicated walking daily), swimming, and hanging (hold a bar for ~10 seconds at a time, repeated over about 5 minutes) to decompress the spine.
- On why teenagers now get back pain: sedentary lifestyle, junk food, extremely soft beds, sitting cross-legged for long periods, riding sports bikes or trekking without warm-up, vitamin D deficiency, tight hamstrings and a weak core.
- On slip disc, he claims 90 out of 100 cases can get better with a chiropractor/physiotherapist without surgery; only the ~10% with red flags like loss of bladder control or a leg that won't move or feel need a surgeon's advice. He explains discs have no blood supply of their own and can reabsorb once surrounding joints are mobilised.
- The cracking sound during adjustment is nitrogen bubbles bursting in the synovial fluid — the crack is a by-product of aligning the joint, not the goal; a joint can align without a crack.
- He advises against self-cracking (e.g., twisting your own neck/shoulder): repeated self-cracking creates joint instability, becomes a tic-like habit, and masks a weak, tight muscle that needs attention.
- Massage guns give only 10–15 minutes of temporary relief by quieting pain receptors; regular massage is fine about once a week (up to twice if you are physically active), but overdoing massage lowers your pain-tolerance level.
- He calls turmeric milk speeding up injury recovery a myth — turmeric is fine for a few days, but he says consuming too much of it can damage the liver.
- The most common pain he sees is a sharp, knife-like pain between the shoulder blades, especially in IT workers; he says it actually originates in the neck (facet joint arthrosis / an early slip-disc sign). Training the scapula and patella — rowing for the rhomboids and middle trapezius, hyperextensions, squats and lunges for the lower back — is, in his words, the way to prevent 90–95% of such problems.
- He lists five pains a chiropractor can fix quickly — headache, neck pain, back pain, knee pain and pain around the spine — claiming about 60% improvement in one visit and 90% within 3–5 visits, with chronic "compensation" cases taking longer.
- He describes "orthorexia" — becoming so preoccupied with food research that you eliminate almost everything (he cites Hollywood actress Teresa Palmer) and end up malnourished; his advice is to mix and match fruits, vegetables, dry fruits and fibre, and simply avoid sugar.
- On the traditional practice of navel (nabhi) setting, he frames it as stimulating the vagus nerve and the mesenteric artery to improve digestion, and links the humming of "Om" chanting to vagus nerve stimulation; he openly admits there is no scientific evidence for navel setting.
- He advises against using a phone (or reading a newspaper) in the bathroom: defecation happens in three strokes (roughly 60%, 30%, 10%), and distraction makes you skip the middle stroke, leaving 20–30% inside, causing repeat urges after breakfast and, he says, raising IBS chances.
- On sleep mistakes, he says playing music or white noise all night keeps the brain processing sound, so you may fall asleep quickly but wake up early with fragmented sleep.

## Claims to be careful with
- Dr Grover claims 92 out of 100 people feel digestion improves after navel setting and that prone sleepers get cervical spondylitis "90% more" than back sleepers — these are his personal figures, and he himself admits navel setting has no scientific evidence behind it.
- His claims that 90% of slip-disc cases recover without surgery and that pains improve 60% in one visit / 90% in 3–5 visits are practitioner claims made on the show, not cited studies.
- The ship captain paracetamol story is a single anecdote used to support the painkiller–kidney claim.
- His statements that excess turmeric damages the liver and that phone use in the bathroom raises IBS risk are presented as his clinical opinion.

## Summary
Chiropractor Dr Harish Grover explains how locked facet joints, lost neck and back curves, stomach-sleeping, cold AC rooms and long desk hours produce modern neck pain, back pain and headaches, and lays out his fixes: sleep on your back or left side with a thin pillow, keep the room near 22°C, set up a 100-degree chair with the desk at tummy height, walk 45 minutes daily, swim and hang to decompress the spine — while warning against self-cracking, painkiller overuse and bathroom phone use.
