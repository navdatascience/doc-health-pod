## Important tidbits
- Toxins (botulinum toxin) and fillers do different jobs, per Dr Singh: a toxin relaxes the muscle so expression lines (the "11" lines, forehead lines, crow's feet) form more slowly and don't become permanent static lines; a filler adds/restores volume and contour.
- Dynamic lines appear only when you express; over time they become static lines visible even at rest — toxin use delays that transition, and the right amount depends on how expressive a person is, not on age alone. She says she has seen people aged 25–30 with strong lines and people in their late 40s with none.
- A well-proportioned face follows the rule of thirds (forehead 1/3, nose 1/3, chin 1/3). She says Indians tend to be a "chin-recessive" community with smaller chins, so chin filler can balance the features and make the face look more attractive.
- Done correctly, filler can actually slim and contour the face rather than make it look fat or "chubby" — e.g., higher cheekbones reflect light and make the area below look lower/slimmer, the same principle as makeup highlighter.
- Botulinum toxin has many therapeutic uses beyond cosmetics, she says: it is used in children as young as 2 for cerebral palsy, and also for excessive sweating (hands, underarms), teeth grinding/jaw clenching and enlarged masseter muscles, and migraine.
- The under-eye area is often the first to show ageing (volume loss, sunken look) and is a common reason people are told they look tired; sunken areas look darker because they don't reflect light, and hyaluronic-acid filler lifts the area so it reflects light and looks brighter.
- Fillers are made of hyaluronic acid, a sugar naturally present in skin; different consistencies are used for different areas — a robust, bone-like filler for the jawline, a softer, more malleable one for lips and under-eyes.
- Fillers can be dissolved in about 5 minutes with an enzyme injection, so an overdone result (e.g., lips too big, chin too large, cheeks too high) can be reversed; she says you return to where you started, and stopping treatment simply resumes ageing from that point.
- Filler results are largely visible "on the table" (~70% immediately, best result at ~2 weeks); toxin results start at ~3 days and are best at ~2 weeks.
- Most fillers last about a year: roughly 6 months in high-movement areas like the lips, longer in the chin/jawline; longevity also depends on the individual's metabolic rate. Most people return in 9–12 months.
- Cost: fillers run roughly ₹20,000+ per ml; lips usually need only 1 ml (₹20,000–25,000), under-eyes about 2 ml total (₹20,000–60,000 range discussed). She warns that anyone offering filler for ₹2,000–5,000 is suspect — unlicensed practitioners and non-doctors do operate, and patients should verify credentials.
- Common complications: bruising is the most common; if filler enters an artery it can block blood supply and cause skin necrosis (cell death) — signs usually appear within 24–48 hours and early detection matters. Filler in an artery near the nose/forehead can in rare cases cause blindness (she cites roughly 20 reported cases worldwide, most from nose fillers, some reversed when treated within ~24 hours).
- Aftercare: expect swelling (lip swelling settles 20–30% in the first 2 days); avoid steam, sauna, facial massage and pressure on the treated area for ~2 weeks; after lip filler avoid hot drinks and kissing for about a week; normal work can resume the next day, cardio in 2–3 days, heavy lifting/yoga later.
- Fillers and toxins are contraindicated in pregnancy and breastfeeding.
- Her clinic's client mix has shifted: 10 years ago roughly 75–80% women / 20% men; now about 60/40. Men most often come for hair (50–60%) and increasingly for lines — e.g., permanent "11" lines making them look angry or unapproachable at work.
- On Korean beauty ideals: she says completely flawless, plump "Korean" skin is largely genetics plus awareness, and Indians should aim for healthy, glowing skin rather than trying to look Korean; she personally prefers faces with some lines and character over total perfection.
- She got her own first filler at 38, and says a healthy lifestyle (exercise, good food) slows ageing overall, while fillers/toxins are for enhancement or correction, not a substitute.

## Claims to be careful with
- The claim that filler-related blindness has occurred in only ~20 reported cases worldwide and can be reversed within ~24 hours is Dr Singh's own account; vascular occlusion is a recognised rare but serious filler risk and outcomes depend heavily on immediate expert management.
- Statements that correctly placed filler "slims" the face and that stopping treatment simply resumes ageing from the improved point are her clinical framing, not guarantees.
- Her estimate that toxin results and filler longevity follow the timelines above varies by product and patient in practice.
- The episode is part of a Cetaphil-presented skin-health series, so product-adjacent framing should be read with that sponsorship in mind.

## Summary
Dermatologist Dr Mikki Singh explains the difference between toxins (muscle relaxants that delay permanent expression lines) and hyaluronic-acid fillers (volume and contour), walking through facial proportions, common treatment areas from under-eyes to hands, realistic costs in India (₹20,000+ per ml), how long results last, and the real complications — bruising, vascular occlusion, necrosis and rare blindness — alongside reversibility with dissolving enzymes. She also discusses why young clients arrive via Instagram trends like "snatched jawlines" and Russian lips, why men now make up ~40% of her aesthetic clients, why fillers are off-limits in pregnancy, and why she thinks Indians should chase healthy skin rather than a flawless Korean look.
