## Important tidbits
- Dr Marwah says dermatologists who sell pills under their own name/brand (e.g. a doctor-branded biotin or "hair regrowth" pill) are running a scam: there is no magic pill, the same medicines are available worldwide, and if he ever gave Raj a "Marwah pill," Raj should throw it in the dustbin in front of him.
- Male-pattern hair loss is genetic (multi-genetic — it can come from any side of the family) and is not caused by vitamin deficiency, so biotin and multivitamins have no role in treating it; he points to meticulously-fed bodybuilders who still go bald. Hair-loss medicine must be taken for as long as you want hair — a clinic that calls you back after a month promising results is a red flag.
- Anti-dandruff shampoos do work (prescription grades work better), used about twice a week with regular washing otherwise. Dandruff, he says, is an oily scalp and the sensitivity of your oil glands to your normal hormones.
- He states food, digestion and gut health have no connection to hair fall, dandruff or balding; hair fall from an actual nutritional deficiency is a separate, fixable problem.
- About 70–80% of people get acne at some point in life. The real purpose of treating acne early is not the pimple itself but preventing the pits/scars it can leave for life; marks and pigmentation can be fixed with medicines, but scar pits need lasers.
- A face wash's only job is to clean the face — it neither increases nor decreases pimples, though an oil-control wash helps an oily face. Very mild acne can settle with OTC creams, but once inflammation starts, oral medication is what works; otherwise you run in circles as each new pimple leaves a mark that takes three months to fade.
- "Blood purifiers" like Safi have no logic, he says: the blood of a person with acne is the same as the blood of a person without it.
- Isotretinoin is, in his words, a "wonder drug" — no amount of creams or neem face washes matches its results. The dryness people fear is the drug's intended effect (it dries the oil glands), not a side effect; bone pain, if it occurs, means stopping and investigating. It should not be given to people with known depression, and women must wait out a washout period before planning pregnancy — textbooks say two months, he advises patients three to six months. He considers it safer than oral contraceptive pills on side-effect profile.
- Dermal fillers are the most popular aesthetic treatment he performs, in Bollywood and everywhere else.
- Glutathione IV became popular for skin lightening as a side effect: it was originally given in hospitals to protect the livers of alcoholics and liver-failure patients. Oral glutathione is destroyed by stomach acid and barely absorbed; sublingual tablets absorb somewhat; IV gives the best result but it is temporary — it takes about two months to start showing, lasts only while you keep taking it, and gives a slight glow/lightening of shade, not a dramatic fairness change.
- Other IV drip therapies (e.g. monthly multivitamin drips) are mostly a gimmick if you can eat and absorb nutrition orally; a drip once in 45 days benefits the doctor more than you.
- Oral collagen is "the biggest scam going on in aesthetics right now": swallowed collagen is broken by gastric acid into amino acids, exactly like protein powder, and those amino acids do not magically rebuild collagen in your skin. He says collagen studies compare it against empty powder, never against protein powder. Collagen creams also cannot work because the collagen molecule is too large to penetrate skin.
- Seven-to-ten-step skincare routines are a scam: nothing absorbs beyond roughly the second layer of skin. All you need is — morning: wash, one active/serum, sunscreen (moisturiser if needed); night: serum plus moisturiser. Sunscreen is the best anti-aging treatment; anti-aging creams only maintain and minimally slow the process, and no cream in the world can remove wrinkles or lift a face.
- Face yoga/skin yoga and crystal rollers (jade rollers etc.) are gimmicks that just keep you busy: facial exercise burns collagen rather than lifting the face, and rollers have no logic beyond selling you the instrument and the serum.
- On ingredients: hyaluronic acid is a good product for dehydrated skin (hydrates, gives glow, but does not lift); salicylic acid is a good exfoliant for blocked oil glands, whiteheads and blackheads, and salicylic peels are used worldwide; niacinamide works but is an overhyped "phase" — he prefers kojic acid for pigmentation and small acne marks; retinol can be started even at 20–25, but should be prescription-grade, started slowly, and may react on dry skin types.
- Toners are obsolete: they existed to balance the pH of old alkaline bar soaps, while modern face washes are already pH-neutral.
- Myth-busting: masturbation does not cause pimples; never pop a pimple (the pimple goes, the pit stays forever); hard face scrubs irritate skin and disturb the acid mantle; higher SPF is not meaningfully better — SPF 30+ reapplied every three hours is enough, the difference between SPF 30 and 50 is minuscule, and for tanning you should look at the PA value, not SPF; sunscreen should be worn daily, even indoors, because UV light is present inside too.
- Dark circles have three main causes — pigmentation, hollow/deep-set eyes (very common in Indians), and post-allergy changes — not mainly lack of sleep; someone with hollow eyes will have dark circles even after 24 hours of sleep. A cold spoon only tightens the skin temporarily. Fairness creams can only reduce pigmentation and uneven tone; they cannot change your natural skin tone, so "three shades fairer" claims refer to pigmentation reduction.
- "Natural" skincare is not safer or better: natural products escape the testing and regulation pharma products undergo — "in the name of natural, you can commit any crime in dermatology."
- CoolSculpting (fat freezing) is US FDA approved and works on small, stubborn pockets of fat in otherwise fit people: results take three months, about 20–30% of fat cells in the treated area die and never regenerate, but the remaining cells can expand again if you gain weight. It cannot beat liposuction, and nothing works in three days despite clinic advertisements.
- A clinical dermatologist's earnings range widely by city and practice size (he cites figures from a couple of lakh up to around ₹15 lakh a month for very large clinical practices), and aesthetic practice can earn roughly double — but aesthetic patients, unlike clinical patients, expect VIP treatment.

## Claims to be careful with
- His blanket statements that oral collagen is a total scam and that gut health/food has no link whatsoever to hair fall, dandruff or acne are his strongly stated positions; other practitioners and some studies argue for more nuance.
- His view that isotretinoin is safer than oral contraceptive pills is his clinical opinion, not a consensus ranking.
- Glutathione IV for skin lightening is a cosmetic use of a drug intended for liver patients; he notes it is not cleared for this everywhere and that results are temporary.

## Summary
Dermatologist Dr Gurjot Marwah gives Raj a blunt tour of skincare reality: doctors' own-branded pills, oral collagen, glutathione creams, seven-step routines, face yoga and jade rollers are scams or gimmicks, while the things that actually work are unglamorous — proper acne medication (isotretinoin is a "wonder drug") to prevent lifelong scars, one or two actives, moisturiser, and daily sunscreen. He explains why hair loss is genetic rather than a vitamin or gut problem, why glutathione IV lightening is temporary, and why "natural" products are less regulated, not safer.
