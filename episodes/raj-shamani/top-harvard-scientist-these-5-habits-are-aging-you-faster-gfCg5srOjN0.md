## Important tidbits
- Sinclair's "information theory of aging": aging is driven mainly by the epigenome — the control system that packages and reads DNA — unraveling over time ("epigenetic noise"), while the DNA itself stays largely intact; each organ and cell ages at a different rate, and identical twins can be ~10 biological years apart.
- His lab's ER100 ("epigenetic reversal") therapy uses three of the four Yamanaka genes (OSK, excluding the cancer-linked c-Myc), delivered by an AAV virus; he says it made old cells ~75–80% younger, regrew optic nerves and cured blindness in mice, improved vision in monkeys, and is now in an FDA-approved human safety trial in the eye with 18 patients.
- A Harvard study he cites found men who did five basics — don't overdrink, don't smoke, don't be overweight, don't stress, eat healthy — gained 14 extra years of healthy life.
- Fasting protocol: for adults, three main meals a day is too much; two meals (or a daily 14–18 hour fast) is his baseline, and "six small meals" and "breakfast is the most important meal" are, he says, food-company marketing. He personally does 3–5 day fasts every few months and once went ~2.5 weeks with almost no food.
- After ~3 days of fasting, he says chaperone-mediated autophagy deep-cleans old proteins and the body makes ketones that fuel the brain — he describes feeling "super awake" and more energetic after day three; hacks to start: shrink meals gradually and fill up on hot water/tea.
- "Xenohormesis": stressed, colorful plants produce polyphenols (e.g., resveratrol in grapes) that activate the body's survival machinery — eat the rainbow (matcha/green tea, dark leafy greens, bell peppers); don't overcook vegetables, because when the color is gone, the polyphenols are gone.
- He frames lifestyle as "adversity mode" vs "abundance mode": fasting, exercise, sauna and plant-based eating signal scarcity and slow aging, while constant meat/protein abundance accelerates it; his suggested compromise is alternating — protein and hard training some days, adversity other days.
- Everyday damage ages you: X-rays and CT scans break DNA, loud concerts age your ears (he wears earplugs and covers his ears at sirens), and sunburn accelerates skin aging — any major cell damage pulls epigenetic regulators away from their posts.
- He has taken ~1 gram of resveratrol every morning for ~20 years and NMN (an NAD precursor, sold in his work as MIB626) for ~13 years, saying NMN raises blood NAD levels about twofold; NAD rises naturally with exercise and fasting and powers the sirtuin repair proteins.
- Inflammation and "zombie" senescent cells accelerate aging body-wide (old fat secretes inflammatory cytokines; an aged hypothalamus ages the whole mouse); he tracks CRP as an inflammation marker and says his dropped within a month of switching to a mostly vegan diet ~5 years ago.
- Exercise guidance: if only one type, choose aerobic — out of breath for 10 minutes at least 3 times a week; add weights for strength, metabolism and fall prevention. Sauna ~20 minutes, 3 times a week is associated in studies he cites with less heart disease.
- Sleep: he says quality beats quantity — deep-sleep efficiency (his is >90% on trackers) matters more than hours, and he and his partner can function on a few hours for about a week before catching up.
- He keeps HbA1c around 5–5.2 (above 7 is diabetic), calls sugar effectively a poison mainly because of hidden daily intake and glycation that permanently damages skin collagen.
- He discloses taking a small dose of an erectile-dysfunction drug (unnamed) purely to open blood vessels and improve tissue/brain blood flow as capillary density declines with age.
- He warns young people against metformin and especially rapamycin (immune suppression, side effects) as longevity drugs, and says he stopped taurine after a newer study contradicted the earlier mouse longevity finding.

## Claims to be careful with
- ER100 age reversal is unproven in humans: the current trial is an 18-patient safety study, and claims of curing blindness, Alzheimer's and other diseases come from mouse/monkey work in Sinclair's own lab; his estimate of "80–90% likely" to work in humans is his own.
- Sinclair has major financial interests in what he promotes: he co-founded Life Biosciences (the company behind ER100), works on the NAD booster MIB626, and mentions his partner Serena's sleep product "Sweet Dreams," which he takes nightly.
- The resveratrol–SIRT1 story was publicly disputed by a major pharmaceutical company (he recounts being accused of being wrong and nearly losing his lab in ~2010); he maintains later work proved him right, but the episode presents only his side.
- Human evidence for NMN, resveratrol and extended fasting changing lifespan (as opposed to animal data and short-term markers) is not established; he concedes no human trial has yet proven the adversity protocol extends human life, and that current interventions reverse aging "by more than a year or two" at most.
- Several figures are his paraphrases without citations in the episode: the "14 extra healthy years" Harvard study, whales stabilizing their epigenome, a cheek-swab signature revealing heart age, and an AI estimate that his lab's screening work would otherwise have taken 160 years and over a billion dollars.
- His off-label use of an ED drug for blood flow and his >90% sleep-efficiency and resting heart-rate claims are personal anecdotes, not recommendations — he himself advises doing any protocol under a doctor's supervision with measurements.

## Summary
David Sinclair explains his information theory of aging — that cells lose epigenetic information rather than DNA — and describes his lab's OSK gene therapy ER100 now in human eye trials, while laying out his personal protocol of two meals a day, periodic multi-day fasts, colorful stressed plants, resveratrol and NMN, aerobic exercise, sauna and deep-sleep tracking, and warning young people off metformin and rapamycin.
