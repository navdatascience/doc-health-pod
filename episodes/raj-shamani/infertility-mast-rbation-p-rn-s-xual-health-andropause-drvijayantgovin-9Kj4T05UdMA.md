## Important tidbits
- Dr Govinda says roughly 30–40% of miscarriages and infertility cases are due to poor sperm quality/DNA, yet society reflexively blames the woman; sperm being ejaculated today began forming ~90 days earlier, so a man's lifestyle (stress, alcohol, smoking, caffeine) in the preceding 7–8+ weeks directly shapes sperm quality, and husbands must clean up their lifestyle alongside wives when planning a baby.
- Sperm counts are falling across generations, he says, with candidate causes including stress, junk food, microplastics, fertilisers, radiation and pollutants; late marriages compound the decline.
- The ideal biological window for having children is 20–30 for both partners; after 30 conception gets harder, and after 35 a woman's egg quality falls sharply — often just as the husband's sperm quality is also found to be poor.
- Egg/sperm freezing is only a stopgap, not a guarantee: like a fixed deposit, the "bank can close" — a frozen egg or sperm may not produce a baby, and an older uterus may not carry the pregnancy well.
- On falling male desire (he cites a study that ~26% of men report low sexual urges): testosterone is linked to libido but is not the only hormone involved (prolactin, E2, SHBG also matter); he blames easy porn/masturbation access heavily — men who masturbate regularly tend to lose interest in partner sex, develop unrealistic "45-minute circus" expectations, and lose confidence.
- Porn drives constant dopamine surges, so arousal thresholds keep rising — some married men in his clinic cannot get aroused by their wives without porn, role play or other external stimuli, and prefer masturbation to sex; he considers solo porn use within a marriage a form of emotional cheating, though couples watching together in a secure relationship is different.
- His advice for controlling urges: daily exercise to discharge pent-up energy, cutting sexualised media consumption, discipline routines (reading before bed rather than sexual content, which otherwise loops in dreams), cold showers or push-up "triggers," accountability groups of men quitting together, a fixed "cheat day," and — for men who genuinely cannot control urges — SSRIs prescribed by a doctor, since serotonin dampens urges.
- After menopause, women's desire falls as hormones drop and vaginal dryness can make sex painful; HRT (hormone replacement therapy), common in the West, can restore sex drive but needs monitoring because it can predispose to cervical, breast and ovarian cancers.
- Andropause is now termed ADAM (androgen deficiency in the ageing male): falling testosterone causes early ageing signs, bone and muscle loss, poor appetite, ED, prostate growth and worse heart health; TRT (testosterone replacement therapy) restores levels to normal (injections, tablets, gels) and, per a recent study he cites, may prolong life and protect memory — distinct from bodybuilders' supraphysiological supplementation.
- Five natural testosterone steps he lists: good dietary fats (testosterone is made from cholesterol), good sleep (the pituitary works during sleep), resistance training (long-distance running/marathons actually lower testosterone; HIIT raises it), vitamin D plus L-arginine and omega fatty acids, and injectables (hCG/testosterone) if a gap remains; he says levels often improve meaningfully in 3–4 months.
- L-arginine benefits he lists: better cardiac/vascular health and blood pressure, improved blood flow helping early ED, metabolic support, and help with egg production and uterine lining in women — but he warns most supplements sold via the food (FSSAI) route have no quality checks, so only drug-grade, regulator-licensed L-arginine from established pharma companies should be used; he also advises cycling supplements (e.g. 2–3 months on, a gap) rather than taking them for life.
- He calls protein powders a major scam area: he cites a food-authority sampling of ~1,100 protein products in India where over 90% did not contain the labelled protein; common frauds are mixing in soy protein (poor amino-acid profile, oestrogenic — he links it to a recalled soy-based infant milk product) or milk powder; only milk and egg are complete proteins, and 1.5 g/kg protein is for athletes — excess unabsorbed protein is simply excreted.
- Orgasm is a shared responsibility, not the man's alone; textbook intercourse duration of 2–3 minutes (over 1 minute) is "normal," though he feels 5–8 minutes is more realistic for a partner's satisfaction; mutual simultaneous climax is rare — most couples climax non-synchronously, and ~90% of couples do not climax together.
- ED has at least eight broad cause categories (psychogenic, arterial, neurogenic, hormonal, traumatic, etc.) and will not fix itself — the cause must be found and treated; situational ED (stress, poor sleep, weekends-only fatigue) is different and lifestyle change is the first step of any ED treatment: fix sleep (6–7 hours undisturbed), resistance training, dietary fats, and Kegel exercises (clench as if stopping urine, hold ~30 seconds, sets morning and evening — he claims noticeable improvement in ~15 days for erection quality, timing, prostate and semen quality).
- Premature ejaculation comes in severe (discharge on touch/kissing), moderate (penetration but under a minute) and mild forms; treatments escalate from Kegels and desensitisation to medication, and newer options like Botox near the penis (lasting ~a year) and PRP ("P-shot"); penile implants are the last-resort surgical fix for ED.
- Oral sex itself does not cause cancer, but the HPV virus transmitted through it can cause oral cancers long-term; a vaccine now exists (given under age 12), which he expects will cut oral cancer rates within a decade.
- Circumcision benefits he lists: lower STD/HIV transmission (promoted in Africa for AIDS prevention), near-elimination of penile cancer risk, and slightly better timing from reduced sensitivity; he does not promote it universally and notes the consent debate for children.
- No pill, lotion or 25-day advertised product can enlarge the penis; genuine enlargement is possible only in correctly chosen patients — adolescents with restricted growth (traction/hormonal help before maturity, so parents should check a son's genital development at 12–14) and adults via ligament-release surgery, fillers or fat grafting.
- Smoking harms sex and sperm two ways: nicotine constricts vessels (worse erections, poorer testicular blood supply) and smoke products damage sperm count — men planning children should quit at least until pregnancy; small amounts of alcohol can help sex (less anxiety, vasodilation, better timing) but heavy drinking suppresses everything.
- Size does matter, he says candidly: sex works on a "trifecta" of hardness, timing and size, and while the first two can compensate for size, a partner genuinely not feeling the penis is a real, treatable problem (e.g. girth fillers); penile fracture is an emergency caused by dynamic force (usually woman-on-top), as is testicular torsion, which he says almost 1 in 100 men may experience.

## Claims to be careful with
- The figure that ED patients face a ~30% chance of heart attack within 5 years, and the claim that guidelines recommend angiography for ED patients, are stated by Dr Govinda without a cited source in the episode.
- His claims that testosterone replacement prolongs life and prevents Alzheimer's/diabetes are presented from "a recent study" he does not name.
- The statistic that over 90% of ~1,100 sampled Indian protein powders failed label claims is attributed loosely to a food/drug authority sampling; treat the exact numbers as his account.
- His views that porn should be regulated/not easily accessible, that solo porn use in marriage is "cheating," and that children should ideally be born by age 30 are personal/professional opinions, not medical consensus.
- Botox injections near the penis and PRP "P-shots" for premature ejaculation are newer, less-established therapies he offers; evidence standards differ from mainstream treatments.
- The claim that soy protein in infant milk formula caused developmental harm leading to a ban is told anecdotally without specifics.

## Summary
Andrologist Dr Vijayant Govinda argues that male fertility and sexual health are half of every couple's story: poor sperm quality drives a large share of miscarriages and infertility, sperm reflects a man's lifestyle from ~90 days earlier, and children are biologically best planned between 20 and 30. He blames ubiquitous porn and masturbation for falling male desire and arousal problems, lays out urge-control strategies, explains andropause/ADAM and testosterone replacement, warns that the supplement and protein-powder market is riddled with fakes, and gives candid, myth-busting answers on ED, premature ejaculation, circumcision, HPV, penis size and smoking — insisting most of these problems are treatable once men stop ignoring them and see a urologist/andrologist.
