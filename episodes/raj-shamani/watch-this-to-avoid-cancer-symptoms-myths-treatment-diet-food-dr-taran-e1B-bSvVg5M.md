## Important tidbits
- His central thesis: "constant irritation causes cancer" — physical, mental or emotional. He gives the example of a patient whose cancer he attributes to years of domestic abuse and unprocessed stress, and says fear kills more people than cancer itself.
- Figures he cites: about 8 lakh (800,000) cancer cases are diagnosed in India each year, and 70–75% of them are found at advanced stages. Blood cancer is, he says, the most ignored — recurrent fever gets masked by paracetamol and antibiotics.
- Screening he recommends: PSA for every man after 40; for women, CBC, LFT, KFT plus tumour markers CA 15.3 (breast), CEA, CA 125 (ovarian) and CA 72.4 (stomach).
- He claims 70% of cancers are preventable if people quit tobacco and alcohol, avoid oily curries, and never eat while stressed or distracted — wait 30 minutes after an argument before eating, because cortisol, he says, changes how food affects the body.
- Risk foods he names: fast food, pizza and burgers, MSG/Ajinomoto-heavy food, processed meat, and food cooked in unknown restaurant oils. Never reuse frying oil; palm oil is harmful, he says. Preferred fats: avocado, sunflower, groundnut and olive oil — and ghee is "best of all," about one spoon.
- Sugar: he says glucose feeds cancer cells, claims 60–70% of his cancer patients are diabetic, advises gur/khand over refined sugar, and rejects artificial sweeteners. He also advises quitting wheat (gluten inflammation) for millets — ragi, amaranth, jowar in summer, bajra in winter — suggesting a 21-day wheat-free trial.
- His top 3 anti-cancer foods: broccoli, blueberry and tomato (lycopene is absorbed when the tomato is lightly grilled); alternatives he names are cauliflower, un-waxed desi apples, banana and guava (he claims pesticides are never used on guava).
- On vaping, hookah and sheesha: he calls vaping 100% carcinogenic, says studies within five years will show vapes cause cancer, and calls the sheesha/hookah industry dangerously unregulated — the flavoured chemicals are inhaled directly, and he rates it as dangerous as smoking.
- Emotional trauma: he calls grief the worst emotion and teaches patients an exercise he abbreviates A-F-R-A — Assign (name the pain and its cause), Feel it, Release it, and move forward carrying the good memories rather than the pain. He also claims a mother's unprocessed emotions during pregnancy can affect the baby, citing a cancer patient diagnosed at 10 days old, and links a childhood fall to a blood cancer diagnosed a year later.
- Heredity: cancer risk, he says, always has a hereditary thread — a dormant gene that a favourable environment activates — so children of cancer patients must be counselled early on smoking, drinking and lifestyle. Physical trauma, too: he cites a fracture site that developed osteogenic sarcoma (bone cancer) two years later.
- His immunity framework is the acronym MEDS Rx: Meditation (daily, for mental health), Exercise (yoga, gym, pranayama/anulom-vilom, or 10,000 steps — whatever suits you), Diet (clean, preferably vegetarian; if non-veg, white meat — chicken and fish — no red meat), Sleep (ideally 8 hours; if only 6, keep fixed timings, e.g., 10 pm to 4 am), Relationships (about four people who will stand by you at 4 am, plus time for spouse and children), and the "x factor" — your hobby/joy factor. He claims that with a strong immune system, "cancer cannot touch you."
- Staging explained: stage 0 is carcinoma in situ (localised); the TNM system (Tumour, Nodes, Metastasis) governs the rest — under ~2 cm and localised is stage 1, larger is stage 2, spread to lymph nodes is stage 3, and spread to other organs (liver, lungs, bones, brain) is stage 4. His warning-sign rule: anything persistently abnormal — alternating diarrhoea and constipation, a "lipoma," recurring symptoms — is not a "new normal"; get it tested and keep asking other doctors.
- His pitch for his centre's immunotherapy: unlike chemotherapy (which he compares to bombing a hotel to kill a few terrorists, damaging civilians), immunotherapy "arms the civilians" — it strengthens normal cells so they destroy cancer cells, and he claims his patients walk in and out with no nausea, hair loss, count drops or pain, with morphine doses tapering down. He claims success rates "as high as 60 to 80 percent" (intestinal cancers ~20%, some others ~60%), a zero treatment-dropout rate, and monthly medicine costs from about ₹40–45,000 up to ₹2.5–3 lakh for aggressive cancers — not covered by insurance, though he says some cases have been paid by the PM/CM relief funds.
- Top herbs he recommends for immunity: tulsi (Ocimum sanctum), giloy (Tinospora cordifolia) and ashwagandha (he notes ashwagandha is often avoided after a certain age because of metal content). He advocates integrating Ayurveda and traditional Indian medicine with Western oncology, as China has done, and says the same chemotherapy available at Johns Hopkins or AIIMS is available in India — the difference is nursing care and commercial pressure.
- Myth/fact round, per him: plastic use increasing cancer — fact; mobile-phone/EMR radiation causing cancer on long-term use — fact; sugar feeds cancer cells — fact; smoking leads to cancer — "200% fact"; chronic stress raises cancer risk — fact; ammonia-based hair dye used for years can raise breast-cancer risk — fact (minimal level); alcohol raises cancer risk — 100% fact; poor hygiene, including oral hygiene, raises risk — fact. Cancer spreading by kissing, touch, sharing food or sex — a myth; he tells families never to isolate a patient, because love and support improve healing. His billboard message: "Your body has the power to heal cancer — you just need to know how to unlock it."

## Claims to be careful with
- The guest runs the Cancer Healer Center and is selling its immunotherapy programme throughout the episode; his claimed 60–80% success rates, "zero dropout," and claims of patients avoiding all chemotherapy side effects are his own unverified figures, not independently audited outcomes. Mainstream immunotherapy is a real, evidence-based oncology field, but it does not match the side-effect-free, cure-oriented picture painted here, and it is not a substitute for standard treatment.
- The thesis that cancer is caused by "constant irritation" — including unprocessed grief, a mother's emotions during pregnancy causing cancer in a 10-day-old baby, or a childhood fall causing blood cancer — is his personal theory, not established oncology.
- His claim that a strong immune system following "MEDS Rx" means "cancer cannot touch you," and that cure is guaranteed with willpower plus "correct treatment," overstates what any lifestyle programme can promise.
- Tumour-marker blood tests (CA 15.3, CA 125, CEA, CA 72.4) are not recommended by mainstream guidelines as general screening tests for healthy people; his screening list goes beyond standard guidance.
- Statements rated "fact" in the myth/fact round — plastic use, mobile-phone radiation, hair dye and poor oral hygiene causing cancer — are more contested or weaker in the scientific literature than his certainty suggests.
- He claims pharma and insurance industries suppress alternative treatments; these are his opinions. He also claims 70% of cancers are preventable through the listed lifestyle steps and that 60–70% of his patients are diabetic — both are his figures, stated without a source.

## Summary
Dr Tarang Krishna of the Cancer Healer Center argues that cancer grows out of constant physical, mental and emotional "irritation," and that a strong immune system — built through his MEDS Rx formula of meditation, exercise, diet, sleep, relationships and a personal joy factor — is both the prevention and the path to healing. He explains staging and warning signs, pushes early screening, condemns tobacco, vaping, hookah, reused oils, sugar and wheat, and makes a sustained case for his centre's immunotherapy over chemotherapy — claims that are commercially interested and well outside mainstream oncology, and are flagged as such above.
