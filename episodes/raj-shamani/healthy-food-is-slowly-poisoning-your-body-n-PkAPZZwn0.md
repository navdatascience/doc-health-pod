## Important tidbits
- Dimple Jangda explains Ayurveda's core idea of prakriti — a unique biological blueprint — built from three doshas: Vata (air + space), Pitta (fire), and Kapha (earth + water), themselves combinations of the five elements (earth, water, fire, air, ether); dosha can be assessed by an Ayurvedic doctor's pulse reading or by detailed quizzes about hair, skin, nails, digestion and dreams.
- Pitta (fire-dominant) people are described as ambitious, competitive and leadership-oriented, but excess fire shows up as acidity, reflux, burning, ulcers, a sensitive liver, skin disorders (acne, rosacea, eczema, psoriasis, pigmentation) and early hair fall/greying.
- For excess Pitta she recommends cooling foods — sabja (sweet basil/tukmaria) seeds soaked in water, cucumber, zucchini, watermelon, muskmelon and sweet grapes — and avoiding sour foods (lemon, tomato, tamarind, citrus), excess salt, and pungent/spicy foods (chillies, capsicum, onion, garlic) while agni is high; she jokes that Pitta types are "addicted" to sour, salty and spicy tastes.
- Kapha (earth + water dominant) people are, in her framework, prone to "heavy" lipid-metabolic disorders — obesity, diabetes, cholesterol, high BP, thyroid issues, PCOS, fibroids and tumours — and she advises fixing the Kapha imbalance first, with weight loss following as a by-product rather than the primary target.
- Vata (air + space dominant) people are described as the thinnest type, with dry skin and hair, weak digestion, bloating and constipation, and vulnerability to bone-density loss, osteoporosis, arthritis, anxiety and overthinking.
- Ayurveda favours cooked food over raw: cooking "pre-digests" food outside the body and roughly halves the gut's work, while smoothies skip chewing and saliva production and dampen digestive fire (agni) over time.
- Foods she says should never be eaten raw: spinach (palak) and kale-type leafy greens, all grains, and all dals, lentils, beans and pulses — these should be soaked overnight and boiled; dry fruits should also be soaked before eating because of phytic acid and other plant defence chemicals.
- Her dal-cooking tip: cook dals in an open vessel rather than a pressure cooker and repeatedly skim off the foam (which she identifies as purines/lectins) — she claims this prevents bloating, uric-acid problems and gout.
- The only raw foods she endorses in Indian tradition are coriander and mint as chutney; she presents coriander and parsley as potent heavy-metal removers.
- She maps emotions to organs: anger and frustration to the liver (the seat of Pitta), grief and heartbreak to the heart, sadness and depression to the lungs, fear to the kidneys, and guilt and shame to the pancreas — and says she routinely asks diabetic patients whether they feel guilty about something.
- Her organising principle: "your intentions control your thoughts, your thoughts impact your emotions, and your emotions impact your organs" — illustrated by her father's story of refusing a planned liver transplant trajectory, deciding he was "done being sick," and returning to long daily walks and exercise.
- She says 70–80% of serotonin and dopamine — the "happiness drugs" — are produced in the gut, so digestion is directly affected by emotions; eating even good food while angry turns it "poisonous" for the body, in her framing.
- Practical tools she recommends for stored stress and rumination: journaling — "downloading" thoughts onto paper so the brain stops rehearsing them — and sitting in silent meditation (she mentions 48 minutes) to observe thoughts without identifying with them.
- On breathing, she argues breath is the only part of physiology under conscious control, and slow, long breathing (pranayama, now also called breathwork/box breathing) supports longevity — contrasting fast-panting, short-lived dogs with slow-breathing elephants and turtles.
- She teaches tongue and face mapping as daily self-checks: the tongue's tip corresponds to the heart, moving back through stomach, pancreas and intestines to the colon, with kidneys, liver/gallbladder and lungs on the sides; on the face, chin/upper-lip acne maps to gynaecological health, the nose tip to the heart, puffy under-eyes to the kidneys, temples to the liver, and the forehead to the small intestine/bladder (Pitta heat).

## Claims to be careful with
- The claim that coriander/parsley juice can remove "80 to 95%" of mercury, cadmium and other heavy metals from the body is not supported by mainstream clinical evidence.
- Her statement that 100% of people with non-alcoholic fatty liver have anger/frustration issues, and the broader framework that specific emotions are stored in specific organs and cause specific diseases (e.g. guilt/shame in the pancreas in diabetes), come from Ayurvedic/Chinese-medicine tradition and anecdote, not validated biomedical research.
- Tongue-chart and face-mapping diagnosis — including the anecdote of a practitioner locating a brain blood clot, later confirmed on MRI, from a photograph of a tongue, and her father's yearly nose-tip pimple preceding his open-heart surgery — are anecdotes, not evidence these methods can diagnose internal disease.
- The claim that meat, seafood and eggs take 48–72 hours to digest and sit undigested in the system is an overstatement of normal digestion times.
- The figures that 70–80% of both serotonin and dopamine are produced in the gut, and that cortisol "comes from the gut," simplify and partly misstate physiology (most serotonin is made in the gut; dopamine and cortisol claims are not accurate as stated).
- Dosha-based predictions (e.g. Kapha types getting diabetes/PCOS, Vata types getting osteoporosis) and ideas like generational trauma and karma as disease causes are presented within the Ayurvedic worldview and should be read as such, not as established medical fact.
- The host's introduction credits her centre with making "5,500+ patients from 64+ countries" healthy — a promotional claim from her own profile, not independently verified.

## Summary
Ayurvedic lifestyle coach Dimple Jangda argues that even "healthy" food can act like poison when it mismatches your constitution: she explains the Vata–Pitta–Kapha blueprint, why Ayurveda prefers cooked over raw food, which foods must never be eaten raw, and how she links emotions to organs — anger to the liver, grief to the heart, guilt to the pancreas — closing with tongue and face mapping as daily self-diagnostic tools. Many of her specific claims go well beyond mainstream evidence and are flagged above.
