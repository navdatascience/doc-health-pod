## Important tidbits
- Dr Niti Gaur named stress as the number one accelerator of skin ageing, ahead of products or procedures.
- Skin defends itself by day and repairs by night following a circadian rhythm, with growth hormone released in sleep — she recommended an ideal sleep window of about 10 PM to 6 AM, saying timing matters, not just getting 8 hours.
- Ageing, she explained, is collagen and elastin breakdown outpacing repair; it can be slowed but not reversed. With integrated care started in the 20s, she said a person can realistically look about 5 years younger, 10 in the ideal case.
- She advised seeing a dermatologist every 3–6 months, and said pigmentation is the first visible hint of ageing — it appears before fine lines; dullness comes from dead-cell pile-up and dehydration.
- Creams work only at surface level (texture, pigmentation, fine lines); sagging needs deeper procedures such as HIFU, radiofrequency, fillers or Profhilo, she said, and expression lines that are becoming wrinkles are the point for timely botulinum toxin.
- Her age ladder for actives: teens need only moisturizer and sunscreen; vitamin C in the 20s–30s; a retinoid from the late 20s only under a dermatologist's graded guidance; peptides, EGF and hyaluronic acid in the 30–40+ range; bakuchiol as a milder retinoid alternative in the late 20s.
- Makeup does not age the skin, she said — it can clog pores, and actually forms a slight photo-protective layer.
- Gua sha, in her assessment, is feel-good lymph drainage with no lifting effect, and should never be used on dry skin; parlour "gold/diamond" facials and wrong-direction massage can weaken ligaments, while medifacials should be done by trained professionals.
- Face slapping has no scientific backing, she said; silk/satin pillowcases help acne more than wrinkles; and repetitive expressions — including chewing gum — contribute to lines over time.
- Screen and studio light (HEV) causes photo-damage, she warned: apply sunscreen every 2 hours in a two-finger amount, and wear sunglasses to reduce squint wrinkles.
- Women age suddenly at perimenopause, she said — collagen falls about 1–2% a year and then drops by roughly 30%; she also cited a 2022 Harvard study describing women ageing 6–8 years faster after a certain age.
- PCOS ages skin faster and is a lifestyle disorder, she said, recommending a daily walk, muscle strengthening, a lower-carb diet and vitamin C.
- Day creams are for defence and night products for repair, with retinoids used at night only; hot water strips the skin's oils, so bathe lukewarm/cold and moisturize within 3 minutes of bathing.
- On coffee, she said the evidence is mixed but more than 2 cups a day (about 100 ml, roughly a teaspoon each) is too much, while green-tea antioxidants help; smoking in any amount accelerates ageing, and all alcohol affects skin similarly through a sudden carb surge, so moderation is key.
- Anti-ageing supplements she named: carotenoids, astaxanthin and omega fatty acids.

## Claims to be careful with
- The "look 5 years younger, 10 ideally" figure and the perimenopause collagen numbers (~1–2% per year, then a ~30% drop) are her clinical estimates as stated, not independently verified here.
- The 2022 Harvard study she cited (women ageing 6–8 years faster after a certain age) is referenced loosely in the conversation and should be checked against the original paper before being quoted.
- Procedure recommendations (HIFU, RF, fillers, Profhilo, botulinum toxin) are individualized medical decisions; her age ladder for actives is a general framework, not a prescription.

## Summary
Dermatologist Dr Niti Gaur argues Gen Z looks older mainly because of stress, poor sleep timing and screen light rather than makeup, and lays out a decade-by-decade skincare ladder — sunscreen and moisturizer in the teens, vitamin C in the 20s, dermatologist-guided retinoids from the late 20s, and procedures only for sagging that creams cannot reach — while debunking gua sha lifting, face slapping and parlour facials.
