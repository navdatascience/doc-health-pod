## Important tidbits
- Dr Navneet Arora says 70–80% of sore throats and seasonal flu are viral (influenza, rhinovirus), so antibiotics cannot help them; a viral illness follows a natural history of about 5–7 days and settles on the seventh day whether you start an antibiotic on day one, day three, or not at all.
- Antibiotics should be taken only for proven or strongly suspected bacterial infections — e.g. bacterial pneumonia, UTI, prostatitis, bacterial sexually transmitted infections — not for viral pneumonia or a common cold; he calls antibiotics for cold/cough a "no-no" unless bacterial infection is seen or clinically suspected.
- Diagnosis is now much better: he cites Infectious Diseases Society of America data that fever's cause was found in only 53–70% of patients earlier, versus roughly 80–90% now with RT-PCR molecular tests and next-generation sequencing; cultures take about 72 hours and PCR about a day.
- Fever is a symptom, not a disease: microbes release toxins, the body releases cytokines, and the hypothalamus raises the temperature set point — partly because many organisms are heat-sensitive (thermolabile) — so treatment should target the underlying disease, not the fever itself.
- His three common fever mistakes: (1) self-medication, (2) ignoring fever or red flags for more than three days, and (3) treating yourself based on hearsay instead of consulting one doctor properly.
- Fever naturally spikes in the evening because of circadian rhythm — normal body temperature is about 1–1.3° higher around 4 pm (up to ~99.9) than in the morning (~98.6); a jump from 100–101 to 105 by evening is an alert sign.
- A recorded temperature above 100°F counts as fever; below 100°F does not. Paracetamol is his drug of choice to reduce fever, and he advises avoiding other painkillers in viral fevers until the cause is known.
- For children he is not a paediatric specialist but advises: while a child is eating, playing and behaving normally, there is less cause for worry; abnormal behaviour with fever means seeing a doctor promptly.
- His three daily habits to avoid infections: 45 minutes of brisk walking/exercise, a balanced diet eaten on time, and good sleep — plus switching off after work.
- Adult vaccines he recommends for India: annual influenza (quadrivalent) very strongly, HPV (Indian guidelines up to age 28, global up to 45; he calls it the only vaccine that protects against cancers — cervical, anal, oral, penile), typhoid, and hepatitis A; pneumonia vaccine after 55 (earlier with comorbidities), and herpes zoster vaccine for the immunocompromised and older adults.
- Uncontrolled diabetes is an immunocompromised state, so diabetics should take these vaccines early; those with a family history should monitor HbA1c every six months.
- He explains "ring vaccination": if you are vaccinated against flu, the vulnerable person in your house (over 65, diabetic) is protected because you won't bring serious flu home.
- Raw or unpasteurised milk — including the raw goat milk people drink during dengue hoping to raise platelets — carries Brucella risk; brucellosis causes prolonged hard-to-diagnose fever, destroys bones, puts pus on heart valves causing permanent damage, and can reach the brain; it is heavily under-reported in India. Boiling milk at 100°C for about 4 minutes pasteurises it, and milk has no role in raising platelets.
- Unprotected sex can present as fever: herpes, HPV, HIV, hepatitis B and C, and chlamydia can all show up first as fever, so sexual history matters in diagnosis and these infections are 100% avoidable with barrier methods, per Arora.
- Sleeping under an AC does not cause fever, and weather change itself doesn't either — seasonal viruses do.
- The belief that getting fever twice a year is lucky or healthy is wrong: fever means the body's "army" fought and took collateral damage; it is better to avoid the infection through lifestyle and precautions than to celebrate the fever.

## Claims to be careful with
- The episode's hook that drinking 2–3 litres of water daily makes "80% of dengue" fine on its own is an oversimplification — dengue can deteriorate quickly and needs medical monitoring, especially for warning signs and plasma leakage.
- Arora says HPV types 16 and 18 account for about 85% of cases; commonly cited figures attribute roughly 70% of cervical cancers to these two types, so treat the percentage as approximate.
- His statement that HIV transmission incidence through intercourse is "33 to 1%" is vague as spoken and should not be relied on as a precise risk figure.
- The claim that memory cells from natural infection wane in 2–3 years while vaccine-generated memory lasts for decades is a broad simplification that varies by disease and vaccine.
- Statements like herpes/genital and other STIs presenting "only" as fever, and that these infections are "100% avoidable," are conversational absolutes rather than precise clinical claims.

## Summary
Infectious disease specialist Dr Navneet Arora explains that fever is a symptom, not a disease, and builds the episode around restraint: most throat infections and flu are viral and resolve in about a week without antibiotics, paracetamol is the safe default for fever, red flags beyond three days need a doctor, and prevention — 45 minutes of daily exercise, timely balanced food, sleep, and adult vaccines like influenza, HPV, typhoid and hepatitis A — matters more than the home remedies and raw-milk trends he warns against.
