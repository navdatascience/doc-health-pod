## Important tidbits
- About 3.5 billion of the world's 8 billion people suffer from some form of dental disease, he says — and the mouth is the gateway to overall health, not a separate compartment.
- A dentist examining the mouth can find signs of systemic disease — HIV/AIDS, diabetes, hypertension, kidney and respiratory problems — and give a provisional diagnosis. Saliva itself can be tested for glucose; he calls saliva the future of diagnostics.
- India is the world capital of oral cancer: about 30% of all cancers in India are oral cancers, he says. The main causes are habits (tobacco in every form — smoking, beedi, khaini kept in the cheek with lime), malnutrition, and ignoring early lesions.
- Chronic irritation can also cause oral cancer: a sharp corner of a broken tooth or a rough denture rubbing the tongue creates a spot that, ignored for six months to a year, can turn cancerous. Simply smoothing the edge prevents it.
- Any mouth ulcer that does not heal is dangerous and can be pre-cancerous; painless ulcers are the easiest to ignore. If caught early they are curable — do not leave them untreated.
- Three symptoms never to ignore: (1) bleeding gums — oral bacteria can enter the bloodstream (bacteremia) and infect the heart valves (subacute bacterial endocarditis), raising heart-attack risk, and bleeding gums are also a sign of diabetes; (2) dry mouth; (3) food repeatedly getting stuck between teeth, which signals a cavity, since healthy teeth are self-cleansing.
- Dry mouth is destructive because saliva keeps the mouth's billions of microbes in balance and washes the teeth. Without saliva flow, Streptococcus mutans bacteria break sugar into acid that demineralises the tooth — that acid, not any "worm," is what a cavity actually is. Dry mouth appears in diabetes and in cancer patients whose salivary glands dry up.
- To keep saliva healthy: drink plenty of water, and if the mouth is dry, chew gum to activate the salivary glands; gland problems or disease need medication.
- Dentists can identify snoring/sleep-apnea risk from a large tongue, small or recessed jaw and obesity. Apnea drops blood oxygen, which he links to heart attack, brain stroke, diabetes and hypertension. Treatments include appliances that stop the tongue falling back and orthodontic devices that bring the jaw forward.
- Do not use mouth tape for mouth-breathing without a doctor's advice — "a half-doctor is a danger to life," he says.
- Tooth powders (dant manjan), charcoal pastes, and baking soda/salt are coarse and abrasive: they scrub off enamel, which can never be repaired, leading to sensitivity, fillings and root canals. On oil pulling, he says there is no proven scientific research that it benefits teeth.
- Alcohol erodes the mouth's mucosa lining, which can ulcerate and become oral cancer; even alcohol-based mouthwash should be used only on a dentist's prescription, not by default.
- Stress shows up in the mouth as night-time teeth grinding (bruxism), which wears teeth down, strains the TMJ joint and causes headaches that are often misdiagnosed as migraine. If grinding is the cause, a night guard fixes it; true migraine needs its own treatment.
- Ignored tooth problems only escalate: decay is slow and painless early but teeth cannot heal themselves, so late treatment costs more money and pain. Cracked-tooth syndrome (pain only when biting, like a split bamboo) ends in extraction if ignored, and an infected tooth can flare up painfully on a flight because of pressure changes.
- Baby teeth must be treated, not neglected: they have to last about 10 years, and losing a decayed milk tooth too early makes the permanent teeth erupt crooked. Orthodontic treatment is normally advised only after all baby teeth are gone, except for habit-breaking appliances (thumb-sucking, tongue-thrusting, lip-sucking).
- Braces versus aligners: the result is the same — metal, ceramic, lingual or clear aligners — provided a professional plans and supervises the treatment. He is against at-home aligner services where a technician scans you without a dentist's examination and an orthodontist's planning, comparing it to taking over-the-counter medicine.
- Don't eat from a shared plate or shared glass, and never share a toothbrush: bacteria pass from person to person. He notes a survey claim that one in four British couples share toothbrushes, which he calls dangerous.
- On infection safety in clinics: examination instruments, drills and handpieces are used once per patient and then washed and autoclaved (sterilised at temperature for about half an hour) in individual packs; needles and anaesthetic cartridges are single-use and discarded.
- Sensitive toothpaste is only for people who actually have sensitivity, and it gives temporary relief, not a cure — the underlying damage must be repaired. Asian teeth are naturally slightly off-white; whitening is only needed for specific professions and is safe when done professionally.
- Prevention basics: brush morning and before bed, and get a dental check-up at least once a year (ideally every six months) — many patients of 45 have never seen a dentist. His "4 A's" for saving India's teeth: Awareness, Availability, Affordability and Accessibility of dental care.

## Claims to be careful with
- The host cites a finding that 96% of Alzheimer's patients carry a gum-disease bacterium (Porphyromonas); Dr Kohli does not endorse a causal link — he only notes Alzheimer's patients develop dry mouth and poor oral hygiene because they cannot clean properly or report symptoms.
- Statistics quoted conversationally — 3.5 billion people with dental disease, 30% of Indian cancers being oral, one in four British couples sharing toothbrushes, one in three home-visit blood-test technicians being properly trained — are as stated in the episode, without sources shown.
- Saliva-based testing for diabetes and other diseases is presented as an emerging/future diagnostic direction rather than current standard practice.
- His view that at-home aligner companies amount to quackery without an examining dentist is his professional position; he concedes the model could work if an orthodontist plans and supervises it.

## Summary
Senior dentist Dr Anil Kohli argues the mouth is the gateway to whole-body health: a dental examination can reveal diabetes, HIV, hypertension and more, while neglected gums, dry mouth and non-healing ulcers can lead to heart infection and oral cancer — of which India is the world capital, driven by tobacco habits and ignored early lesions. He debunks abrasive tooth powders, charcoal pastes and unsupervised mouth taping and aligner startups, and closes with practical rules: brush twice, check up yearly, treat baby teeth, and fix small problems before they become root canals.
