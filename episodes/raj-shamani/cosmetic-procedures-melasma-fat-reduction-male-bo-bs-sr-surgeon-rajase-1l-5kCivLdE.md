## Important tidbits
- Dermatology treats skin diseases; cosmetology is the beauty-focused subset — correcting pigmentation and complexion. Dr Rajasekhar says demand for flawless skin is largely driven by Western media and corporate beauty standards, and that 70–80% of his clients come for a specific occasion (wedding, film, big event) while 20–30% come without one.
- Product quality decides safety: a poor-quality filler can cause bad results, acne outbreaks or infection, and may melt in ~4 months instead of lasting 3–4 years. His clinic's practice is to open the sealed product box in front of the patient.
- To pick a genuine doctor, he advises checking actual degrees/certifications (many non-doctors, homeopaths and salon operators perform laser, peels and treatments in India), reading reviews critically since before/after photos and ratings can be faked, and noting where celebrities actually go.
- Male "boobs" (gynecomastia) are increasingly common, he says, including in boys as young as 15, and cause severe insecurity — boys avoid swimming, sports and socialising. He attributes the rise to hormonal changes from food, saying chicken and many vegetables are "injected." There is no exercise for it; the only fix is liposuction plus gland removal.
- Gynecomastia surgery as he describes it: day-care procedure under short general anaesthesia; fat is first loosened with a fluid mix (saline, lignocaine, adrenaline, fat-breaking liquid) and suctioned out through a 4–5 mm keyhole placed in the chest fold so no scar shows; excess gland is removed through a small cut at the nipple edge; a compression garment is worn afterwards (about 2 months), waterproof plaster from day 1, heavy exercise avoided for a month, bruising settles in about 7–10 days.
- Liposuction is body-shaping, not weight-loss surgery, and does not work well for the morbidly obese. Typical areas: arms, axillary fat, hips, belly, inner thighs, love handles. A 48-inch waist can come down to ~42 inches (5–6 inches is a good-case result). Pain lasts about a week to 10 days, managed with painkillers and pain-killing patches.
- Once fat and the adipose tissue structure are removed from an area, fat does not refill that spot for years, he says — but if you don't maintain diet and activity, fat deposits in other untreated areas. Patients are told not to buy new clothes for 2–2.5 months because sizes keep changing.
- Melasma (brown-black patches on cheeks/forehead, common in pregnancy, after miscarriage, and around menopause at 41–42) forms in the inner skin layers, so peeling or lasering it off damages skin and it returns. His clinic's approach is internal detox plus external depigmentation: non-steroidal depigmenting creams, antioxidants (food sources like beetroot, citrus, vitamin C, berries; glutathione as the "mother of antioxidants" via sprays, dispersible tablets or injections) and good sunscreen. He says ~70 of 100 patients can be made happy; severe/genetic cases may only improve 40–50%, while early, small-area cases can clear 80–100% in about 2.5 months.
- HIFU (high-intensity focused ultrasound) tightens and lifts loose skin non-surgically by triggering collagen and elastin regeneration — used on face, neck, breast, abdomen and even vaginal tightening (performed only by lady doctors/nurses, ~30–40 minutes, with about a month's abstinence during the treatment course). Thread lifts anchor skin with dissolvable threads (lasting ~1–1.5 years, threads melt in ~6 months); the two are often combined.
- HydraFacial is a machine cleansing treatment using enzyme/saline bottles that sucks out blackheads, whiteheads and dust while boosting enzymes for softer, glowing skin; it can be done monthly or every few months.
- Nose jobs (rhinoplasty): possible from age 15 to 60–65 if fit for anaesthesia; tape/splint stays 11–15 days, no swimming for 1–1.5 months, travel is fine from the next day. Fillers can fix a saddle nose or minor bridge unevenness for 3–4 years but cannot narrow a broad tip — that needs tip-plasty surgery. Rhinoplasty costs range from ~₹60,000–70,000 to ₹2.5–3 lakh depending on corrections and implant origin.
- Cosmetic surgery for appearance is not covered by insurance; reconstructive plastic surgery (burns, trauma, cleft lip/palate) is. A deviated nasal septum (DNS) is a crooked wall between the nostrils that can block breathing and cause sinus problems.
- Nerve damage in cosmetic surgery is rare (he estimates ~0.1–0.2%, e.g., 3–4 in 1,000 facelift-type cases, and much of his discussion concerns trauma cases) because nerves are fine structures; an experienced surgeon's anatomy knowledge is the protection, and nerve correction is only sometimes possible.
- By age: women in their 20s mostly want nose jobs, breast work and skin whitening; 30s bring Botox, fillers, post-pregnancy liposuction, "mommy makeovers" and vaginaplasty; 50s clients are few and mostly come for wrinkles and under-eye bags. Men mostly come for skin whitening, hair transplants and gynecomastia.
- On skin whitening, he says you cannot change a person's fundamental colour — glutathione (injections, tablets, soaps, sprays) detoxifies the body and can lighten complexion by one or two shades as a "side effect," which he believes explains celebrities who look much fairer than before.
- Breast implants are permanent and feel different from natural tissue; breast filler (placed only in surrounding fat tissue, never the gland) lasts a few years, blends into tissue, and does not interfere with breastfeeding — nor do implants placed behind the gland.
- Looking at before/after photos, he lists what Kylie Jenner has likely had done: nose job, lip filler, cheek filler, brows, jawline/masseter work, chin filler; and for Cristiano Ronaldo: hairline, eyebrows (microblading/botox lift), cheeks, nose and teeth. His caution: anything done against nature in excess will be bad, and repeated fillers for decades are not advisable unless your profession demands it — but stopped fillers simply dissolve and you return to your pre-treatment look (plus normal ageing), with no extra damage or faster ageing.

## Claims to be careful with
- The claim that gynecomastia and PCOD are rising because chicken and vegetables are "injected" with hormones is Dr Rajasekhar's assertion; it is not an established medical consensus explanation.
- His melasma "detox" model (internal toxins surfacing as pigment) and antioxidant/glutathione treatment results (70% of patients happy, some cases 100% cleared) are his clinic's own approach and experience, not standard dermatological consensus; melasma is notoriously recurrent.
- The skin-lightening claims for glutathione (1–2 shades lighter) reflect his practice's marketing-adjacent framing; evidence for glutathione as a skin-whitening agent is limited and contested.
- Several product endorsements appear in the episode (e.g., sunscreens by Bioderma, Sesderma and a Hyderabad brand he uses, "Prismas Beauty") — he has a commercial interest as a clinic owner selling treatments and products.
- His nerve-damage and complication percentages are rough personal estimates, not audited figures.

## Summary
Senior cosmetic surgeon Dr Rajasekhar gives a tour of India's cosmetic-procedure boom: how to tell a qualified doctor from a salon operator, why product quality and sealed-box transparency matter, and deep dives into gynecomastia surgery (liposuction plus gland removal as the only fix for "male boobs"), liposuction as body-shaping rather than weight loss, his antioxidant-based approach to melasma, HIFU and thread lifts, HydraFacials, rhinoplasty costs and recovery, insurance exclusions, and what celebrities like Kylie Jenner and Cristiano Ronaldo have likely had done — with the caveat that anything done against nature in excess will eventually cause problems.
