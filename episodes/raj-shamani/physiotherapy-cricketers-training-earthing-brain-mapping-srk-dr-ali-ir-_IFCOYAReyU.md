## Important tidbits
- His central framework for living to 100: breathing, eating, sleeping and activity — he argues lifespan fell as technology rose because people lost earthing, deep breathing, floor-sitting and early sleep.
- Earthing/grounding: he demonstrated a body-voltage meter on Raj — body voltage read ~327–345, dropped to ~41–45 on touching an earthed mat, and jumped to ~480+ when a phone charger was switched on nearby. He claims 15–20 minutes of bare feet on soil/grass after a long flight removes "jet lag," which he attributes to raised body voltage rather than time zones.
- Practical earthing: keep phone chargers and Wi-Fi out of the bedroom (charge in another room or use a power bank at night); use a small earthed mat (about 1 m × 1 ft) on the bed or under the desk for a few hours a day — the plug only uses the earth pin and stays switched off.
- Sleep: add one hour to whatever you sleep now; the body's repair window is 11 PM–4 AM and cannot be shifted later; eat dinner at least ~3 hours before bed (he cites the Jain practice of eating before sunset); avoid water/cold drinks immediately with meals because they blunt digestion.
- Eating sitting cross-legged on the floor makes you eat less — stomach pressure stops you at roughly three-quarters full, unlike chair-and-table eating.
- Lungs: he says lungs were "made for" ~5–5.5 litres but most Indians now have a vital capacity under 2 litres (women ~1.5 L), and claims those whose vital capacity was under 1.5 litres did not survive COVID. Deep nasal breathing (belly out on inhale), pranayama, and exhaling slowly through the teeth raise capacity; morning walkers beat evening walkers, he says, because of ozone in the ~1.5 hours before sunrise.
- Nose is for breathing, mouth is not; mouth breathing worsens snoring. More than 80% of people he tested had a neck problem; his fixes are chin-tuck presses (5 reps, holding back, not looking up) and the "unwinding" position — lying prone with a bolster under the chest for ~30 minutes morning and evening. He rates swimming as the best exercise because it aligns the spine and works arms, legs and shoulders together.
- Magnesium is his go-to for aches and poor sleep: chana (served as prasad in temples/gurudwaras) and a tablespoon of pumpkin seeds daily; an Epsom-salt foot soak (half a bucket of warm water) works faster than oral supplements — he recounts a patient with restless legs who slept through the night after one soak.
- He wants vitamin B12 kept between 400–800 and notes most Indian vegetarians test under 200; even 8 football teams training 4 hours daily in the sun all tested low on vitamin D3 — sun alone doesn't guarantee D3 without magnesium to hold it. A morning orange/sweet-lime (ideally eaten with pulp, not juiced) helps the body retain B12/D3.
- Hydro colon therapy: he recommends cleansing the large intestine with 40–50 litres of water over ~40 minutes once every six months, claiming stuck fat in the colon blocks absorption of medicines (people keep escalating BP doses instead).
- Injuries: ice first — 20 minutes only (longer adds nothing), from the fridge not the freezer; heat only after 3 days. Pain-relief sprays and painkillers are counter-irritants that block the pain signal rather than fix the cause; pain and spasm are the body's protection signals. Taping supports and relaxes muscles.
- Cricketers: one complete rest day a week (meals in bed, no activity that raises heart rate — not "active rest"); warm-up before stretching; cool-down should be half the warm-up time (40 min warm-up → 20 min cool-down). Most common cricketer injuries: hamstring, and shoulder (bowlers). He names Virat Kohli the most powerful/strongest cricketer he has seen, describing Kohli arriving at 8 AM for his own routine before 10 AM team practice.
- Brain mapping: the brain works in five zones — delta (0–2 Hz, deep sleep), theta (2–5 Hz), alpha (~5/6–12 Hz), beta (up to ~30–42 Hz) and gamma. Athletes recalling a 200-run innings and top students recalling a good exam tested in alpha; poor performances tested in beta/gamma. "Alpha is the zone of living" — reached via meditation, sound/vibration, and simply being happy with friends. He claims the brain "cannot lie" — frequencies change when someone gives a wrong name, which he used to catch players faking injuries.
- On Shah Rukh Khan: SRK barely sleeps and works through the night, but compensates with heavy exercise — he argues 1–1.5 hours of daily exercise can offset an irregular lifestyle, though 7–8 hours of quality sleep is still the baseline (normal sleep apnoea is up to ~5 breathing stops/hour; he says many Indians hit 25–30).
- Sex/masturbation does not hurt athletic performance if it relaxes you — he tells of a coach who, after two losses, took the team out to relax instead of practising, after which they won the next match and the World Cup. Massage once a week or fortnight is good, but daily massage becomes an endorphin addiction.
- Water: he prefers boiled tap water over RO/mineral water, claiming tap water retains ~38 minerals while RO strips everything; soft drinks tested as pure acid on his tester.
- Sleep position: right side first choice, left second, never on the stomach; head to the east, feet to the west. Artificial flowers/plants "take energy" — keep a real snake plant or money plant instead. His closing advice: 40 days without gossip will change your life.

## Claims to be careful with
- The body-voltage/earthing demonstrations and claims (chargers raising body voltage causing BP, stress and sleep problems; earthing curing jet lag in 15–20 minutes) are his framework, demonstrated with a meter on the show but not established medical science.
- The claim that COVID deaths tracked vital capacity under 1.5 litres, and that lungs are designed for 5.5 litres while most Indians are under 2 litres, is stated without a cited study.
- Hydro colon therapy every six months to improve medicine absorption is not a mainstream medical recommendation and carries its own risks.
- His strong advocacy of CBD oil (cannabis leaf; "Vijaya" in Sanskrit, AYUSH-prescription, sold online) for cancer, rheumatism, epilepsy and sleep is his position; the evidence base and legal status vary and it should not be read as settled.
- Acupressure-style claims (a point under the big toe bringing fever down with a ball-pen, ear points mapping the whole body, a foot spot that releases the hamstring in 2 minutes) are presented anecdotally.
- Brain-frequency lie detection and "alpha zone" performance claims come from his own informal testing of players and students, not published research.

## Summary
Physiotherapist Dr Ali Irani — long associated with the Indian cricket team — argues Indians stopped living to 100 because modern life removed earthing, deep breathing, floor-sitting, early sleep and activity. He demonstrates body-voltage and earthing mats, prescribes more sleep (repair window 11 PM–4 AM), floor eating, magnesium from chana and pumpkin seeds, six-monthly colon cleansing, ice-first injury care, complete weekly rest for athletes, and brain-mapping into the "alpha zone," with side discussions on CBD oil, SRK's sleep habits, and why sex does not harm performance.
