## Important tidbits
- Dr Tarang Krishna distinguished good stress (short bursts that peak performance) from sustained bad stress, which he said is the harmful kind.
- Stress signs he listed: rapid heartbeat, sweating, restlessness such as knuckle-cracking, and shallow mouth breathing replacing normal belly breathing.
- The sequence he described when stress is ignored: dopamine and serotonin fall, cortisol rises, women get irregular periods, men get dandruff, gut trouble and joint pain, and people wake up unrefreshed.
- He estimated that 80% of Indians are sick with stress as the unrecognized root cause, and recommended rating your own health daily on a 1–10 scale across mental, physical, emotional and spiritual dimensions.
- His psychological advice included avoiding comparison ("comparison is the thief of joy"), practising gratitude, and using positive self-talk and "I am…" affirmations.
- He claimed toxic thoughts damage the whole system more than junk food damages the gut.
- Tests he recommended: an 8 AM fasting cortisol, a gut microbiome stool test, and annual CBC, LFT and KFT blood panels.
- He contrasted ancient fight-or-flight stress with modern stress from phones and social media — being "present but not present" — and noted that screens have eliminated boredom.
- Five emotional signs of stress he named: restlessness, aggression, taking everything to heart, pessimistic "what-if" thinking, and irritation; he also discussed PTSD and emotional numbness in Gen Z rooted in childhood events.
- He framed the gut as remembering trauma ("the body keeps the account") and linked stress eating to PTSD; one case he told was of a 62-year-old with a sattvic lifestyle whose cancer he traced to childhood witnessing of his father beating his mother.
- He described supervised ice baths of about 1–3 minutes as a way to surface repressed emotions, stressing that supervision is required.
- His PENAM acronym for stress sources: Parents, Environment, News, Associations, Media — along with the idea of emotional contagion from people around you.
- His reset pillars: meditation (he does 30 minutes daily), exercise, a sattvic diet (he named moringa, amla and ashwagandha), sleep, relationships and self-care.
- Immediate stress-release techniques he gave: left-nostril breathing five times before meals and belly breathing; he also said digestion starts in the mouth (salivary amylase, chew each bite 32 times) and that alcohol-based mouthwash kills the oral microbiome.
- Weekly stress mistakes he listed: phone charging beside the bed and not on airplane mode, checking WhatsApp first thing on waking, alcohol mouthwash, no water on waking (he recommends lukewarm lemon water), phone use between gym sets, and getting irritated in traffic.
- Worst foods for stress in his view: fried food and MSG-heavy Chinese food; he also advised cancer patients to keep a "positive" environment and bar pitying visitors, and said suppressed anger is worse than anxiety.

## Claims to be careful with
- **Central claim to treat with strong caution:** Dr Krishna asserted that chronic stress "100%" causes cancer via cortisol → inflammation → immune suppression → DNA changes, and that unhealed trauma "finally takes the form of cancer." Mainstream oncology does not establish stress as a direct, certain cause of cancer; these are his claims, presented here as stated.
- His claim that cancer begins when health falls below 5–6 on his self-rated 1–10 scale is his personal framework, not a validated medical measure.
- His estimate that 80% of Indians are sick because of stress is his assertion, not a cited statistic.
- The case of a 10-day-old baby with blood cancer, which he attributed to the mother's pregnancy trauma (her husband's affair and abuse) via "fetal programming/fetal memory," is an extreme anecdote, not evidence of causation.
- Similarly, his stage-4 breast cancer case linked to childhood colourism teasing ("kali"), and his 62-year-old case linked to witnessing domestic violence, are retrospective anecdotes.
- His "food has memory" framing (a slaughtered chicken's cortisol, a mother's loving cooking, prasad) is a belief-based claim without scientific support as stated.
- Ice baths to "surface repressed emotions" are his therapeutic claim; he himself noted they require supervision and they are not a substitute for professional mental-health care.

## Summary
Dr Tarang Krishna, MD of Cancer Healer Center, argues that chronic stress and unhealed emotional trauma are the root drivers of disease — in his strongest and most contested claim, of cancer itself — and prescribes a reset built on meditation, exercise, sattvic food, sleep, relationships and daily self-rating, alongside cortisol and microbiome testing; his causal claims about stress, trauma and cancer go well beyond established medical consensus and are flagged above.
