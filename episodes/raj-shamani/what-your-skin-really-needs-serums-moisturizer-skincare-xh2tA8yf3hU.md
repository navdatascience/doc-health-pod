## Important tidbits
- Less than 20% of people use a moisturizer, per a stat Raj cites; Dr Geetanjali says in her Mumbai practice only about 1–2 out of 10 people moisturize, usually only when they feel dryness — and many wrongly use body moisturizer on the face, which makes it greasy because facial skin is thinner and has more oil glands than body skin.
- Dry skin and dehydrated skin are different things: you are born with dry skin, but dehydration is caused by lifestyle — too much coffee, smoking, not sleeping for days, not drinking enough water, and travel all dehydrate the skin.
- Everyone should moisturize, even in a humid city like Mumbai; if your sunscreen already contains moisturizer (most now do), a separate one may not be needed.
- Moisturized skin burns and pigments later than dry skin — she uses the analogy of a dry leaf versus a lotus leaf left in the sun: the dry leaf burns first. People who follow a skincare regime from early on get pigmentation later in age.
- Don't judge sun damage by a single 5-minute exposure: sun damage is cumulative — multiply those minutes per month, per year, across your age. SPF 30 sunscreen protects for roughly 150 minutes against UVB.
- Know the two rays: UVA causes ageing, UVB causes burning. SPF on the bottle is for UVB; PA+++ is for UVA. A sunscreen carrying both is "broad spectrum" — and with screens everywhere, blue light is now a third exposure, which is why she recommends using sunscreen indoors too.
- In the US, the American Academy of Dermatology recommends sunscreen from six months of age; she says that level of awareness has not yet reached India, where many people still don't know which sunscreen to use, how much, or how often — and men avoid it because it feels greasy or stings the eyes.
- Pigmentation basics: excess pigment is hyperpigmentation (melasma, chloasma, post-inflammatory pigmentation); too little is hypopigmentation (vitiligo/leucoderma).
- Why Indians pigment more: on sun exposure the body's first response is to lay down a melanin layer — the tan. Skin type 1 (Western) skin has almost no melanin, so it burns instead and gets far more skin cancers (melanoma, basal cell carcinoma). Tanning is actually protection against skin cancer; it becomes a pigmentary disorder only when severe or repeated.
- Climate matters: dry skin in a dry place burns easily; humid Mumbai's stable weather is gentler. Her personal favorite gentle range is Cetaphil, because its cream/lotion formulations can be used safely across many age groups.
- Her clinic method: she takes a history, writes down the patient's concerns and her own, and treats the patient's biggest concern first. Face wash, sunscreen and moisturizer go to every patient; the night cream is concern-based — an active for acne, retinol or hyaluronic acid for ageing, and skin lighteners such as lactic acid or Rumex occidentalis for pigmentation.
- Beware DIY "natural" skincare copied from influencers — applying lemon on your face because an influencer does it is wrong skincare. Even aloe vera has many variants (Aloe barbadensis is the one used in formulated products); the plant outside your kitchen is untested, and she sees many patients with itching and side effects from such home remedies.
- A harsh cleanser on dry skin damages it more: she compares putting a strong salicylic-acid cleanser versus a soft hydrating cleanser on a dry leaf — the right product depends on your skin type (normal/dry/combination), age, profession, and sun exposure.
- Her practice runs on counseling: every patient gets about 10 minutes of sunscreen counseling (and 10 minutes for minoxidil counseling), delegated to her doctors, and she does not charge a follow-up fee when a patient doesn't get a result.

## Claims to be careful with
- The "less than 20% of people use moisturizer" figure comes from what Raj himself describes as a random Google stat, not a research study — treat it as indicative only.
- The claim that moisturized skin measurably delays pigmentation is presented through analogy and clinical experience rather than cited trial data.
- Product preferences (e.g., the Cetaphil range) are her personal clinical preference, not an independent comparison.

## Summary
Dermatologist Dr Geetanjali breaks down what skin actually needs: daily moisturizer chosen for the face (not the body), broad-spectrum sunscreen used correctly and even indoors, and a night active matched to your main concern — acne, ageing, or pigmentation. She explains why Indian skin tans and pigments instead of burning, why sun damage must be counted cumulatively over a lifetime, and why influencer-driven natural remedies like lemon and raw aloe vera land so many people in her clinic.

यू हैड दैट वॉइस। या थैंक यू सो मच ये एपिसोड एंड तक देखने के लिए। अब आपको तीन चीजें करनी है। सबसे पहले इस चैनल को सब्सक्राइब कर लो क्योंकि जितना आप सब्सक्राइब करोगे उतने ही बेहतर और वैल्यूुएबल गेस्ट हम ला पाएंगे आपके लिए। नंबर टू मुझे कमेंट्स में बताओ इस एपिसोड में आपको क्या अच्छा लगा और क्या गंदा लगा ताकि हम वो मिस्टेक्स रिपीट ना कर पाए एंड कौन से ऐसे गेस्ट हैं जो आप देखना चाहते हो वापस हमारे पडकास्ट पे ताकि हम उन्हें लेके आए और आपको ज्यादा से ज्यादा वैल्यू दे पाएं। एंड नंबर थ्री यह एपिसोड किसी एक इंसान के साथ जरूर शेयर करना क्योंकि वन कॉन्वर्सेशन कैन चेंज समवनस लाइफ। आई विल सी यू नेक्स्ट टाइम। अंटिल देन कीप फिगरिंग आउट। [संगीत]
