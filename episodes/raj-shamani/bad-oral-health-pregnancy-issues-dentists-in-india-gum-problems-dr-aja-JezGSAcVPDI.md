## Important tidbits
- Dr Kakkar says the two main dental problems are tooth pain from cavities and bleeding, inflamed gums — and both end in tooth loss if treatment keeps being postponed: the first molar is usually the first to decay, and lower front teeth loosen and fall out from gum disease.
- Gum problems begin when teeth are not cleaned properly; he says if cleaning were 100% impeccable, the chance of gum problems would come down to less than 1%.
- Brushing technique matters more than duration: brush for 3 minutes, not 15, and cover all six surfaces — upper teeth (outside, inside, biting) and lower teeth (outside, inside, biting); he says almost nobody cleans the inside surface of the lower teeth.
- His basic prescription: brush twice a day and visit a dentist at least once a year — twice a year is better — instead of only going when there is pain, which he says is the Indian habit.
- He confirms gum disease is linked to heart disease: a protein produced in the gums (C-reactive protein) rising above normal can be an indicator of a coming cardiac problem, though he stresses the research is still in a formative stage.
- He cites a US retrospective study that went back over data from around 1960 and found women with periodontal disease delivered premature babies (around 7–7.5 months) far more often — so, in his words, going to a dentist and getting scaling/cleaning done during pregnancy is mandatory, a must.
- Pregnancy hormones make gums prone to inflammation and bleeding, and he says the common mistake is to stop cleaning the teeth during pregnancy; cleaning is in fact compulsory at that time, both for the mother's teeth and to reduce the risk of premature delivery.
- Sensitivity is exposed dentin: the dentin in the middle of the tooth is normally covered by enamel on top and cementum below the gum, and it contains nerve fibrils; aggressive brushing with a hard brush wears the enamel away and exposes it, causing sharp reactions to cold, sweet or hot foods.
- Acid reflux is another cause of enamel erosion, because stomach juices repeatedly enter the mouth; however, he says he does not believe an occasional soda erodes enamel, since a drink stays in the mouth only 2–3 seconds and any action needs time to have a reaction.
- Sensitivity toothpastes work by regenerating a hydroxyapatite layer over the exposed dentin — a process he says takes about 7–12 days — and can be used long-term without harm.
- The brush does about 60% of the work even without paste; toothpaste adds the remaining ~40% and makes brushing easier. Fluoride is essential for tooth strength, and he notes "natural" toothpastes lack fluoride and that dental science has no proven alternative to it.
- He says there are really only about five toothpaste categories — regular, whitening, sensitivity, fast-healing sensitivity, and bad-breath (plus gum-care pastes such as the sodium bicarbonate one he uses himself) — everything else is a tweak of the same molecules.
- Sugar is not the whole story: a chapati is also a carbohydrate and a precursor of sugar, so the real rule is to rinse your mouth after every meal or snack.
- Mouthwash is an aid, never an alternative to brushing: as a liquid it reaches areas a brush cannot and breaks down stubborn plaque; if you can avoid alcohol-based mouthwash, he prefers non-alcohol.
- Published papers, he says, clearly show a brush is more effective than a datun (chewing stick), and finger brushing is meaningless; he also says a hard brush should never be used by anybody — only a soft brush that moulds to the tooth's contour.
- Bleeding gums: a rare one-off bleed from something poking or stuck is a minor self-repairing injury, but bleeding every day while brushing is a major problem — ignore it for about five years and the teeth start loosening; caught in time, he says, it is 100% reversible.
- Bad breath has two origins: oral causes (food stuck between teeth fermenting, plaque calcifying into tartar/calculus, and bacteria at the back of the tongue) and internal causes from the digestive system, stomach and liver; he estimates the split at roughly 50–50. Tongue cleaning once every 2–3 days with a normal brush is enough.
- He notes Asian countries have relatively poor oral health compared with Western countries like Sweden, Denmark, the USA and Canada, where a six-monthly dental appointment is a cultural compulsion; in India even the most educated people visit a dentist only when something hurts.

## Claims to be careful with
- The gum disease links to heart disease, Alzheimer's/dementia and other systemic diseases are, by Dr Kakkar's own admission, still in the research/formative stage — associations being investigated, not settled causation.
- The premature-birth connection comes from retrospective data (association), which he presents strongly; it should be read as an association found in records, not proof that gum disease causes early delivery.
- Figures like "less than 1% gum problems with perfect cleaning", "brush does 60%, paste 40%" and the "50–50" split of bad-breath causes are his clinical estimates, not cited study results.
- His view that soda does not erode enamel because of short contact time is his personal reasoning and differs from common dental advice about frequent acidic drink consumption.

## Summary
Periodontist Dr Ajay Kakkar argues that almost every dental problem — cavities, bleeding gums, sensitivity, bad breath and eventual tooth loss — traces back to one cause: teeth not being cleaned properly. He explains the six-surface, three-minute brushing method, why a dentist visit during pregnancy is mandatory given the research linking periodontal disease to premature birth and possibly heart disease, and dismantles common habits like hard brushes, datun, mouthwash-as-substitute and skipping the dentist until it hurts.
