## Important tidbits
- This is a solo episode: Raj shares 24 hacks in four parts — Influence, Relationships, Health and Wisdom — compiled from notes he writes every night about his own mistakes and observations. His advice is to implement only two hacks a month so all 24 fit into a year.
- Influence: don't try to be interesting, be genuinely interested — ask questions and listen when you meet people, and ignore those you are genuinely not interested in. He says his whole career is built on being a listener.
- Compliment people beyond physical attributes — on their character, intelligence, emotional intelligence, stability and attitude ("I love the way you think") — because such compliments are rare and make you stand out.
- Relationships: have more "meaningless" conversations. Deep bonds form through random, agenda-free, fun time together, not through purposeful talks.
- When a partner, friend or family member vents, do not give solutions — just listen. He illustrates with his own irritation at his girlfriend's friends arriving late: he wanted to vent, not to be handed fixes.
- Stop telling women "you shouldn't feel this way" or "this isn't worth taking tension over" — it invalidates what they feel; and stop telling men "I am not your priority" in a way that polices their time, work and friendships — men experience it as control and pull away.
- "Never cry alone": make it a rule in your circle that nobody cries alone — when someone is at their lowest, sit with them "in the mud" without trying to fix them.
- Triggers are good: whatever angers or irritates you is an opportunity to self-reflect on your own unresolved issues and childhood patterns, so keep triggering people slightly close.
- Health — the part he says he ignored most: chasing dreams and success, he ate, slept and worked out irregularly until health issues began. Without health, he argues, influence, relationships and money are all pointless.
- Mental fitness: set mental-fitness goals alongside physical-fitness goals. Train the brain with tiny daily decisions you actually complete (for example, deciding to brush twice a day and doing it) — each kept micro-commitment makes the brain feel it achieves what it sets, building confidence and reducing loneliness.
- A one-minute plank every morning, advised by his doctor, fixed the lower-back pain he got from sitting 1–2 hours at a desk, in podcasts or meetings; he does it immediately after getting out of bed.
- The bowl-of-salad hack: citing fitness creator Gaurav Taneja's glucose self-test, he says eating a bowl of salad before ice cream cut the sugar spike to less than half, and his own nutritionist confirmed that fibre before junk food blunts its impact and reduces appetite — useful for people who will eat junk food anyway.
- Go paraben-free: he says parabens, common in perfumes, deodorants, cosmetics, creams and face washes, can lower testosterone in men and affect fertility in women, and urges checking labels on every product. (He also promotes his own perfume line here, made, he says, with this in mind.)
- Wisdom — needs vs wants: learn from the parents' generation, who can instantly tell a need from a want. Write two columns, Needs and Wants, before upgrading phones, setups or vehicles.
- Wisdom — find your own red flags instead of other people's: his own was ending every argument with "stop it, we'll talk tomorrow" instead of listening, which his brother called out.
- Wisdom — start taking small decisions (what to wear, eat, watch) to train the brain for big decisions; he cites a survey claiming 86% of Gen Z suffers "menu anxiety" at restaurants.
- Wisdom — the under-one-minute rule: if a task takes less than a minute — paying a bill, an OTP, a phone call, booking a blood test — do it immediately. He admits procrastinating left his mother's TV disconnected, his father fined on an electricity bill, and his own blood test undone for a year. Close with two habits: ask yourself nightly "where do I need to reach, and what did I not do today?", and shift from self-help to learning how to help others.

## Claims to be careful with
- The "86% of Gen Z has menu anxiety" figure is attributed to an unnamed survey and is not sourced in the episode.
- The paraben claim — that parabens lower testosterone and harm fertility, stated as "proven" — is stronger than the current scientific consensus; parabens are endocrine-disruptor suspects, but human evidence at cosmetic exposure levels is limited.
- The salad-before-junk glucose claim rests on one creator's n=1 self-experiment plus his nutritionist's confirmation, not a controlled study.
- The one-minute plank curing back pain is his personal anecdote on his doctor's advice, not general medical guidance.
- The episode is motivational opinion from a non-medical host, and the paraben segment doubles as promotion for his own perfume brand.

## Summary
In a rare solo episode, Raj Shamani shares 24 life hacks across influence, relationships, health and wisdom, drawn from his nightly notes on his own mistakes: be interested rather than interesting, compliment character over looks, listen instead of fixing, never cry alone, treat triggers as self-reflection, train mental fitness with micro-commitments, do a daily one-minute plank for back pain, eat a salad before junk food, avoid parabens, separate needs from wants, hunt your own red flags, take small decisions, and do any sub-one-minute task immediately — implementing just two a month.
