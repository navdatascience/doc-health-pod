## Important tidbits
- His headline claim: spectacle numbers can be reversed with Ayurvedic treatment — in 30–40% of his patients the glasses come off entirely, and in many others the number drops and stabilizes (his example: a −4 coming down to −2 or −3). He says the topic gets little attention because the glasses, lens and LASIK business depends on people keeping their numbers.
- His signature eye drop, as he describes it: it can dissolve an immature (unripe) cataract and restore vision without surgery, and once dissolved, he says the cataract does not return for at least 15–20 years.
- On glaucoma — which he calls the biggest cause of blindness, and set to become a major issue in the next 20 years — he advises patients to keep taking allopathic pressure medicines because his drops do not control eye pressure; his claimed contribution is protecting the optic nerve from degeneration and achieving some regeneration, so vision stops worsening.
- He claims color blindness, widely considered untreatable, has been treated in many of his patients through eye drops alone, including aspirants preparing for railway and other services that require color-vision tests.
- Conditions where he says Ayurveda offers what modern medicine cannot: diabetic retinopathy and other retinal degeneration (where allopathy offers only repeated injections and laser that help for a month or two at a time), optic nerve degeneration, and retinitis pigmentosa. His reasoning: fix the retina and 80–90% of blindness-causing problems are addressed, because the picture is formed there.
- Foods maintain vision but cannot improve it, in his words: amla, carrots, green leafy vegetables, fish and other omega-3 sources, and vitamin-A/antioxidant-rich foods keep eyesight stable and correct deficiencies — they do not raise your number back.
- Do not splash cold water directly into your eyes: it washes out the eye's natural lubricant and increases dryness; you may feel relief for 5–10 minutes and then end up drier. Washing the face is fine.
- Contact lenses: expect dryness, and without strict hygiene, conjunctivitis, infections and redness; around 20–30% of people turn out to be allergic to lenses and should not wear them at all. Colored lenses are fine occasionally, like makeup, but if you feel irritation or redness the very first time, remove them and never wear them again — keeping them in can scratch the cornea. Overall he rates glasses as the better option, with lenses justified where glasses genuinely cannot be used (e.g., sports, wider field of view).
- LASIK long-term side effects as he lists them: a lot of dryness, glare in sunlight, and — in patients whose retina was already weak — the number returning after 5–6 years and then worsening fast, with operated eyes often not accepting glasses well. He notes not every patient is eligible for LASIK in the first place, and high myopes with retinal problems cannot have it.
- Everyday things he says can blind you: screen time, living in constant stress, a broken sleep cycle, and above all smoking — smoking directly damages the optic nerve and accelerates blindness in glaucoma and optic-nerve cases.
- For dry eyes he recommends Panchakarma therapies, says lubricants and artificial tears alone will not resolve it, and adds a simple habit: consciously maintain your blink rate, especially on screens.
- Traditional ingredients he names for eye regeneration used by vaids of earlier eras: Punarnava, Apamarga and Palash.
- On quality and regulation, his defense of modern Ayurveda: his own factory is WHO-GMP certified (sourced, lab-certified ingredients, in-house testing, 3–4 day CDSCO inspections) and his eye drops hold a CoPP certificate from CDSCO — a process that took 3–4 years. He argues every Ayurvedic manufacturer should be held to the same norms to counter the "they put anything in it" criticism.
- On diabetes he is cautious: starting Ayurvedic medicine alongside insulin/allopathic treatment is reasonable, but stopping insulin to switch outright would be very risky; combination first, and only reconsider once there is evidence of benefit.
- Money, as he estimates it: a top Ayurvedic doctor with a strong practice and research can earn ₹1–1.5 crore a month — roughly three times the ₹40–50 lakh a month he was told top allopathic doctors make — and products, not practice, are where the real scale is. His stated family mission, inherited from his father who began the research about 40 years ago, is to eradicate blindness the way polio was eradicated.

## Claims to be careful with
- The episode's central claims — reversing spectacle numbers, dissolving cataracts with drops, treating color blindness, and regenerating the optic nerve — are the guest's claims from his own practice and research. They are not the mainstream ophthalmology consensus, and no independent verification or published trial data is presented in the episode itself.
- The guest has a direct commercial interest: he manufactures and sells the eye drops discussed, and his earnings and "suppression by a modern-medicine lobby" arguments should be read in that light.
- His criticism of LASIK (numbers returning, rapid vision loss) is one-sided as presented; outcomes vary and his account focuses on patients who came to him after problems.
- The suggestion that Ayurveda may treat major diseases including cancer in the coming years is speculative — he describes research as ongoing (e.g., herb-based work at Tata Hospital) rather than established.

## Summary
Ayurvedic eye specialist Dr Mandeep Singh Basu argues that Ayurveda can do things modern ophthalmology cannot: reduce or remove spectacle numbers in 30–40% of his patients, dissolve immature cataracts with his signature eye drops, protect the optic nerve in glaucoma, and treat color blindness and retinal degeneration. He also gives practical guidance — don't splash water into your eyes, be careful with contact and colored lenses, know LASIK's long-term trade-offs, quit smoking to protect the optic nerve — and defends regulated, WHO-GMP-certified Ayurveda against the charge that it is untested, while acknowledging insulin should never be stopped abruptly in favor of herbs.
