## Important tidbits
- Her practice is now split roughly 50/50: half patients with actual skin problems (psoriasis, eczema, acne, hair fall) and half with no problem who want to look better and feel confident — driven by social media, Zoom calls and constant comparison.
- Skincare is holistic and basic: cleanser, moisturiser, sunscreen — not 20-layer routines with every serum (AHA, BHA, niacinamide, snail mucin) mixed together. Diet (avoid sugar), sleep, exercise, no smoking (smoking breaks down collagen early), and stress control matter as much; sunscreen alone, used regularly, visibly changes skin colour over ~2 years.
- Ageing cannot be stopped by lifestyle alone because the face ages in layers — skin, fat, muscle and bone all shrink; the eye socket itself enlarges as bone recedes. Lifestyle slows it; reversing hollows, sagging and lines needs an external push, if the person wants it.
- Her scientific ideal of an attractive man: flat forehead, straight bushy brows sitting on or just below the orbital rim, slight crow's feet, concave/straight temples, square face with a defined jaw angle and a chin as wide as the nose-corner lines; fuller lips matter in men too. A sharp nose is not required (Shah Rukh Khan is her example). Fillers should enhance individual features, not chase the ideal or cross into obsession.
- Botox is botulinum toxin (the brand name became generic, like Xerox): a purified protein from *Clostridium* bacteria that relaxes the injected muscle for about 4 months. Most side effects are injector-caused — wrong muscle or overdose (e.g., eyelid droop for ~4 months) — and cannot be reversed once done. Beyond wrinkles, FDA-approved uses include chronic migraine, excessive underarm sweating, overactive bladder, crossed eyes (its original use — the Carruthers discovered the wrinkle effect when eye patients reported smooth skin; first aesthetic approval 1993 for frown lines), and severe neck/trapezius pain; non-approved uses discussed include depression linked to appearance (a study cited ~74% improvement), premature ejaculation and painful sex under "cosmetic gynaecology."
- Looking tired usually comes from the eyes: the tear-trough deformity — a concave bone structure common genetically in Indians that absorbs light instead of reflecting it — worsened by bone loss, smoking, pollution, sugar/maida and stress. Glow/hydration can be fixed by lifestyle; the hollowness itself needs filler, PRP or newer polynucleotide injections (regenerative medicine), since it is bone structure.
- Under-eye filler safety: hyaluronic acid (HA) filler stays where placed and is not synthetic; worst usual outcome is 4–5 days of bruising. Reported blindness cases came from filler wrongly injected into a blood vessel — a trained injector who knows anatomy can reverse an error with hyaluronidase, which dissolves HA within 24 hours. Filler does not cause cancer. Overfilling (the Madonna/Grammy example) happens when neither patient nor doctor calls a stop.
- Filler longevity by area: chin and nose 2–3 years (immobile areas), under-eyes ~1–1.5 years, lips 8–10 months (6 months initially). HA is also a biostimulant — it stimulates fibroblasts to make new collagen, so each repeat session needs less filler and intervals stretch (a 90% hollow may improve to ~85% after a year); the doctor should eventually tell you to stop.
- Products people waste money or harm themselves with: under-eye creams (dark circles may not go), anti-wrinkle creams (cannot erase wrinkles), Bio-Oil for stretch marks (does nothing; stretch marks are very difficult unless treated within a year). Pharmacy skin-lightening creams are dangerous — many contain mercury or steroids; Betnovate is a steroid and will damage skin.
- Hair: 100–150 hairs shed daily is normal (telogen phase). Minoxidil holds hair in the growth (anagen) phase longer; stopping it just restarts the normal cycle — pattern baldness can be slowed, never reversed, and minoxidil is lifelong only for genetic pattern loss. Its common side effect is a vascular headache from increased blood flow; she says erectile dysfunction is associated with finasteride-type DHT blockers, not minoxidil.
- Sunscreen: reapply every 2–3 hours outdoors (sweat can wash it off in 30 minutes) — the reason Raj tanned at a festival despite expensive sunscreen. Sunscreen must also protect against infrared, blue and visible light, not just UVA/UVB; look for PA++++, iron oxide and vitamin C, SPF 30 normally and 50–60 outdoors. Quantity matters: about half a teaspoon / two finger-lengths for the face, then wait 10 minutes to absorb. Brands she named: Heliocare, True Derma, and the Indian brand Eclipse (~₹750); plain zinc oxide (what cricketers wear) is the cheapest durable option.
- Hydration basics from the episode's rapid-fire: sugar-free diet, no alcohol, no smoking, lots of brightly coloured fruits/vegetables and water, and ceramide-based moisturisers.

## Claims to be careful with
- She is a practising aesthetic dermatologist describing her own field favourably; statements like "filler does not cause cancer" and the safety framing reflect her clinical position — rare vascular complications (including blindness) are acknowledged in the episode itself and depend entirely on injector skill.
- The ~74% depression-improvement figure for Botox and uses like premature-ejaculation treatment are explicitly noted in the episode as not FDA-approved and based on early studies.
- Brand recommendations (Heliocare, True Derma, Eclipse) are her personal picks; she states she receives nothing from them, but they are endorsements within a sponsored-content ecosystem.
- Botox for migraine, sweating, bladder and neck pain is presented as FDA-approved in the conversation; approval status varies by country and indication.

## Summary
Celebrity dermatologist Dr Jaishree Sharath (24 years in practice) explains why half her patients now come in with no disease at all, lays out the anatomical ideal of an attractive face, and gives a frank tour of Botox (a muscle-relaxing medicine whose side effects are mostly injector errors) and hyaluronic-acid fillers (longevity by area, collagen stimulation, hyaluronidase as the safety net). She debunks under-eye creams, anti-wrinkle creams, Bio-Oil and steroid fairness creams, explains tear-trough hollows and minoxidil honestly, and closes with strict sunscreen rules — two finger-lengths, PA++++, reapplied every 2–3 hours.
