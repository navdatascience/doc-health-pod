## Important tidbits
- Decode a person's baseline first: ask no-stakes, low-pressure questions and note their normal blink rate, gesture pattern and voice pitch — any later deviation from that baseline is where the real signal is.
- She says lying rarely shows as a single "tell"; it shows as a change from baseline, because the cognitive load of lying tends to freeze a person's usual movements and gestures.
- Self-soothing ("pacifier") gestures — non-sexual self-touching such as rubbing the neck, arms or face — signal discomfort or stress in the moment, not necessarily dishonesty.
- Preening gestures (fixing hair, adjusting clothes) are a bid for approval and a sign someone wants to be noticed or liked.
- Attraction cues she lists: during group laughter, a person's first eye-gaze usually goes to the person they like most; feet pointing toward each other; entering intimate space (under roughly 1 foot); and increased, lingering eye contact.
- Contempt — a one-sided lip raise, identified in Paul Ekman's research — is, per the Gottmans' research she cites, the expression most destructive to relationships, especially when paired with disgust.
- To command a room: keep chest and shoulders open but natural, chin slightly up, gaze steady rather than shifty, and hands out of pockets; slumped, closed postures read as low power.
- She contrasts a collapsed low-power stance with Amy Cuddy's "power posing," but her own approach is "inside-out": her universal first assignment to clients is to list what they like and dislike about themselves, physically and mentally, before working on outward signals.
- Party strategy: look at people's feet to find open groups (feet angled outward = joinable), and standing near food and drinks makes you seem more approachable and gets you into more conversations.
- Reading leaders from clips: she reads Putin as projecting more power than Obama, Trump's handshake pull as a dominance move, Kim Jong Un's "dead," frozen free hand as nervousness, and Zelensky's nose flares as suppressed anger — adding that when a meeting is going badly it should be paused or deflected.
- She rated Raj's own body language 9/10, noting how he has grown into a calmer, better listener over the years of the show.

## Claims to be careful with
- Most specific readings (e.g., diagnosing a leader's nervousness or suppressed anger from a single clip, or the claim that contempt is reportedly absent in isolated Papua New Guinea tribes) are her interpretations and cited research summaries, not independently verified in the episode.
- Body-language "lie detection" from baseline deviation is presented as her professional method; the episode offers no controlled evidence for its accuracy.
