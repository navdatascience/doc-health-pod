## Important tidbits
- Of all foods, carbohydrates trigger insulin the most, protein triggers it much less, and fat triggers it the least — so controlling insulin starts with cutting carbs, not fat (Dr Arun Kumar).
- The real problem is not insulin resistance itself but hyperinsulinemia: because insulin stops working well, the pancreas compensates by secreting 30–50 units instead of ~10, and that excess insulin damages organs — in the ovaries it drives PCOD, and everywhere it converts food into stored fat.
- Black patches on the back of the neck and in the armpits (acanthosis nigricans) are the first visible sign of insulin resistance and can appear in children as young as 5; he checks fasting insulin in such children even when their sugars are normal, because diabetes diagnosed at 30 typically began ~20 years earlier.
- HbA1c is a better test than fasting or postprandial sugar; an obese patient can have normal sugars while a fasting insulin of ~50 shows the pancreas is already overworking five-fold.
- The insulin-resistance disease cluster he lists: obesity, type 2 diabetes, high triglycerides with low HDL (he distinguishes this from genetic high LDL), fatty liver, PCOS, and gout (high uric acid).
- High triglycerides come from excess glucose being converted to fat by excess insulin, not from eating fat: he reports triglycerides of 600–700 falling below 200 on a low-carb diet, and notes statins do not lower triglycerides (fibrates do, but only partially).
- He reports grade 3 fatty liver reversing to grade 1 or normal within 6–8 months of low-carb eating, and women with 10–12 years of infertility (after repeated failed IUI/IVF) conceiving within 4–5 months once insulin was lowered.
- His low-carb prescription ("LCHF" = low carb, *healthy* fat): cut refined and processed carbs, raise healthy fats and fiber (vegetables), and keep protein adequate rather than high; insulin falls and, he says, sugar levels start dropping from day one.
- Carb targets by severity: strict low carb is under ~40–50 g of carbs per day (mostly from vegetables and nuts; keto is under ~20 g), while a moderate diet is ~100–150 g/day with grains or millets in only one meal a day — roughly 200–250 g of cooked rice or 2–3 rotis in that meal.
- The grain-based meal should ideally not be dinner: a protein-and-vegetable dinner barely stimulates insulin, so it is "technically equivalent to not eating" — and he cites studies that a night-time protein meal helps build lean muscle and control hunger.
- Hunger on low carb is an insulin problem, not a willpower problem: 1 kg of body fat holds ~7,700 kcal, but high insulin locks that store away (like a fixed deposit you cannot withdraw); once carbs are cut and insulin falls after roughly a week, appetite suppresses on its own. For early hunger he advises water, soups, or unsweetened black tea/coffee, since "hunger" is often thirst.
- In his own analysis of 50 obese children, ~10% already had prediabetes (HbA1c 5.8–6.0), 20–25% had fatty liver/raised liver enzymes and high triglycerides, and he describes a 5-year-old, 40 kg girl with type 2 diabetes (HbA1c 8).
- Statistically only ~2% of childhood obesity is genetic/endocrine; 98% is exogenous (food and activity). If a child's BMI/weight is above the 90th–95th percentile on growth charts, he says check HbA1c and fasting insulin, not just thyroid — obesity itself raises TSH more often than hypothyroidism causes obesity.
- Children now eat 4 meals plus 3–4 snacks a day (including compulsory school snack breaks), constantly spiking insulin; his fixes: consolidate meals, make breakfast protein-based (e.g., a 2-egg omelette or ~40 g nuts instead of idli/roti), swap one dinner dosa/roti for eggs, chicken, paneer or sprouts, never force-feed or feed in front of screens, and make healthy food tasty and colorful (e.g., cheese on vegetables) so kids actually eat it.
- For vegetarians needing protein he recommends paneer, mushrooms, soya products and groundnuts (25 g protein per 100 g); soy's phytoestrogens, he says, are too low at normal food quantities to disrupt hormones, and most costly infant formulas/health drinks quietly use soy protein anyway.
- On oils: what is sold as palm oil is mostly palm olein — about 40% saturated fat and 45–50% monounsaturated (oleic) fat, similar to groundnut/sesame oil — while coconut oil is ~92% saturated yet also raises HDL and lowers triglycerides; the bigger problem, he argues, is omega-6-rich seed oils, and ultimately the answer is to use very little oil of any kind and get fats from whole foods like nuts, eggs and meat.

## Claims to be careful with
- Dr Arun Kumar's claim that 80% of type 2 diabetics can be weaned off medication completely within 15 days to one month on a low-carb diet, with doses halved from day one — a strong claim from his practice; medication changes of that speed require medical supervision.
- His reading of re-analyzed studies (e.g., Sydney Diet Heart Study, Framingham) that polyunsaturated seed oils lower cholesterol but increase mortality is a contested interpretation of the literature.
- The case of a 5-year-old with type 2 diabetes and the PCOS/infertility reversal stories are individual clinical anecdotes, not trial results.
- His illustration that a very obese person carries ~400,000 kcal of fat and could "technically survive 200 days on water and electrolytes" is a theoretical framing, not a recommendation.

## Summary
Dr Arun Kumar argues that excess insulin — not fat intake — is the root driver of obesity, type 2 diabetes, fatty liver, PCOS and high triglycerides, and that a low-carb, healthy-fat diet (strict at under ~50 g carbs/day for severe disease, moderate at 100–150 g with grains in one meal for milder cases) lowers insulin and reverses these conditions, while warning that insulin resistance now starts in childhood, visible as neck pigmentation, snack-driven eating patterns and early prediabetes.
