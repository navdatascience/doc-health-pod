## Important tidbits
- How to lacto-ferment at home: submerge vegetables fully in a 2% salt brine (salt = 2% of the combined weight of vegetable plus water), keep the contents anaerobic (weighted down, cloth cover rather than an airtight seal), and skim off the harmless white kahm yeast. Cucumbers ferment in 2–3 days, carrots take longer, and chillies take 2–3 weeks — fermented chillies make a far more flavorful hot sauce (Sriracha is fermented, though vinegar added later kills the live microbes).
- Lactobacillus co-evolved with humans and tolerates up to ~2% salt, which suppresses rival pathogenic bacteria — that salt tolerance is the whole basis of lacto-fermentation. Kombucha is different: it uses a SCOBY (symbiotic culture of bacteria and yeast), where yeast makes alcohol and bacteria make lactic acid. Even idli batter and bread dough contain a little alcohol, which mostly evaporates on steaming/baking.
- Most store-bought pickles are NOT fermented — factories preserve them with oil, vinegar and acid because fermentation is unpredictable. Traditional homemade water-and-salt pickles (like raw mango pickle) genuinely lacto-ferment and are probiotic.
- Cooked fermented foods are "postbiotic": bacteria die above ~50–55 °C, so idli and dosa deliver the short-chain fatty acids and other compounds bacteria created, but no live organisms. Uncooked ferments like pazhaya sadham (overnight fermented rice) and curd retain live cultures.
- Why probiotics need daily eating: stomach acid kills ~99.9% of ingested bacteria, and the resident gut bacteria actively resist newcomers, so you must keep sending "reinforcements" — building diversity takes roughly 3 months of consistent intake.
- Eating yogurt or kefir does not cancel a bad diet: you also have to feed the good bacteria with a wide diversity of plants, fiber, resistant starches and adequate protein. Krish's hierarchy mirrors Dr Pal's: fiber first, fermented food second.
- Aluminium cookware myth-bust: aluminium is the most abundant metal in the Earth's crust (8%), has been used for ~150 years, conducts heat so well it can save 30–40% of fuel, and the Alzheimer's Association states there is no connection between dietary aluminium and Alzheimer's disease; healthy kidneys excrete it. Only long, slow cooking of very sour dishes warrants mild caution. Aluminium oxide (the layer that forms on the vessel) is non-reactive and safe.
- "Orthorexia" — being paralyzed by fear of trace ingredients — is itself the problem: if you look at health advisories from governments worldwide, the universal top risks are alcohol, smoking, and an imbalanced diet heavy in ultra-processed food, with over-consumption of processed meats next. Microwaves, non-stick pans, air fryers, preservatives and pesticide residues do not appear on any actual expert list of dangers.
- Non-stick (PTFE) is inert enough to be used in medical implants and stents; the hazards people cite are from manufacturing fumes, not the finished pan, and modern pans are PFOA-free. Microwaving is actually the reheating method that loses the *least* vitamin B and C. Squeeze lime onto food after cooking, not during, to protect vitamin C.
- Air fryers are small convection ovens: less oil, but ~220 °C heat destroys vitamin C, B vitamins and polyphenols — fine as one component of a varied meal, a problem if air-fried vegetables are your only vegetables. High-temperature cooking in general (above ~110 °C Maillard range) generates acrylamide and polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons; air-fried fries can carry more acrylamide than oil-fried ones because of longer dry heating, so the real fix is eating fewer fries. Cook chicken to ~67–70 °C because of salmonella risk.
- GMO foods: every crop we eat is the product of thousands of years of human artificial selection; lab gene editing differs in speed, not kind. Krish cites multi-year studies finding no health danger from GMO crops — the legitimate objections are corporate practices like terminator seeds. Insulin itself is produced by genetically modified yeast carrying human DNA.
- A1 vs A2 milk: the difference is a single amino acid in beta-casein, and the BCM7 concern rests on weak rat-study evidence. "A2 ghee" is pure marketing — ghee is 100% fat and the protein that distinguishes A1 from A2 is removed in making it. His advice: drink whichever milk works for you; if milk causes bloating, cut down and get the benefits from fermented dairy (curd, buttermilk) or paneer, which are lower in lactose.
- Organic and "dirty dozen" fears are low priority: pesticide residues rank far below the major risks, washing vegetables mitigates them, and the health gain from simply eating *more* vegetables dwarfs the incremental gain of organic over conventional (though soil degradation has reduced vegetable micronutrients over the last century).
- Broiler chicken: today's birds are 3–4× larger purely through artificial selection, not hormones — growth hormone is a protein that stomach acid destroys, is species-specific, and is far too expensive for Indian producers anyway. The genuine concern is antibiotic use in cramped, unhygienic cage farming driving antibiotic resistance. Brown and white eggs are nutritionally identical.
- Fats: the total share of calories from fat matters more than which fat; keep saturated fat to ~10% of calories and eat a mix. Most dietary omega-3 comes from cold-water fatty fish (salmon, sardines) — Bay of Bengal fish provide very little.
- Paratha vs parotta: the Malabar parotta is not originally from Kerala — it came from Jaffna (Ceylon) with Tamil/Malayali tea-plantation workers and reached Kerala only in the 1960s; a large aloo paratha with melted butter can equal or exceed a parotta in calories. Neither is "superior"; context and overall diet decide.
- Environment beats willpower: if snacks are in the house you will eat them, so change the environment (like keeping water by the bed). Krish applies the same logic to social media with a personal "consumption vs creation" ratio — consuming more content than you create is unhealthy.

## Claims to be careful with
- Several precise figures are stated by the guest without citations: 30–40% fuel savings from aluminium, ~20–30% of supermarket chickens carrying salmonella, 99.9% of probiotics killed by stomach acid, and gut changes taking "3 months." Treat them as the guest's approximations.
- The BCM7/A2-milk discussion relies on rat studies, which the guest himself acknowledges are weak evidence.
- The blanket statement that multi-year studies show "no health danger" from GMO crops is the guest's summary of the scientific consensus position; regulatory views differ by country.
- The suggestion that gut-microbiome genetic profiling will soon let doctors prescribe personalized diets is speculative and forward-looking.

## Summary
Food-science writer Krish Ashok (Masala Lab) walks Dr Pal through the science of lacto-fermentation — 2% brine, anaerobic conditions, postbiotic vs probiotic foods — and then myth-busts a string of modern food fears: aluminium cookware, non-stick pans, microwaves, air fryers, GMOs, A1/A2 milk and ghee, organic produce, and broiler chicken. His consistent message is that the real risks are the boring ones (alcohol, smoking, ultra-processed food, too little fiber and plant diversity), that amount and overall dietary pattern matter more than any single ingredient, and that fear-driven "orthorexia" does more harm than the trace chemicals people panic about.
