# Colon Cancer vs Piles vs Constipation – Know the Difference & What to Do | Dr. Param

## Important tidbits
- Dr Param states that about 36% of India's population has a colorectal problem at any given time, and at least 50% of people experience one (constipation, fissure, a bleed) at least once in their lifetime.
- The youngest rectal cancer he has seen was in a 23-year-old; he cites the American College of Colorectal Surgeons projecting roughly a 30% increase in colon cancer by 2030 in the ~30-year age group, with India following the same "geocultural shift" as diets westernise.
- Per the ICMR figures he cites, 78% of colon cancers in India are diagnosed late — yet if caught early, colon cancer can be completely cured and the patient can have a normal life expectancy.
- Red-flag symptoms that should bring you to a doctor immediately: blood in the stool, alternating constipation and diarrhoea, mucus in the stool, loss of appetite, weight loss, or a family history of colon cancer. Even a simple bleed, loose stool, or abdominal pain should not be neglected.
- There is no blood test that diagnoses colon cancer — CEA is only a marker. For an asymptomatic 45-year-old he recommends a stool occult blood test (hidden blood in stool) and a colonoscopy if it is positive.
- Black, tarry stools point to upper GI bleeding (e.g., a bleeding stomach ulcer); colorectal cancer more typically causes frank red rectal bleeding.
- Piles (haemorrhoids) are graded: Grade 1 stays inside; Grade 2 comes out and goes back in on its own; Grade 3 comes out and must be pushed back by hand; Grade 4 stays out even after pushing. He has seen patients bleed down to haemoglobin of 3–5 g/dL.
- About 75–80% of anal complaints get self-labelled "piles," but anal pain or bleeding can equally be a fissure, an abscess, or a rectal cancer — a school teacher treated for "piles" for two years turned out to have a low rectal cancer found on a simple finger examination.
- Quack treatments are dangerous: he describes quacks dilating the sphincter with a bullock's horn (causing a sigmoid perforation he had to repair), acid/injection/powder treatments, and a ₹25,000 silver ring sold as a piles cure. He cites a figure of ~1.5 million quacks in India.
- A digital rectal examination is not very painful when done with lubricant, the left-lateral position, and privacy drapes — but a finger should never be inserted when a patient has severe pain or a suspected abscess.
- His clinic treats piles with laser as a daycare procedure: about 90–95% of patients go home the same day and resume normal activity the next day, though swelling takes 2–3 weeks to settle.
- Embarrassment delays diagnosis: a district collector secretly hired a private taxi, demanded a back-door entry, and hid his identity just to get piles surgery without his district finding out.
- Pregnancy can mask colon cancer: a young IPS officer's pain was repeatedly attributed to her pregnancy until a post-delivery CT scan revealed an advanced splenic-flexure colon cancer (she was salvaged with surgery and is disease-free, he says).
- Unlike blindness, oral, cervical and breast cancer, India has no national programme for digestive/colorectal diseases, he points out.
- Smiles Hospitals treats patients who cannot afford care free of cost, and never sends them back, per Dr Param.

## Claims to be careful with
- The figures for the "quack economy" (each quack earning ~₹20,000/day, totalling ~₹67 lakh crore — "double the budget of Karnataka") are the guest's own arithmetic and estimates, not audited data.
- The story that Napoleon lost the Battle of Waterloo because of piles is a popular anecdote, not established history.
- The 36% prevalence, 78% late-diagnosis and 1.5 million quack figures are statistics as stated by the guest in conversation; exact sources were not shown on screen.
- Laser piles treatment outcomes (90–95% next-day recovery) reflect his own clinic's experience as he reports it.

## Summary
Colorectal surgeon Dr Param of Smiles Hospitals, Bangalore, walks Dr Pal through how to tell piles, constipation, fissures and colon cancer apart — and why embarrassment, self-diagnosis and quacks make colorectal cancer India's most late-diagnosed cancer. He covers red-flag symptoms, screening at 45 (stool occult blood, then colonoscopy), the four grades of piles, why a rectal exam needn't hurt, and the hazards of quack remedies, closing with lifestyle basics: fibre, solid sleep and physical activity.
