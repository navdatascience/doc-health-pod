## Important tidbits
- COVID itself is pro-thrombogenic — it makes blood thicker and more likely to clot; most clots form in the legs and travel, so roughly 95% of lung clots are a "travel mechanism" that ends as a pulmonary embolism, which Dr Parimal calls a "lung attack" alongside heart attack and brain attack (stroke).
- Immobility is a major clot trigger: on long flights (he cites San Francisco–Dubai at 14–15 hours) get up and walk regularly, and if you have diabetes, blood pressure or cholesterol, get up roughly every hour; wear tight/compression stockings on long drives, flights, even long horse rides. A friend's father flew Chennai to San Francisco and developed a lung clot two days after landing.
- Post-COVID heart attacks in young people are explained by a stack of factors: lockdown deconditioning, Indians' genetically smaller blood vessels, cholesterol-rich diet (ghee "in moderation" — but daily ghee is not moderation), residual clotting tendency for 2–3 years after infection, and sudden intense exertion such as the Garba deaths reported in Gujarat, worsened by dehydration.
- After COVID, return to exercise gradually: long-COVID clinics start with low-intensity activity for 2–3 weeks and build back to full activity over 4–6 months; jumping straight to an hour on the elliptical at a heart rate of 180 is dangerous.
- Long COVID is real (it has an ICD code): the CDC threshold is symptoms beyond 4 weeks, WHO says beyond 3 months. Typical symptoms are fatigue, shortness of breath and chronic cough, and they can persist 2+ years; before calling breathlessness psychological, he insists on ruling out lung scarring with an X-ray, CT scan, pulmonary function ("breathing") test and a six-minute walk test — the first walk test should be done in a monitored setting, not alone at home.
- ICU stays can cause PTSD lasting a year or two; his unit now runs an "awake and walking ICU" with minimal sedation because muscles — including the paper-thin diaphragm — begin to atrophy within about eight hours of lying in bed.
- Vaping has exploded among school-age kids (13–18) in both the US and India despite bans; vape liquids contain known carcinogens such as formaldehyde and cinnamaldehyde, but the dose needed to cause cancer is unknown. Flavoured, USB-drive-shaped devices hook teenagers and act as a gateway to cigarettes — parents should ask kids about "Juuling," watch for unexplained fruity smells, and use open-ended, non-judgemental questions.
- His quitting framework is the 5 A's: Ask, Advise, Assess readiness, Assist, and Arrange follow-up — most people need three or four attempts. Nicotine patches taper 21 mg → 14 mg → 7 mg depending on cigarette count; nicotine gum uses a "chew and park" technique (chew until the peppery taste appears, then park it between gum and cheek). Always set a quit date and aim to be off nicotine replacement in 3–6 months; never cut a patch, as it dumps the nicotine at once.
- Vaping is acceptable to him only as a short-term quitting tool at low dose with a firm stop date — "vaping was introduced to quit smoking, not to start smoking." A strong motivator for young men: smoking is linked to erectile dysfunction within about 5–10 years, far sooner than cancer or COPD.
- Secondhand smoke harms family members who never smoke, and thirdhand smoke is real too — nicotine and toxins leach out of a smoker's clothes, bedding and car seats for days, exposing whoever does the laundry or cleans the room.
- PM2.5 particles are roughly 50–70 times thinner than a hair and can travel from the nose to the alveoli and even cross into the blood and brain. On the AQI scale, under 50 is good, 50–100 needs caution if you have lung disease, and over 100 is bad even for healthy people; Delhi can exceed 500–800. Meta-analyses link air pollution to increased heart attack risk, not just lung damage.
- Practical pollution defences: walk or cycle short trips, carpool, avoid peak hours, and wear an N95 (the 95 means ~95% protection) — a chunni/dupatta does nothing because its weave gaps are far bigger than 2.5 microns. He has mixed feelings on home air purifiers; keeping the house clean matters more for most households.
- Everyone should learn CPR and the Heimlich manoeuvre: modern CPR is compressions only (no mouth-to-mouth), you must know where to compress, how hard and how fast, and broken ribs are acceptable if the person survives. Without CPR, 3–4 minutes without oxygen can leave a revived person in a vegetative state.
- Dr Parimal himself developed post-COVID reactive airway disease (a precursor of asthma): cold air and perfumes (Bath & Body Works, Yankee Candle stores, his wife's perfume in a closed car) trigger coughing spells, managed with occasional inhalers.

## Claims to be careful with
- The episode repeats reports linking the AstraZeneca/Covishield vaccine to thrombocytopenia/thrombosis (TTS); Dr Parimal's own position is that vaccine-caused clots are very rare and hard to prove, since most vaccinated people were also exposed to COVID — presented as unresolved, not established.
- The claim that muscles (including the diaphragm) begin to atrophy "within eight hours" of bed rest is stated as research-based but is a strong simplification.
- The particle-size discussion contains garbled numbers (hair thickness is misstated at one point as 7,200 microns); the general point about PM2.5 being far smaller than a hair stands, but the exact figures are unreliable.
- The erectile-dysfunction-in-5–10-years framing is used as a motivational tool; individual risk varies widely and the timeline is not a precise medical threshold.
- Suggestions that oxygen bars lace oxygen with addictive ingredients are speculation/opinion, not evidence.

## Summary
Pulmonologist and intensivist Dr Parimal explains why heart attacks and lung clots rose after COVID: the virus makes blood clot-prone, lockdowns left people deconditioned, and sudden strenuous exertion on narrowed vessels (as in the Gujarat Garba deaths) proved fatal — so recovery exercise must be graded over months. He then covers long COVID's definitions and workup, the teen vaping epidemic and a practical nicotine-replacement quitting plan (patches 21/14/7 mg, chew-and-park gum, a firm quit date), secondhand and thirdhand smoke, PM2.5/AQI risks with N95 protection, and why every adult should learn CPR and the Heimlich manoeuvre.
