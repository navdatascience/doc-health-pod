## Important tidbits
- His list of the Indian community's major problems: overeating, too much omega-6 seed oil driving cellular inflammation, too little exercise, inadequate protein (especially among vegetarians), and poor sleep — he says many Indians sleep less than 7-8 hours and get only 30-40 minutes of deep sleep, with poor heart rate variability and resting heart rate as a result.
- His daily "toll" for living: walk 30 minutes every day, and do resistance exercise three times a week; he argues 80% of health is breathing and sleeping, both of which are free.
- Fats he favours: ghee and butter (butter, he notes, contains C15 fat), and high-quality extra-virgin olive oil for its polyphenols — but olive oil should not be used for cooking; steam the food and drizzle the oil on top.
- His sleep routine: skip dinner, take 5 g of glycine and magnesium before bed, drink chamomile tea, and have Greek yogurt with a gooseberry or blueberry — he claims this gives fantastic sleep.
- Morning routine: drink 250 ml of water on waking (unless you have a kidney problem — he advises kidney patients not to drink water after 6 pm so the kidney can rest), and look at the sun on waking so the ganglion cells in the eye set the brain's circadian rhythm for sleep and work.
- Eat at fixed times so the gut's own circadian rhythm works correctly; he advises skipping dinner altogether — resting heart rate and stomach workload come down — and says fasting beyond 16 hours triggers autophagy, where good cells clear out bad cells.
- Meal order matters: take protein first at every meal (vegetarians should start with fibre-based vegetables), and cut down rice and wheat; he says grain protein is absorbed at a maximum of about 67% efficiency and that soya is the complete plant protein.
- The one food he says everyone must add: black dal with its skin — its fibre lines the gut wall like a grease layer, slowing gastric emptying and carbohydrate absorption "like acarbose"; second is ginger in any form (fresh, dried or juice), including first thing in the morning, which he says modulates GLP-1 via tongue receptors.
- Glycine is the one supplement he recommends for everyone in India: the body requires about 13 g a day but produces only about 3 g, so take at least 5 g extra; he suggests glycine plus NAC instead of glutathione IVs.
- Do not drink any soft drink with sugar: 55 g of sugar in plain water would make you vomit, but cola doesn't because phosphoric acid is added; he also warns that artificial sweeteners can increase the insulin response even without raising sugar, and limits himself to one can of cola in about 15 days.
- Keep carbohydrate low — he suggests roughly 50 to 100 g a day, coming from cruciferous vegetables and greens (red cabbage is "cheap insurance") — arguing there is no essential carbohydrate and the body can manage on about 56 g.
- Cravings are controlled by protein: they fade within 24-48 hours, adequate protein prevents them, and if a craving hits, eat 20 g of protein or two egg whites; protein at one meal also reduces calorie intake at the next; chew each mouthful about 30 times and eat mindfully, because "the teeth are in the mouth, not in the stomach."
- He cites 1.2 g of ginger before a meal to slow gastric emptying and absorption, and says gut bacteria are most active around 5 pm and least active around 5 am, so the last meal should be around 4:35 pm.
- Choose berries over other fruit: raspberries carry about 8 g of fibre per ounce and berries barely raise glucose; he takes 1 g of dried maqui berry daily; ripe fruit and fruit juice are the problem because fructose can be handled only by the liver — unripe green banana and raw jackfruit are fine, ripe jackfruit is not; sugar in all its forms (he notes it hides under dozens of names, including jaggery and honey) should be avoided.
- Coffee: black coffee is excellent, and his add-ons include ginger, 2 g of taurine, a drop of ghee, and magnesium threonate, which he says crosses the blood-brain barrier; he calls milk inflammatory and prefers Greek or Icelandic yogurt and kefir; prebiotic fibre should be at least 25 g a day, and can go up to 50 g.
- For constipation: take fibre, massage the stomach, do malasana yoga, and use a squatting posture so evacuation is easy — he says the first thing anyone wanting good health must ensure is that they are not constipated.

## Claims to be careful with
- The guest is a businessman and wellness enthusiast, not a doctor; the routines above (skipping dinner daily, 50-100 g of carbohydrate, glycine and taurine dosing) are his personal regimen and opinions, presented without cited studies.
- Several specific numbers are asserted without sources in the episode: taurine extending life by 18 years, olive oil "improving 40 biomarkers," only about 40% of Indians having the gut bacteria to make urolithin from pomegranate, grain protein absorption capped at 67%, and gut bacteria peaking at 5 pm.
- His advice that kidney patients stop drinking water after 6 pm is his own rule — fluid restriction for kidney disease is highly individual and should follow a nephrologist's advice, not a podcast.
- "Milk is inflammatory" and the claim that fasting beyond 16 hours makes "good cells eat the bad cells" are simplifications of contested or more nuanced science.

## Summary
Wellness enthusiast Sivasankaran lays out his personal health system for Dr Pal: Indians overeat, eat too much omega-6 seed oil, under-consume protein, and sleep badly, so he prescribes daily 30-minute walks, resistance training three times a week, 7-8 hours of sleep helped by glycine, magnesium and skipping dinner, fixed meal times, protein-first low-carb eating built on black dal, ginger, berries and cruciferous vegetables, no sugary drinks, and black coffee with add-ons like taurine and ghee. His closing tip is to sleep without worry — "love is the solution" for gut and mental health alike.
