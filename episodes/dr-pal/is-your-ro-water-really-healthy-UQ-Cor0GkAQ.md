## Important tidbits
- Dr Pal says purification matters in India because water sources vary widely — borewell, municipal tap and tanker water carry different contaminants, heavy metals and microorganisms, and municipal supply is not standardised the way tap water in the US is, where he says you can drink from the tap.
- The guest says an RO membrane works like an ultra-fine sieve that removes microorganisms, heavy metals and impurities, but it has no "intelligence" to distinguish good from bad — so essential minerals (sodium, potassium, calcium, magnesium) are washed away along with the contaminants, leaving clean water that may not be beneficial water.
- Dr Pal links this to rising micronutrient deficiencies: he says vitamin D and mineral deficiencies are increasingly common, and the micronutrient content of rice and wheat is not what it was 20 years ago because of soil and cultivation changes.
- Mineral cartridges in RO systems add back a standard, manufacturer-chosen set of minerals, but the guest argues added minerals are not the same as native ones — he compares it to a whole orange versus orange-flavoured juice, and processed packaged food versus natural homemade food — both in absorption and in the water's regional "nativity."
- TDS (total dissolved solids) is explained as the total salts/solids that give water its hardness; the guest says a TDS below 50 typically means the calcium and other minerals have essentially all been removed, and that the myth "higher TDS is bad, lower TDS is good" ignores that a right level of TDS is essential for the body.
- The guest puts the desirable retained range at roughly 150–200 TDS, with calcium around 30–40 and magnesium around 20–25 (units as stated in the episode), so minerals are retained in their natural ratios rather than added or stripped.
- CDI (capacitive deionisation) is presented as the alternative: low-voltage electricity passes through a chamber with positive and negative electrodes that attract ions like a magnet, flushing out only what is not required and retaining what is required — described as a first-of-its-kind technology for residential use in India.
- The described CDI process is staged: a micro-deactivation chamber for microbes (the guest says it is similar in effect to boiling, so no boiling is needed afterwards), an activated charcoal chamber to remove odour and chlorine, advanced filtration for heavy metals, then the CDI chamber itself.
- On water waste, the guest says RO needs about 4 litres of water to produce 1 litre of drinking water, while CDI saves about 70% of water by comparison; he estimates 100 families using the CDI purifier for a year would save about 6 lakh litres.
- As evidence, the guest cites a 7-day fenugreek seed-germination test run under identical light, water and conditions, varying only the water source: seeds given CDI water showed about 50% more growth than those given RO and other waters, with higher protein/mineral retention — a process he says was supervised and certified by TÜV.
- On canned/bubble-top water at gyms and offices, the guest warns the seal, storage conditions and journey matter: microplastics can leach in depending on temperature and storage, and he says the cans themselves should not be used beyond 6 months and should then be disposed of, or microplastics can enter the water.
- The CDI unit is described as a connected (IoT/app) machine that shows daily TDS and tracks how many litres you drink, with reminders — so users do not need a separate TDS-checking device.
- On price, the guest says the product launches at ₹39,999 including everything for the first five years, arguing total cost of ownership is similar to or less than RO once RO annual maintenance and filter changes are counted; it launches under Crompton's premium Rion brand, with fully refundable ₹999 pre-booking, initially in Chennai, Coimbatore, Madurai and Bengaluru, in stores from around mid-September.
- The guest is explicit that water is only one part of health: he says drinking this water alone will not make you healthier — food, activity, sleep and lifestyle still determine the holistic outcome — though he expects a noticeable taste difference at first as palatability moves from bland RO water toward the region's natural water taste.

## Claims to be careful with
- This episode is substantially a product presentation for Crompton Rion's Bio CDI purifier by a company representative — the CDI-versus-RO comparisons, the 70% water-saving figure, the 6-lakh-litre estimate and the seed-germination results are the manufacturer's own claims and test, not independent clinical evidence.
- The claim that retained minerals in CDI water are better absorbed than minerals added back by an RO mineral cartridge is asserted by analogy (whole fruit versus flavoured juice) rather than demonstrated with human absorption data in the episode.
- The TÜV-supervised seed test used plant germination over 7 days as its outcome; plant growth is not direct evidence of a health benefit in people.

## Summary
Dr Pal hosts a Crompton Rion representative to question whether RO water is really healthy: RO removes contaminants but also strips native minerals and wastes roughly 3 litres for every litre produced, while the company's CDI technology uses low-voltage electrodes to selectively remove unwanted dissolved solids and retain natural minerals at a TDS of about 150–200, backed by a TÜV-observed seed-germination test — with the caveat that this is a manufacturer-led product episode and water is only one part of overall health.
