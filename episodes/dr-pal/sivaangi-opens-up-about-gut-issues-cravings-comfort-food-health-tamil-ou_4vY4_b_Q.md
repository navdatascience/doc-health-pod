## Important tidbits
- Sivaangi has been lactose intolerant since childhood — cow's milk always made her vomit. Dr Pal explains most people keep milk tolerance until 25–30, but her gut enzyme lactase was likely low from birth. She uses almond and oat milk, yet eats curd freely: she ate curd rice for lunch and dinner for about 15 years.
- Greek yogurt has roughly 10 g of protein versus 3–4 g in regular curd, because it is strained and concentrated. Hung curd — curd strained in a muslin cloth — is the home equivalent, and it also makes raita and chutneys less sour and tastier.
- The gut carries about 100 trillion bacteria, Dr Pal says. Babies born vaginally pick up bacteria from the birth canal; C-section babies pick up bacteria from the environment instead, and mother's milk helps balance things out for them.
- "Plant points": aim for at least 5 different plant foods a day, which Dr Pal says gets you to roughly 25 g of fibre daily. Sivaangi's typical day — lady's finger, potato, mango, plus delivered salads with cucumber and capsicum — already gets her there; Nendran banana as a first food helps her "flush out" by night because of its fibre.
- Her pizza era: three pizzas a week, a whole medium pizza eaten solo, cheese-stuffed garlic bread every evening. She was diagnosed with thyroid at 13 (around 2013) and hit puberty at 10 — Dr Pal notes puberty at 10–11 is now common. A pizza slice runs 250–300 kcal, mostly from the maida base and cheese; she now stops at about 3 slices and prefers fermented sourdough pizza (Franco Manca in London is her pick), which she finds light and quick to digest.
- The craving loop, per Dr Pal: ultra-processed food grows the bad gut bacteria, and those bacteria signal the brain that the food tastes good and to get more of it. PMS cravings specifically are driven by progesterone — and he says you cannot and should not "cut off" the hormone; keeping the gut healthy helps it regulate itself.
- Vegetarian deficiencies: Sivaangi lists hers — B12, vitamin D, calcium ("a disaster" because she takes no milk) and iron. Dr Pal says B12 deficiency is super common in vegetarians with no real veg source, and mushrooms are the only vegetarian vitamin D source — exposing mushrooms to sunlight/UV sharply raises their vitamin D.
- Her sleep is the big problem: she watches reels until about 2 AM and manages shoot days on roughly 4 hours of sleep. Dr Pal's fix is gradual — shift bedtime back 15 minutes at a time (2 AM → 1:45 → 1:30) and keep the phone out of reach for the last 30 minutes, comparing a sudden 10 PM bedtime to a smoker quitting cold turkey.
- The insulin "breakup" story they sing through: years of late nights, ultra-processed food and no exercise make insulin stop responding — and most people only discover diabetes and BP at their 35–40 health checks, not at 25–30. Three things win insulin back: a 12-hour fast (dinner by 8 PM, breakfast at 8–9 AM), about 7 hours of sleep, and 20 minutes of movement every day.
- On sleep for women: Dr Pal says recent studies suggest women need about one extra hour of sleep because of their greater hormonal load — ideally 8 hours, against the 4 she currently gets.
- Don't stack carb on carb: curd rice plus mango together is a heavy sugar load at one sitting (a whole mango is about 300 kcal; two slices roughly 50). Eat the mango at a different time, or pair it with a protein like grilled paneer, tofu or channa.
- Street-food fixes: pani puri is not bad as street food goes — make it healthier by swapping the potato filling for sprouts, channa or kala channa. Panakam (ginger and jaggery) gets a pass for the ginger, but jaggery is not automatically healthy: about 1 tablespoon of white sugar equals 2 tablespoons of jaggery in sugar terms, and karupatti coffee typically uses 2–3 times more sweetener to taste right — total sugar amount is what matters.
- Popcorn is a genuinely light snack plain (a small tub is about 120 kcal), but butter and seasoning take it to about 450 kcal, and the seasoning is loaded with sodium. Steamed momos with vegetable, corn, mushroom or cabbage fillings beat the fried, saucy kind she used to eat.
- Frozen food is fine: Dr Pal says people are not obese because food is frozen but because they eat too much — his own US staple was frozen carrots and peas boiled with pepper and salt. Air-frying vadam beats deep-frying it, and microwave radiation is negligible. Oats are also fine — the difference from white rice is fibre; polished white rice has had everything stripped, while brown, red and matta rice keep the bran and husk.
- Water: Dr Pal puts the target at about 3 litres a day, 3.5 litres if you work out. Sivaangi admits she drinks under 500 ml and rarely feels thirst — flagged on the show as a real problem.
- Two of Dr Pal's closing frameworks: post-meal sweet cravings come from gut bacteria, not from "you" — trick them with something like dark-chocolate-covered almonds; and sugar genuinely ages the skin faster, because gut, skin and brain are connected (the gut has the second-largest number of neurons after the brain, and skin is the largest organ). Pets in the home are good for small kids because exposure to varied bacteria trains the body to tell helpful from harmful.

## Claims to be careful with
- Dr Pal speculates on air that Sivaangi's pizza-heavy diet may have played a small role in her thyroid diagnosis at 13, while also saying genetics is the main factor and that in her case "can't say" — treat the food–thyroid link here as speculation.
- On stevia: asked whether it causes cancer, Dr Pal advises avoiding it if possible, says each use gives the gut lining "a tiny hit," but allows short-term use (about 4–6 weeks) as a weight-loss motivator — this is his personal take, not a settled safety verdict.
- The claim that women need an extra hour of sleep, and that fixing the gut controlled some hyperthyroid patients' thyroid levels within 2–3 years, are stated loosely on the show without cited studies.
- "100 trillion bacteria" in the gut and bacteria passing "to another body" after death are Dr Pal's simplified ways of describing the microbiome.
- Sourdough pizza "digesting in less than 5 hours" is Sivaangi's personal experience as stated on the show.

## Summary
In this Tamil episode of *Gut Feeling with Dr Pal*, actor and singer Sivaangi talks through her gut issues with disarming honesty: lifelong lactose intolerance alongside a 15-year curd-rice habit, a teenage pizza obsession and thyroid diagnosis, deficiencies in B12, vitamin D, calcium and iron, almost no water intake, and a 2 AM reels habit that leaves her running on 4 hours of sleep. Dr Pal turns it into a practical gut-health lesson — plant points and fibre, gradual sleep repair, the 12-hour fast, and why your cravings are your gut bacteria talking.
