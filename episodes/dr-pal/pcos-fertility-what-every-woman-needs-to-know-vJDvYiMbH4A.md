## Important tidbits
- PCOD vs PCOS, as Dr Deepti explains it: PCOD (the disease) means irregular cycles, usually higher BMI (though lean PCOS exists), fatigue and hair thinning, together with the typical polycystic pattern of the ovaries on ultrasound; it becomes PCOS (the syndrome) when metabolic features appear — insulin resistance, excess androgen, excessive facial hair (hirsutism), an abnormal waist-hip ratio, or a borderline-diabetic range.
- Because PCOS involves the metabolic system, it raises the risk of type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular disease — that systemic involvement is what makes it a syndrome rather than just a disease.
- Do not self-diagnose: she says patients routinely arrive having diagnosed themselves from the internet; the first step is to come to a doctor (a gynaecologist) and let them confirm it.
- Lifestyle change is the first treatment for both PCOD and PCOS — if you are in a higher BMI range, check your lifestyle before anything else.
- Stress, sleeplessness and junk food form a vicious cycle: irregular periods make you moody, you eat more junk to soothe yourself, and the cycle worsens; she notes children from class 9-10 are pushed into only studies with no physical activity, and thyroid problems are another cause of irregular periods to rule out.
- A period delayed by a month is definitely abnormal and should be checked.
- Her gadget rule for the whole family: stop using phones after 8 pm — read a book instead, which will make you sleepy anyway; when parents scroll late, children's dinner and sleep also get delayed.
- Breastfeeding mothers should not watch phones while feeding their babies — she advises skin-to-skin contact, a calm, dim room and soft music instead of a screen.
- On timing pregnancy: if you marry around 24-25, plan pregnancy within one or two years and preferably complete your family before 30; chromosomal abnormalities are more common both in very early pregnancies (under about 19-20) and after 35-37, and "advanced maternal age" is now counted after 37 rather than 35.
- Egg freezing is safe and not complicated: the egg stays the age at which it was frozen (freeze at 25 and the egg is still "25" when you are 35), it is best done before 35 because egg quality declines sharply after that, eggs are graded and only good-quality ones are preserved, and frozen eggs can be used any time — even 10 or 15 years later.
- Sperm freezing is an option for couples where the husband is away most of the year — for example sailors and navy personnel who are home only 3 to 4 months — so the couple is not forced to spend those months only trying to conceive.
- For permanent contraception, she says vasectomy is a much simpler procedure than tubal ligation and needs far more awareness.
- Menstrual cramps actually reduce when you avoid junk food and caffeine; she advises increasing seeds in the diet, and says walking, yoga or even swimming (with a menstrual cup) can relieve cramps better.
- Her practical diet prescription for PCOS patients is small tweaks: fibre in every meal, increased water intake, fixed sleep, and reduced gadget use — patients feel a lot better with just these changes.
- The most common dietary mistake she sees: sugary, processed food ordered daily under the logic of "it's only today," which continues until patients are bloated, dull, and need medicine to induce a period.

## Claims to be careful with
- Dr Deepti advises that birth control pills should not be used for more than 3 to 6 months and links excessive pill use to thromboembolism (blood clots), stroke, and even ovarian or breast cancers — this is stronger and more categorical than standard contraceptive guidance; pill choice and duration should be individualised with your own doctor.
- Her recommendation to "complete your family before 30" is her professional advice based on chromosomal-abnormality risk; it is not a rule that fits every woman's circumstances.

## Summary
OB-GYN Dr Deepti explains the difference between PCOD (irregular cycles, fatigue, hair thinning and the ultrasound ovarian pattern) and PCOS (the syndrome, once insulin resistance, excess androgen and metabolic risk are involved), warns against self-diagnosis, and puts lifestyle change — fibre at every meal, sleep, less gadget time, less junk — ahead of medicine. She also covers fertility timing (preferably before 30, advanced maternal age now after 37), egg and sperm freezing as safe options for delayed or long-distance marriages, vasectomy awareness, and simple ways to ease menstrual cramps.
