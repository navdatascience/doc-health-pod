## Important tidbits
- Basu's core mantra: everyone should think and live like an athlete — train well, eat well, sleep well, repeat. A proper meal plan automatically covers carbohydrates, protein, good fats, vegetables, and fibre, so you don't need to overthink individual nutrients. At 56, he plays three hours of table tennis daily, which he calls "dynamic meditation."
- Fibre: Dr Pal recommends a minimum of 20–30 g of fibre per day, and Basu agrees it is a key factor, especially for non-vegetarians. Food order matters — start a meal with protein, good fat, or fibre (Basu starts his day with avocado, olives, and coconut, then eggs; soup and salad before lunch) so the insulin spike from sugars is blunted.
- "You don't lose weight to become healthy — you become healthy, you lose weight automatically." A person with excess body fat is usually under-fed on nutrients; starving switches on fat-storing enzymes and is the wrong route to fat loss. Athletes eat three solid meals plus snacks.
- Ignore the scale and get a DEXA scan: what matters is whether you are losing fat or muscle. Adding muscle is inversely related to fat percentage, and muscle is the most metabolically active tissue — Basu says clients should come to him with a DEXA report the way a business starts with accounts, and that the scan's radiation is negligible (less than a chest or dental X-ray).
- For non-vegetarians, his one-line rule: for every bite of chicken or meat, take a bite of fruit or vegetable alongside. He notes the Indian restaurant problem — chicken arrives with more carbs and a token red chilli, whereas in the US it comes with salad or vegetables.
- On ultra-processed food, Dr Pal's line: every packet you tear open, you tear a piece of your gut — one mistake occasionally is fine, but the same mistake daily at multiple levels eventually causes significant damage.
- The only two supplements Basu endorses are whey protein and creatine — he calls creatine the most researched supplement ever. He is strongly against anabolic steroids, which he says are pushed on young athletes by local coaches or pharmacists chasing medals, with no thought for long-term damage; Dr Pal recounts an 18-year-old whose stomach lining was "completely ripped off" (severe gastritis) from steroid use for American football.
- Hydration rule of thumb from Basu: body weight × 4 (in centilitres) — a 50 kg person needs at least about 2 litres a day — and increase it by roughly 25% when playing in summer heat. Dr Pal advises front-loading water in the morning and easing off after about 5–6 pm so the kidneys can rest overnight; the body adapts to a new intake pattern over time.
- Best time to work out, per Dr Pal: around 2–3 pm, when insulin and anabolic hormones are favourable for building muscle — if that timing is convenient.
- Move organically toward about 15,000 steps a day through lifestyle rather than forced treadmill sessions. Don't stack 30 minutes of cardio, then weights, then 20 more minutes of cardio in one session: strength training is anabolic (testosterone up), while excessive cardio is catabolic and raises cortisol — he cites trainer Charles Poliquin's term "chunky aerobic instructor syndrome" for high-fat-percentage aerobics instructors who lost muscle from too much cardio. Cardio still matters for heart health, in the right dose.
- Intermittent fasting is a kick-start tool, not a permanent lifestyle: Dr Pal lost weight from 100 kg to 70 kg on time-restricted feeding but lost much of his muscle because he skipped strength training, and rebuilding it later is far harder. Basu warns that prolonged fasting or going under ~1,200 calories shuts down metabolic rate and eventually triggers fat storage; athletes cannot perform or recover while fasting.
- Alcohol: Basu says after weekend drinking it takes until about Wednesday for a man's testosterone to return to normal — so drinking every Saturday means never fully recovering.
- Before starting at a gym: get blood tests, a DEXA scan, and check blood pressure and family history (heart attack, diabetes). Sudden deaths in gyms, he says, usually involve pre-existing conditions, prescribed medication not being taken, years of poor lifestyle, or a beginner doing too much too soon — exercise itself is not the culprit, and the same risk exists while walking or working at a desk.
- Crunches do not reduce belly fat: fat is stored and lost overall, its distribution is partly genetic (abdomen, thighs, subscapular, suprailiac patterns vary), and spot reduction doesn't work. Excessive crunches mainly produced back pain — which, he jokes, built his rehab business.
- Mental fitness is now built into elite cricket: most top players have personal mental coaches alongside physios, dietitians, and trainers, and even state-level players access them. Bench players stay resilient despite competing against a population of 1.5 billion for a place; he credits Jonty Rhodes' 1992 World Cup run-out with putting fielding on the map, and Virat Kohli (from about 2014–15) with making supreme fitness the norm in cricket.

## Claims to be careful with
- Basu mentions creatine injections helping older clients with low serum creatine recover from aches and pains — this is not a standard, mainstream use of creatine.
- His theory that "hill boys" like Rishabh Pant, MS Dhoni, and Pawan Negi are naturally stronger because of higher haemoglobin from growing up at altitude is presented as his personal theory, not established evidence.
- The claim that testosterone takes until about Wednesday to normalise after weekend alcohol is a coaching rule of thumb, not a measured figure.
- Dr Pal's framing that kidneys "rest" overnight on a circadian rhythm (and that nephrons adapt to drinking schedules) is a simplified explanation rather than established renal physiology.

## Summary
Strength and conditioning coach Basu Shankar — the man behind the fitness transformation of Virat Kohli, MS Dhoni, and the RCB setup — lays out his athlete's lifestyle for ordinary people: eat balanced meals starting with protein/fat/fibre, judge progress by DEXA-measured fat and muscle rather than the scale, take only whey and creatine, hydrate by body weight, favour strength training over excessive cardio, use fasting only as a starter, get medically screened before joining a gym, and accept that crunches will never spot-reduce a belly.
