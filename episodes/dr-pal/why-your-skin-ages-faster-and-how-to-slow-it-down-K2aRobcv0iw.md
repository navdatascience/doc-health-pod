## Important tidbits
- Dr Vaz says skin ageing has no fixed start age — he has seen it begin at 16–18 in some and stay delayed into the late 30s in others; it depends on genes, lifestyle, climate and how early skincare is maintained.
- The first mirror signs are not wrinkles: look at skin tone and texture first — mild pigmentary changes and crepiness (crinkly, crumpled-paper-like skin) — then fine lines. Lines can be dynamic (only on expression) or static (always present); forehead lines are most prominent in Indians because they are expressive.
- Ageing cannot be stopped, only slowed: maintain gut/diet, use sunscreen regularly, and keep a basic regimen matched to skin type rather than fancy multi-step routines.
- Retinol genuinely helps but is overhyped as an "elixir": used wrongly or on the wrong skin type it can darken skin and worsen lines. It must be paired with sunscreen and a ceramide-based moisturiser; his own pattern is alternate nights, short contact, layered with moisturiser.
- On sunscreen, he says the 12–18-hour protection claims are lab-based; in real life reapplication about once every 3 hours is good enough, and the right product changes with climate, skin type and season (what suits in the US differs from Mumbai).
- Botox/neurotoxins relax muscles; on-label areas are forehead lines, frown (corrugator) lines and crow's feet. At optimal dose a treatment lasts about 4–6 months (company claims of 8–9 months are "jargon"); a deliberately lighter dose (e.g. 6 units instead of 10) lasts only 3–4 months. Masseter injections can slim a square face, but that is a specialised off-label use.
- Rapid weight loss removes facial fat and muscle — on GLP-1 drugs (Ozempic/Mounjaro), which he notes can produce 15–20% weight loss, this creates the "Ozempic/zombie face." In Indians, he observes the under-eye fat goes first, then the buccal (cheek) fat, and gravity drags the rest forward.
- Collagen is the skin's structural pillar. Collagen creams cannot rebuild lost structure, and oral collagen does not replace collagen — it acts as a protein precursor that stimulates your own collagen production over time.
- Fillers vs bio-stimulators: cross-linked fillers lift, project or volumise (structural change); bio-stimulators stimulate fibroblasts to make collagen without much added volume. A bio-stimulator typically needs two sessions about 30 days apart for roughly 1–1.5 years of effect; one session gives about 6–8 months. These are ~30-minute "lunchtime" injection procedures.
- He dismisses face yoga: yoga was built for the body, not the face; expression alone cannot tone facial muscle the way resistance builds a bicep.
- Sugar accelerates ageing, he says, by binding to collagen and elastin and making them brittle (glycation) while blocking regrowth — he describes sibling pairs where the high-sugar one looked late-40s and the other early-20s in their mid-30s.
- Stress raises cortisol and inflammatory factors, and skin, nails and hair are outward manifestations of your internal state. Sleep cannot be traded for skincare: sleeping 5–6 hours while doing elaborate skincare will not compensate — lifestyle basics come first.
- Peels work when matched to the skin's demand, not the patient's wish: a basic maintenance peel about once in 3 weeks to a month; stronger phenol-based peels (e.g. Nomelan) for oily, acne-prone skin and scarring.
- Safety warning: fillers injected by unqualified people (salons, parlours, unlicensed injectors) can enter a vessel and cause vascular occlusion — extreme pain, loss of tissue such as the lip, and even blindness, for which he says documented cases exist. Choose a licensed, certified injector; read the content of Google reviews (many are paid), and take a second opinion if you feel oversold.

## Claims to be careful with
- His statement that sunscreen "doesn't last for more than a few minutes" sits oddly with his own once-every-3-hours reapplication advice — treat the exact duration claims cautiously.
- Longevity figures for bio-stimulators (1–1.5 years for two sessions) and filler durations are from his clinical practice, not controlled data presented in the episode.
- Exosomes and PDRN are described as promising for skin tone/texture, but he acknowledges they are still being researched.
- The sibling sugar-ageing comparison and the "zombie face" framing are anecdotal/clinical impressions, not study results.

## Summary
Interventional dermatologist Dr Jeffrey Vaz walks through how skin ageing actually shows up (tone, texture, crepiness, then dynamic and static lines), what genuinely slows it — diet, sunscreen, judicious retinol, sleep and stress control — and what interventional dermatology can and cannot do for fat loss in the face in the Ozempic era: fillers for structure, bio-stimulators for collagen, and why collagen creams, face yoga and unlicensed injectors deserve scepticism.
