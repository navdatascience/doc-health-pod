## Important tidbits
- Anu Hasan credits her upbringing: appearance was never a focus at home — her parents focused on abilities, her mother insisted on eating well, and the house rule was "whatever is on your plate, finish it," which taught her to see food as fuel and eat anything.
- Her father had his first heart attack in 1995, changed his lifestyle immediately, and stayed hale and healthy until he passed away in 2017 — her example that lifestyle change after a cardiac event works.
- "Diet" was a bad word in their family; they had no TV at home for a long time, and she still has no TV at home.
- She sleeps 7 to 8 hours and uses no alarm — her body wakes up when it has had its 7 to 8 hours.
- Strength training became crucial around perimenopause: through progressive overload she has gone from 2 kg bicep curls and a 20 kg deadlift to a 10 kg isometric bicep hold and an 81 kg maximum deadlift.
- Myth busted: lemon water does not burn belly fat — she says she wishes it were true, but it is not.
- Myth busted: lifting weights does not make women bulky — women don't have the testosterone for it; her own arms look leaner, not bulkier, since lifting heavier, and bulking would need deliberate high protein/creatine intake.
- After menopause, resistance training matters more, not less: with estrogen gone, muscle and bone loss accelerate; Dr Pal notes even men lose about 10% of muscle per decade after age 40, so women need to actively fight that loss.
- Myth busted: carbs are not bad — they are essential for energy, moderation is the key, and the glucose spike seen on a CGM after eating (even idly/idli) is a normal process; the real problem is insulin resistance, when insulin stops handling the spike.
- Skipping meals by itself doesn't cause weight loss: a calorie deficit does (Dr Pal says in about 90% of cases, absent a health condition), but skipping breakfast and then eating two to three times as much at lunch and dinner will not work; in menopause a deficit may also not work the same way.
- She eats roughly 1,800 to 2,000 calories a day and flatly rejected a doctor who once told her to eat only 1,000 calories.
- Her rule for her weakness, potato chips: she never stocks them at home — if she wants chips she must walk to the shop, buy them and walk back, which makes it happen far less often; she also enjoys desserts and even promises herself cake as a reward for a fast workout round, in moderation.
- Her father was diagnosed diabetic at 43 and teased her that she would be too; she bet she would not be diabetic until 50 — at 55 her HbA1c is 5.4, which she credits to her muscle mass, since muscle consumes sugar and also protects her bones if she falls.
- A personal trainer is not essential — free apps such as Nike Training Club have plenty of workouts — but if you use a trainer, pick one who understands your mind as well as your body; if you are constantly getting injured, he is the wrong trainer for you.
- On loneliness: being alone is not the same as being lonely — loneliness is feeling that nobody understands you, and the fix is to find even one person who understands you; they don't need to be a constant companion, just knowing someone "gets you" is itself the solution.

## Claims to be careful with
- Dr Pal says that in his US practice his patients on Ozempic (GLP-1) are seeing side effects including bone-density loss and a stomach that "doesn't contract, it is paralysed" (gastroparesis) — this is his anecdotal practice experience as stated in the episode, not clinical-trial data.
- Dr Pal's "calorie deficit works in 90% of cases" figure is a rough estimate offered in conversation, not a cited statistic.
- Anu Hasan's HbA1c of 5.4 at 55 is her personal result; it does not guarantee the same routine prevents diabetes for everyone.

## Summary
Actress and fitness enthusiast Anu Hasan (55) describes a childhood where food was fuel and appearance was never discussed, and how that foundation became a lifestyle built on 7-8 hours of sleep, progressive strength training (up to an 81 kg deadlift) that became essential at perimenopause, and moderation rather than restriction. She and Dr Pal bust common myths — lemon water for belly fat, weights making women bulky, carbs being bad, and meal-skipping for weight loss — and she closes with a distinction between being alone and being lonely, arguing mental wellbeing starts with being understood.
