## Important tidbits
- Revant studied nutrition for a year in the US in 2015 and worked in consulting there before returning to India; his first label-reading video (on Bournvita) came out in April 2023 and went viral.
- He argues that roughly 70% of what Indians eat today is packaged food, and that learning to read a food label is the most important skill of the 21st century — he jokes it matters more than coding.
- His core rule: the front of a pack is marketing, the back is the truth. Most Indians, he says, only check two things — MRP and expiry date — before buying.
- Ingredients are listed in descending order of weight, so he tells people to avoid products where maida, sugar or palm oil appear in the first three to five ingredients.
- On Kissan Fresh Tomato Ketchup: the word "fresh" is trademarked, and a tomato-shaped symbol replaces the usual asterisk to hide the disclaimer; sugar is 3.8 g per 15 g serving — about one teaspoon (he uses 4 g = 1 teaspoon as a rule of thumb).
- His main target is hidden sugar: baked samosas eaten with ketchup can add up to around 10 teaspoons of sugar in a meal without the eater realising it, because ketchup is now marketed for daily use on dosas, rotis and school tiffins.
- He attacks "everything in moderation" as an abused phrase: having 20 different unhealthy things in moderation across a day is not moderation at all — collectively, he says, it becomes inflammation.
- Sucralose is about 200 times sweeter than sugar, so it is used in tiny amounts and never appears in the top three ingredients — but he says gut bacteria adapt to it and can drive cravings for it over two to three months.
- Palm oil is one of the cheapest oils available and is high in saturated fat; he says India imports about ₹50,000 crore worth of palm oil every year, and notes Pringles uses palm oil in India but not in the US.
- Many drinks sold as "ORS" are not genuine WHO-formula oral rehydration salts — he estimates only about 20–30% are — and some even carry a warning not to consume them during diarrhoea, which he calls a serious deception for parents.
- Bourbon biscuits, per gram, contain more sugar than KitKat, he says — yet families treat biscuits as fine for daily eating (around 28 biscuits a week) while chocolate is an occasional treat.
- A 300 mL bottle of Frooti contains about 10 teaspoons of sugar, with just 11.2% mango pulp; water is the first ingredient and sugar the second — many people switch from Coke to Frooti believing the mango drink is healthier.
- In a rapid-fire label check, he notes a "cashew cookies" pack contains only 1.8% cashew nuts, with maida as the first ingredient; and a cereal claiming 20 g of protein gets 15 g of that from the milk, not the cereal.
- Alcohol carries no nutrition label or ingredient list at all — which he attributes to strong lobbying — and a 1.5 oz serving of hard liquor is roughly 120–140 calories, or around 300 calories once mixed with a sweet mixer.
- His shopping advice: walk the periphery of a grocery store, where the fresh food sits, and avoid the centre aisles, which are mostly packaged food.
- He wants nutrition taught as a school subject from Class 1 to 8, and says his "sugar board" idea — showing the sugar content of drinks in school canteens — has already been adopted by many schools.
- As a would-be food policy minister, he would mandate a QR code on every pack translating the label into regional languages, noting only about 5–10% of Indians understand English while every label is printed in English.

## Claims to be careful with
- The "70% of what we eat is packaged food" and "5–10% of Indians understand English" figures are his own estimates, offered without a source.
- The claim that gut bacteria adapt to sucralose and create cravings for it is his own framing; the evidence on non-nutritive sweeteners and the microbiome is still evolving and contested.
- The "20–30% of ORS products are genuine WHO-formula" estimate is his own, not a published survey result.
- He says Bourbon biscuits have more sugar per gram than KitKat and that 28 biscuits a week versus one chocolate is the real diabetes driver — the per-gram comparison is presented without a named source.
- His figure of ₹50,000 crore of annual palm oil imports is stated from memory without a cited source.
- His claim that alcohol has no nutrition label because of "strong lobbying" is speculation about industry influence, not an established fact.

## Summary
Revant Himatsingka, the creator behind @Foodpharmer, walks Dr Pal through how to read a food label — from the order-of-weight ingredient list and the maida/sugar/palm-oil rule to the hidden sugars in ketchup, Frooti and biscuits, the fake ORS products, and the missing labels on alcohol — arguing that India's nutrition problem is not occasional treats but everyday packaged foods, and that schools, clearer front-of-pack labelling and regional-language QR codes are the fixes.
