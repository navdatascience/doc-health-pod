## Important tidbits
- Osteoarthritis is striking younger: over Dr Bose's 25 years of practice the average knee-replacement patient has dropped from around 70 to about 55 years old, and he has replaced knees in patients under 50.
- Women get knee osteoarthritis more than men (roughly a 3:1 ratio in his surgical practice), and he sees higher incidence in Telugu, Gujarati and Punjabi populations, which he attributes partly to genetics, alongside obesity, diet and lifestyle.
- Modern knee replacement recovery is fast: patients walk the same evening, climb stairs by day 2, go home around day 3 with a stick, and are back to normal activities in about 3–4 weeks.
- On supplements, his core point: estimate blood levels before supplementing — never give calcium or vitamin D blindly. Excess calcium is linked to kidney stones and cardiac and intestinal problems, and excess vitamin C also causes harm once you are no longer deficient.
- Vitamin D, per Dr Bose: levels up to ~30 are what matter for bone and dental health; above that it matters for metabolic health, which is why he calls vitamin D a hormone affecting every organ system. Labs don't print a normal range for vitamin D because the range is controversial.
- Ortho shoes and custom insoles are only for people with symptoms like foot pain; for healthy people he advises walking barefoot, because if footwear does the foot's work, you lose the ability — "use it or lose it."
- Asked to pick the best sport for a child's lifelong fitness, he chooses tennis over cricket, swimming, basketball, football, table tennis and athletics: swimming alone is "unidimensional" (cardio only), while tennis adds bursts of speed and plyometric-type movement. Every sport, he says, now needs cross-training.
- An ankle sprain with significant swelling may be an ATFL ligament injury: the healing potential of a tendon/ligament is only the first 3–4 weeks, so skipping proper treatment (e.g., a cast) in that window leads to chronic ankle instability that even 6 months of casting later cannot fix.
- RICE — rest, ice, compression, elevation — works well for minor sprains and pains; lots of swelling, however, usually means a busted ligament that needs proper treatment.
- Low back pain in youngsters is mostly postural and lifestyle-driven: IT professionals sit 10–12 hours a day, the body undergoes "negative adaptation," and "sitting is the new smoking." Desk workers need core-strengthening, spine and neck exercises because the body was not made to sit.
- He warns strongly against forceful neck manipulation by untrained practitioners: he has seen patients become paraplegic or quadriplegic after such "adjustments."
- Traditional tight-bandage ("puttu") fracture/sprain treatments have no robust scientific evidence behind them, and tight bandaging can cut off blood supply and nerve function — he sees complications, including loss of hand/limb use, on a daily basis.
- Exercise must progressively increase in load — overload, adaptability, reversibility, specificity. Walking the same 2 km for five years gives real benefit mainly in the first year or two; after that it is mostly psychological unless the load increases.
- His own routine: tennis 3–4 times a week plus gym, and a daily ~10-minute flexibility and mobility routine including calisthenics learned largely from online videos.

## Claims to be careful with
- "Sitting is the new smoking" is a slogan he uses rhetorically, not a literal risk equivalence.
- The higher osteoarthritis rates in specific communities (Telugu, Gujarati, Punjabi) are his clinical impression of a genetic component, not presented as study data.
- His vitamin D framing (30 as the bone-health threshold, metabolic role above it) reflects one position in a debate he himself acknowledges is controversial.
- The paralysis cases after neck manipulation and daily bandage-treatment complications are his reported case experience, not incidence statistics.
- His pick of tennis as the single best sport is a personal professional opinion (he plays tennis himself, as he jokes in the episode).

## Summary
Orthopaedic surgeon Dr Vijay Bose (6,000 knee replacements, SIMS Hospital, Chennai) explains why joints are wearing out decades earlier — lifestyle, obesity, sitting, and blind supplement use. He argues for testing before supplementing calcium or vitamin D, treating ligament injuries within their 3–4 week healing window, using RICE for minor sprains, and avoiding unqualified manipulation and tight-bandage healers whose complications he sees daily. He closes with his own fitness formula — tennis, gym, daily mobility work — and the principle that exercise only works if the load keeps increasing.
