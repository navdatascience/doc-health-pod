## Important tidbits
- Infertility is rising and is common: WHO data puts it at about 1 in 7 couples worldwide wanting but unable to conceive, and Dr Gutgutia estimates roughly 1 in 6–7 couples in India too — with secondary infertility (wanting a second child) heavily under-reported. In his practice, 3 of 10 women seeking infertility care now have PCOS, up from ~1 in 10 two decades ago, which he links to diet, sedentary lifestyle, pollution and hormonal/circadian disruption.
- Male factors are involved in ~50% of couples — "one in two" — through poor sperm quality, sexual dysfunction, obesity/diabetes and similar causes, yet infertility is still taught and treated as a women's problem (it sits inside OB-GYN in most medical colleges) and men are reluctant patients who avoid consultation.
- When to seek help: try for one year of regular unprotected intercourse (at least twice a week) before being labelled infertile — but if the woman is over 35, seek help after 6 months instead of waiting the year.
- The female biological clock in numbers: women are born with a fixed egg supply, lose ~30 eggs a day (~1,000 a month), have ~400,000 eggs at menarche (about 400 menstrual cycles), and have used up roughly three-fifths of them by age 30; risk of chromosomal abnormalities in the baby rises with maternal age over 35. Men make sperm throughout life, though quality declines with age.
- Egg freezing is "reproductive insurance," not a replacement for natural conception: eggs can legally be frozen for ~10 years, a frozen-egg cycle gives roughly a 30–50% chance of conception depending on age at freezing and number of eggs, and only ~20% of women who freeze ever come back to use the eggs — most conceive naturally. Nova's egg-freezing numbers jumped ~4x after COVID and grow ~20–25% a year in the West, he says.
- Check before you decide: the AMH (anti-Müllerian hormone) blood test — any day of the cycle, no fasting needed — indicates ovarian reserve; a 28-year-old with good reserve can wait a couple of years before freezing, while a lower AMH argues for consulting a fertility doctor sooner.
- Fertility preservation is also for cancer patients (oncofertility): eggs or sperm should be frozen BEFORE surgery, chemotherapy or radiotherapy, which can damage or destroy the gametes.
- Sperm quality is declining across humans and even marine/animal populations, which he attributes first to sedentary lifestyle and obesity, and to endocrine disruptors — pollution and microplastics; Delhi-level air quality (AQI 400–500) is raised as a direct concern for male infertility in urban India. Smoking is a major male factor (declining), but early research suggests vaping is WORSE than smoking for fertility, in both sexes.
- Only ~20% of couples who consult actually need IVF — 4 of 5 conceive without it, often with simple correction: most PCOS infertility is anovulatory (eggs present but not maturing) and responds to ovulation medication, and many men respond to simple therapies. IVF itself means a "self cycle": your own eggs and your partner's sperm — ~80% of Nova's cycles are self cycles and ~20% donor (donor egg, donor sperm, or rarely embryo donation).
- Lifestyle genuinely moves outcomes: in an Italian study he cites, ~70% of women with PCOS and obesity conceived naturally with weight-loss therapy alone, and even 5–7% weight loss (5–6 kg for an 80 kg woman) reduces anovulation and regularises cycles. Smoking and alcohol abuse must stop before treatment starts; gut health/microbiome optimisation alongside treatment ("ART = ASSISTED, not artificial, reproductive techniques") is an emerging focus.
- ICSI removes the numbers barrier for men: a normal sample has ~30–40 million sperm, but ICSI injects a single healthy sperm into one egg, so men with extremely poor samples can still father a child — a side effect being that men now seek quality-improvement treatment even less.
- Embryo testing and AI: at the blastocyst stage (~120+ cells, day 5; 8 cells expected day 3) cells can be biopsied for PGT (pre-implantation genetic testing) to screen chromosomal and genetic defects — e.g. transferring a thalassemia-free embryo when both parents are carriers. AI trained on ~10,000 embryos that led to births scores implantation chances (e.g. 75% vs 95%) as a double-check on the embryologist; double witnessing and electronic witnessing (alarms if the wrong gametes come near) protect against mix-ups.
- Success and cost in India: IVF pregnancy rates rose from ~20% per attempt 20 years ago to ~55% per attempt now, driven by standardised labs, air-handling units, incubators, time-lapse monitoring, laser hatching and AI. Cost is roughly ₹1.5–3 lakh per cycle (mostly self-funded; 0%-interest finance is appearing) — 10–12x cheaper than the US. He says ~95% of couples who persist through 3–4 attempts end up with their own (self-cycle) child.
- Rapid-fire myth-busting: IVF babies are NOT more likely to be sick; spicy food does not cause infertility; IVF is not only for the rich; moderate caffeine and moderate alcohol are acceptable (excess/abuse are not); eggs help (protein, choline) but don't guarantee conception; a failed first cycle does not predict failure — chances are similar across the first 3–4 cycles.
- Stress, guilt and support: women carry most of the guilt and shame, largely imposed by family after the informal "married 2 years, no child" deadline; men feel shame about even consulting. He says stress does not directly change IVF outcomes but causes hasty decisions and delays return after a failed cycle — Nova uses trained fertility counsellors and peer groups of couples at different journey stages to manage it.

## Claims to be careful with
- "Multiple IVF cycles can permanently harm a woman's body — absolutely no, IVF is safe, period" is Dr Gutgutia's categorical position; he is a Nova IVF specialist discussing his own field, and long-term safety questions are more nuanced in the literature than the rapid-fire format allows.
- The claims that vaping is worse than smoking for fertility and that endocrine disruptors/microplastics are driving sperm decline across species are presented as emerging early research, not established findings.
- Several figures are his practice-based estimates rather than published Indian data — the 1-in-6/7 Indian infertility rate, the tripling of PCOS share, ~50% male-factor involvement, and the ~95% cumulative self-cycle success over 3–4 attempts — and he notes Indian reporting is not robust. Nova IVF's own services (self cycles, counsellors, egg freezing growth) are also promoted through the episode.

## Summary
Fertility specialist Dr Rohit Gutgutia of Nova IVF walks Dr Pal through modern infertility: it affects roughly 1 in 7 couples, half of it involves the man, and PCOS has tripled as a cause in two decades. He explains when to seek help (a year of trying, six months over 35), the egg-freezing option backed by a simple AMH reserve test, why only about one in five couples who consult actually need IVF, how ICSI, embryo genetic testing (PGT) and AI embryo selection have pushed success to ~55% per attempt at ₹1.5–3 lakh in India, and why 5–7% weight loss alone can restore natural conception in PCOS — while repeatedly attacking the guilt and shame, borne mostly by women, that surrounds the diagnosis.
