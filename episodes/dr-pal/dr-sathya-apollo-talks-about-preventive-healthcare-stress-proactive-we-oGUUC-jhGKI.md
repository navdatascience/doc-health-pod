- **Guest:** Dr Sathya, CEO of Preventive Healthcare at Apollo Hospitals and architect of the Apollo ProHealth programme

## Important tidbits
- Non-communicable (lifestyle) diseases rose from 25% of deaths in India in 1990 to 65% in 2019, driven by food, movement, sleep, stress and substance use on top of genetics.
- A symptom is the point where the body "gives up" compensating for your lifestyle choices — screening aims to catch problems while they are still intangible and treatable, before symptoms appear.
- Simple home check: measure waist at the belly button and hip at the widest part; a waist-to-hip ratio above ~0.95 for men or ~0.8 for women signals metabolic risk worth screening.
- Being fit is not the same as being healthy — heavy exercisers and marathon runners should still check the heart's pumping capacity and electrical function, and fitness does not remove cancer risk.
- Dr Pal noted that in about 1 in 4 colonoscopies he performs, he finds precancerous growths; a polyp left alone has roughly a 25% chance of becoming cancer over 10 years, while removing it eliminates that risk. Stool testing and virtual colonography are alternatives for people avoiding colonoscopy.
- For children (roughly 10–16), watch mental health: academic stress and subtle bullying are rising; parents should talk with kids about their day and friends, share their own stressors, and use teachers or the paediatrician (who can speak to the child alone) if the child won't open up.
- Dental and eye screening are neglected: eyesight should be tested from as early as age 5, dental checks every 6 months (at least yearly), with twice-daily brushing and flossing built as habits early.
- In your 20s, check a lipid profile around age 25 and repeat every 5 years — annually if it's abnormal or there's a family history of high cholesterol, diabetes or heart disease. Beware "high normal" values creeping up over years rather than obsessing over a single green report.
- Women in their 20s should also watch for thyroid problems and PCOS, which tie into weight, hormones and later fertility issues.
- Prolonged sitting drops basal metabolic rate toward sleeping levels within 30–45 minutes; break sitting every 30–45 minutes, take stairs for up to five floors, park farther away, and use standing meetings/desks where possible.
- In your 30s, do a comprehensive annual health check (a couple of hours once a year) with imaging, not just home blood tests, and make sure a doctor actually evaluates the report.
- Women should get mammograms from around age 40–45 — Dr Sathya shared a case where breast cancer was only found after it had spread to the liver (stage 4, ~6–12 months prognosis), which earlier mammography could have caught at stage 1.
- Lung cancer screening (chest X-ray, low-dose CT where available) matters in polluted cities — cases are rising even in non-smokers and passive smokers.
- In your 60s, bone health is central: a DEXA scan assesses osteoporosis and fracture risk (FRAX score); women with early menopause (late 30s) should start DEXA in their 50s rather than waiting until 60. Calcium/vitamin D and simple resistance work (e.g., lifting a 1 kg weight, water-bottle leg lifts) help preserve bone and muscle.
- Cognitive screening (a 15–20 minute phone-based game in their programme) can flag declining working memory in the 60s; staying mentally active through work, volunteering, mentoring or grandchildren helps protect brain function.
- Make screening a habit by anchoring it to a milestone — e.g., your birthday week/month or a wedding anniversary — the way her parents schedule annual checks around their anniversary.

## Claims to be careful with
- The claim that lack of physical activity is "almost equal to smoking 15 cigarettes a day" was raised loosely in conversation; the speakers themselves said not to fixate on the exact number.
- The suggestion that air pollution will become the number-one driver of lung cancer was presented as the speakers' expectation ("not proven," as Dr Sathya acknowledged), not established evidence.
- Several figures (e.g., polyp-to-cancer progression chances, stage-4 survival of 6–12 months) are individual clinicians' practice-based statements rather than cited study data.

## Summary
Dr Sathya of Apollo Hospitals walks through decade-by-decade preventive screening — from children's mental health, dental and eye checks, to lipid profiles in the 20s, annual comprehensive checks in the 30s, mammograms and lung screening in the 40s–50s, and DEXA bone scans plus cognitive screening in the 60s — arguing that catching risk early through habits like milestone-anchored annual screening prevents the ICU visits that symptom-driven care leads to.
